# Silver Linings and Wolfpack Woes: Hitmen Shred the Competition in 7-1 Rout

**By: The Rinkside Reporter**
**Date: May 11, 2026**

If you caught the late-night tilt at the rink this Monday, you didn't just see a hockey game—you witnessed a clinic. The Hitmen Silver rolled into the 9:30 PM puck drop with fire in their eyes and left the ice having absolutely dismantled the One Man Wolfpack, walking away with a dominant 7-1 victory that officially puts the rest of the league on notice.

### Period 1: Setting the Tone
The Hitmen came out moving the biscuit with surgeon-like precision. It didn't take long for the offense to find its rhythm. At the 11:12 mark, Lukas Vodicka opened the floodgates, burying a beauty off setups from Eric Guthrie and Nicklas Clark. 

The Hitmen’s momentum was infectious. Shortly after, Bobby Pickett joined the party at 6:10, cleaning up a gorgeous sequence orchestrated by Josh Moyer and Clark. While Moyer spent two minutes in the sin bin for hooking at the 8:46 mark, the Hitmen’s penalty kill looked impenetrable, protecting their lead with the grit of a team that refuses to concede an inch of ice.

### Period 2: The Moyer-Clark Masterclass
If the first period was a warm-up, the second was a full-blown supernova. Josh Moyer put on a masterclass, lighting the lamp twice—once at 3:37 and again at 7:14—both times with the help of the night’s unsung hero, Nicklas Clark. 

Speaking of Clark, he decided he was done just dishing out apples and tallied one for himself at 7:36, tucking it top shelf where mom keeps the cookies. The Wolfpack managed to break the shutout at 8:20 when Steven Purn found the back of the net off a feed from James Whitacre, but it was nothing more than a momentary spark in a hurricane. By the time the buzzer sounded for the second intermission, the writing was on the wall, the frame, and the rafters.

### Period 3: Closing the Curtain
The final frame saw the Hitmen maintain their foot on the gas. As the game grew chippy, resulting in a late unsportsmanlike conduct penalty for the Wolfpack’s Garrett Williams, the Hitmen didn't lose their focus. Bobby Pickett added his second of the night at 12:59, and Josh Moyer finished his hat trick—no, wait, his *fourth* point performance—by capping off the scoring at 5:25. 

When the dust settled, the Hitmen had turned the ice into their personal playground.

### The Takeaway
With this decisive 7-1 victory, the Hitmen Silver climb to 4 points in the standings, signaling that their chemistry is peaking at exactly the right time. They played a complete game, with Nicklas Clark effectively acting as the conductor of this high-octane offensive orchestra.

For the One Man Wolfpack, sitting at 0 points, the road ahead is clearly defined: they need to tighten up the gaps and find a way to stop the "traffic in front of the net" that haunted them all night long. 

It was a tough night to be a Wolfpack fan, but for the Hitmen faithful? It was a beautiful night for a hockey game. Catch you at the next drop!
