# Sting Operation: Bad Bees Swarm the Power Puck Girls in 6-2 Clinic

**By: "Slapshot" Sally, Rinkside Reporter**
**Date:** May 17, 2026 | **Arena:** The Cooler

If you arrived at the rink this morning looking for a quiet, defensive stalemate, you were at the wrong barn. This Sunday morning tilt was a full-blown, high-octane buzzer-beater of a game that left the ice scarred and the Bad Bees leaving with a healthy dose of momentum. In a game that saw the Bad Bees sting early and often, they walked away with a convincing 6-2 victory over the Power Puck Girls.

### First Period: The Bees Find the Honey
The Bad Bees came out of the gate looking like they’d had an extra shot of espresso in their pre-game smoothies. They wasted absolutely no time, lighting the lamp early to set the tone. 

The Power Puck Girls, showing the kind of grit that keeps fans coming back, tried to claw their way into the contest when Addison Gaut hammered home a beautiful feed from Allison Flanders at the 1:18 mark, temporarily silencing the buzzing crowd. However, that was about as much breathing room as the Bees would grant them. 

The Bees’ offense was a relentless swarm. Grace Wolff, Michelle Karjadi, and Remisha Blation all found the back of the net in quick succession. By the time the dust settled on the first period, the Bees had turned the ice into their personal playground, heading into the break with a commanding lead and the Puck Girls looking for some answers in the locker room.

### Second Period: Too Little, Too Late
The second period opened with the Power Puck Girls looking for a spark. They found it at the 14:13 mark when Allison Flanders—who was essentially a one-woman wrecking crew this morning—ripped one home courtesy of a nifty assist from Macy Alford. It was a sniper’s shot, top shelf where mom keeps the cookies, and for a fleeting second, the hometown crowd dared to dream of a comeback.

But the Bad Bees weren't about to let the game slip away. Just 18 seconds later, Mary J Wolff put the final nail in the coffin, burying a beauty from Andrea Evans to restore the multi-goal lead. 

The Bees’ defensive unit tightened up the neutral zone for the remainder of the frame, effectively turning the game into a perimeter passing drill. The Puck Girls threw plenty of rubber at the net, but the Bees’ goaltending held firm against the traffic, ensuring the door was shut tight.

### The Aftermath: What It Means
For the Bad Bees (now 4 points), this win is a massive statement. They’ve proven they can control the tempo and capitalize on defensive lapses, keeping them perched comfortably near the top of the standings. They play a fast, hungry game, and if they keep finding the back of the net with this consistency, they’re going to be a nightmare for any goalie in this league.

On the other side of the red line, the Power Puck Girls (0 points) find themselves in a tough spot. It’s early in the season, and while the scoreboard didn’t go their way, they have flashes of brilliance—specifically the connection between Flanders and Gaut—that they can build on. They’ll need to tighten up the coverage in front of their own crease if they want to stop the bleeding and get their first points on the board.

The skates are back in the bag, the ice is being resurfaced, and for now, the Bees hold the bragging rights. Stay tuned, folks—the season is just heating up.
