### First Period: The Trading Post

But if you blinked, you missed the momentum shift. The Knights’ Eron Richards leveled the playing field just minutes later, burying a feed from Dustin Giebel. 

Not to be outdone, Joshua Hopwood decided he wanted a piece of the action. With helpers from Trent Ohman and Joshua Smith, Hopwood rifled one home at 9:01 to put Gold as Ice back in the driver’s seat. It was fast, it was loose, and it was exactly how hockey is meant to be played.

### Second Period: The Grinding Gear
The second frame was all about the whistle and the grit. The Knights found themselves in a bit of a pickle early on with a "Too Many Men" call, but they managed to kill off the penalty with the kind of defensive resolve that makes coaches smile. 

The turning point? Dustin Giebel, a constant thorn in the side of the Gold as Ice defense, knotted the game back up at 6:04 with a tidy finish from Toby Temple and Thomas Aurilio. With Ben Gartner heading to the box for high-sticking shortly after, the Knights looked poised to take control, but the Bronze defensive corps held the line, keeping the score deadlocked heading into the final act.

### Third Period: The Scoring Blitz
If the first two periods were the appetizer, the third was a full-course buffet of goals. 

Joshua Hopwood ignited the fuse at 11:01, going solo to put Gold as Ice up 3-2. That seemed to open the floodgates. Makenzie Dillard joined the party just over a minute later, and when Garrett Williams potted his own goal at 13:07, it looked like the game was heading into a blowout. 

But hold your horses—the Knights aren't the type to fold their jerseys and go home. Fuad Shimi clawed one back at 13:32, and as the clock ticked down, Giebel found the back of the net *again* with just under a minute left to make it a one-goal game. 

The Knights pulled their goalie, hoping for a miracle, but Triston Wilson put the dagger in the heart of the comeback effort with an empty-netter at 5:51, assisted by Kenneth Watkins.
