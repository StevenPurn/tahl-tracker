# Murder in the Rink: Tulsa Dismantles Mojo in 7-1 Statement Win

**TULSA** – If you were looking for a quiet Sunday afternoon at the arena, you picked the wrong zip code. The Tulsa Murder turned the ice into their own personal playground this afternoon, putting on a clinic that left the Mojo looking for answers they just didn’t have. By the time the final horn sounded, the scoreboard read 7-1, and the Murder had officially sent a message to the rest of the league: Tulsa is here to play.

### First Period: The Hodges Hustle
The game opened with the intensity of a playoff tilt, but it didn't take long for Tulsa to establish their dominance. At the 6:56 mark, **Mark Hodges** decided he didn’t need any help, weaving through the Mojo defense like a hot knife through butter. He buried a beauty past the netminder to draw first blood. It was an unassisted individual effort that set the tone for the afternoon—fast, physical, and opportunistic.

### Second Period: A Period of Pure Chaos
If the first was a warm-up, the second was a full-blown barn burner. 

The Murder’s discipline wobbled slightly when Jeffrey Pruitt took a seat for high-sticking, but the penalty kill held firm. Once back at full strength, Tulsa regained the momentum. **Tom Mortimer** extended the lead at 12:47 with another unassisted tally, showcasing the kind of clinical finishing that keeps opposing goalies up at night.

As the period wound down, the Murder kept their foot on the gas. With just 38.4 seconds left on the clock, **Pete Fleming** hammered one home off a crisp feed from Hodges, sending the crowd into a frenzy.

However, Mojo finally found a flicker of life mid-way through. After Spencer Charles sat for elbowing, Bradley Fidler managed to crash the crease and ruin the shutout bid for the Murder at 6:36. But the Murder response was immediate and ruthless; Adam Hoffdahl answered back less than two minutes later, assisted by Pruitt, to keep the Mojo firmly in the rear-view mirror.

### Third Period: The Pruitt Parade
By the third, it was all Tulsa, all the time. The Murder offense began clicking like a finely tuned engine. Jarred Butler found the back of the net at 13:37, thanks to a beautiful setup from the Henderson-Hoffdahl duo.

But the final act belonged to **Jeffrey Pruitt**. After spending time in the box earlier, Pruitt made up for it in spades, scoring twice in the final ten minutes. His second goal, coming at 9:59 with a helper from Butler, was the cherry on top of a seven-course blowout. He went top shelf where mom keeps the cookies, leaving no doubt about who owned the ice today.

### The Aftermath
When the dust settled, Tulsa walked away with their second point of the season, moving to a dominant 2-0 record. They looked cohesive, dangerous in traffic, and entirely unflappable. 

For the Mojo, it’s a long bus ride home. Sitting at 0-2, they’ll need to do some serious soul-searching after failing to contain the Murder’s depth scoring. 

The Murder are clearly clicking, and if they keep playing with this level of swagger, the rest of the league might want to start checking their rearview mirrors. We’ll be back at it next week, but for now, the city of Tulsa sleeps a little sounder knowing their team knows how to put the game to bed.
