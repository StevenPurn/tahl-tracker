# The Bees Are Buzzing: Bad Bees Sting Power Puck Girls in Sunday Morning Shellacking

**By: "Slap-Shot" Sammy, Hometown Beat Reporter**
**Date: May 17, 2026**

The ice was barely dry at the 10:15 a.m. puck drop, but if you blinked, you missed the opening act of what turned into a full-blown offensive clinic. On a bright Sunday morning, the Bad Bees swarmed the zone and refused to vacate, buzzing their way to a decisive 6-2 victory over the Power Puck Girls. 

It was the kind of game where the coffee in the stands was still steaming, but the Bees were already playing with a fire that would make a goal lamp explode.

### Period One: A Frenzy at the Blue Line
The Bees wasted zero time establishing the "home team" dominance, with an early, unassisted tally just over two minutes in to set the tone. But the Puck Girls showed some early heart, responding quickly when Addison Gaut—fed by a beautiful dish from Allison Flanders—found the back of the net to equalize.

However, once the Bees got their stingers out, they didn't put them away. Grace Wolff started a late-period onslaught, followed shortly by a sniper shot from Michelle Karjadi, who was set up perfectly by Mary J. Wolff. With Remisha Blation joining the party and a second, mysterious "Unknown" tally adding to the Bees' tally, the Puck Girls found themselves staring down a mountain of a deficit heading into the intermission. The Bees were winning every puck battle, creating consistent traffic in front of the net, and turning the crease into a high-stress zone.

### Period Two: Trying to Find the Flow
Trailing by a healthy margin, the Puck Girls came out for the second frame with a renewed sense of urgency. The chemistry between Allison Flanders and Macy Alford paid dividends at the 14:13 mark, with Flanders burying a beauty to give the home crowd a glimmer of hope. It was a classic "top shelf where mom keeps the cookies" kind of finish.

But the "Bad" in Bad Bees proved to be more than just a name. Less than twenty seconds after the Puck Girls clawed one back, Mary J. Wolff struck gold, capitalizing on an assist from Andrea Evans to restore the four-goal lead and effectively put this one on ice.

### The Breakdown
For the Bees, this was a statement win. They played a disciplined, aggressive game, suffocating the Puck Girls' breakouts and forcing turnovers that translated directly to the scoreboard. They move to 2 points on the season with an efficiency that suggests they aren't just here to participate—they’re here to take over the division.

For the Power Puck Girls, it’s a tough morning at the office. Sitting at 0 points, the squad will need to go back to the drawing board, tighten up the defensive coverage in front of their own house, and find a way to sustain momentum when they do manage to light the lamp.

It’s early in the season, and as any veteran of the rink knows, you don’t hang your head after a Sunday morning loss. You lace 'em up, tighten the straps, and wait for the next barn burner. 

**Final Score:** Bad Bees 6, Power Puck Girls 2.
