# Wendigos Weather the Storm: Eagles Clipped in 4-2 Barn Burner

**By: Your Hometown Rink-Side Reporter**
**Date: May 10, 2026**

The ice was barely dry at the arena last night, but by the time the final buzzer echoed through the rafters, the air was thick with the scent of high-octane hockey and broken sticks. In a clash that saw more grit than a sandpaper factory, the Wendigos skated away with a 4-2 victory over the Lite Beer Eagles, proving once again that in this league, you’ve got to earn every inch of ice.

### First Period: The Wendigos Come Out Flying
The Eagles came into the night hungry for their first points of the season, but the Wendigos had other plans. They hit the ground running, turning the offensive zone into their personal living room. 

The scoring dam broke at the 10:16 mark when Zac Hughes tickled the twine, set up perfectly by Taylor Chinowth. Not even five minutes later, Joshua Copeland decided he didn't need any help, potting an unassisted beauty to double the lead. The Eagles were reeling, and things nearly went from bad to worse when Patrick Flannery headed to the sin bin for roughing, but the Eagles couldn't capitalize on the man advantage. By the end of one, the Eagles were looking up at a steep mountain to climb.

### Second Period: The Eagles Find Their Wings
If you thought the Eagles were going to roll over, you haven't been watching this team long enough. The second period turned into a classic chess match of physical play. 

Kalea Maxwell pushed the Wendigo lead to 3-0 at the 11:58 mark, once again finding the connection with Chinowth. It looked like the rout was on, but Jonathan McCormick had a different narrative in mind. After serving a stint in the box for roughing, McCormick returned to the ice with fire in his eyes. At 1:35, he found the back of the net off a dish from Christopher Webber, finally putting the Eagles on the board and giving the boys in sweaters a much-needed shot of adrenaline heading into the final frame.

### Third Period: Too Little, Too Late
The third period was a true test of nerves. The Eagles started clawing back when McCormick struck again at the 10:15 mark, burying a feed from Jeff Horvath. At 3-2, the arena was shaking—this was a one-goal game, and the traffic in front of the net was getting heavy enough to cause a pileup on the expressway.

The Wendigos found themselves in hot water with Matthew Palmer taking a pair of trips to the box, but the Eagles couldn't find the equalizer. Just as the momentum felt like it might shift for good, Taylor Chinowth—who had been a thorn in the Eagles' side all night—sealed the deal with an unassisted dagger at 3:49. 

### The Aftermath
When the dust settled, the scoreboard read 4-2 in favor of the Wendigos. For the visitors, it’s a massive result that pushes them to a clean 2 points in the standings, showcasing a lethal transition game and a knack for keeping their composure.

For the Lite Beer Eagles, the 0-point start is a tough pill to swallow, but they showed heart by battling back from a 3-0 deficit. They’ll need to clean up the defensive zone and find more consistency if they want to get their campaign off the ground. 

It was a night of hard hits, heavy legs, and the kind of hockey that makes you glad you braved the cold to sit in the stands. The season is still young, folks—and if this game is any indication, we’re in for one heck of a ride. See you at the next drop!
