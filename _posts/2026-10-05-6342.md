# Hitmen Silver Edge Dirty Birds in Chaotic Friday Night Tilt

If you’re looking for a lesson in how to play disciplined, structured hockey, the Hitmen Silver’s 5-4 victory over the Dirty Birds on Friday night isn’t exactly the tape you show the local youth squad. But if you’re looking for a game that features more trips to the penalty box than a repeat offender in traffic court, you’ve come to the right place.

The Hitmen came into the matchup sitting pretty at the top of the standings, and for the first two periods, they looked every bit the part. Bobby Pickett kept his hot streak alive, lighting the lamp at 7:17 in the first to open the scoring. By the time Nicklas Clark added two quick goals in the second period, it looked like the Hitmen might just cruise to another win. 

Clark, who extended his goal-scoring streak to three games, seems to have found a permanent residence in the Dirty Birds’ defensive zone. "I’m just trying to stay out of the way of the chaos," Clark joked after the game, clearly ignoring the fact that he was the primary architect of it.

The Dirty Birds, to their credit, refused to roll over. Matthan Hillberg proved to be a one-man wrecking crew—quite literally. Hillberg accounted for two goals in the second period, but his night eventually devolved into a whistle-heavy marathon. By the third period, Hillberg was seemingly trying to collect as many penalty minutes as the league would allow, culminating in a 10-minute unsportsmanlike conduct stint and an early exit for excessive penalties.

The third period was less of a hockey game and more of a series of cameos by the officiating crew. With Hillberg out of the mix, the Dirty Birds somehow found a second wind. Triston Wilson and Kyle Rachal both tallied late goals to tighten the screws, turning a comfortable Hitmen lead into a nervous finish.

However, the Hitmen found the definitive answer at 7:31 of the final frame, when Joshua Moyer buried the game-winner off a feed from Skylar Miller. Moyer’s tally—his third goal in three games—was a masterclass in staying calm while the rest of the ice descended into a scrum of cross-checks and roughing calls.

"It was a bit like herding cats out there," one Hitmen veteran noted while unlacing his skates. "You just try to keep your head up and your stick on the ice. If you do that, eventually the puck finds you."

The win keeps the Hitmen Silver firmly in the driver’s seat of the league standings, proving they have the fortitude to close out games even when the opposition decides to take the "dirty" part of their name a little too literally. The Dirty Birds, meanwhile, will have to reflect on a night where they spent more time chatting with the refs than putting shots on net.

It wasn't a game for the highlight reels, but it was two points in the bank for the Hitmen. In this league, that's all that really matters.
