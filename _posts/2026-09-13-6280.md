# Bad Bees Sting Early in Dominant 5-1 Victory

If you blinked during the opening period on Saturday morning, you might have missed the entire game. The Bad Bees stormed out of the gates at the 10:15 a.m. puck drop, putting on a clinic in offensive pressure that left the Power Puck Girls chasing shadows and the Bees buzzing all the way to a 5-1 victory.

The Bees didn't just knock on the door; they kicked it down. Lauren A. Mincey set the tone early, lighting the lamp just 83 seconds into the opening frame. That goal seemed to open the floodgates, and by the time the dust settled on the first period, the Bees had rattled off five unanswered goals to put the game effectively on ice before the Zamboni crew even had a chance to think about the intermission.

Following Mincey’s opener, an "Unknown" marksman doubled the lead at the 3:48 mark. Jessica Harper joined the party shortly after, and Remisha Blation added to the tally with a beautiful strike at 9:31. Just seventeen seconds later, the Bees found the back of the net once more—this time courtesy of an "Unknown" player cleaning up a feed from Kristin Brooks—to cap a frantic, five-goal barrage that turned a competitive matchup into a lopsided affair.

To their credit, the Power Puck Girls refused to roll over. They emerged from the locker room with a renewed sense of purpose, tightening up their defensive zone and finally breaking through the Bees' wall when Addison Gaut found the back of the net at 8:21 of the second period. While the marker didn't spark the miraculous comeback they were looking for, it did prove that the Power Puck Girls aren't going to make life easy on their opponents regardless of the score.

"We definitely felt like we were playing a game of catch-up against a hurricane," said one anonymous rink-side observer. "The Bees had the honey flowing early, but you have to admire the Power Puck girls for holding their ground in the second."

With this result, the Bad Bees climb the standings with their first win of the season, moving to a 1-0-1 record. For the Power Puck Girls, it’s a tough morning at the office, but they’ll look to reset as they head back to the drawing board. If the Bees can maintain this kind of blistering pace, the rest of the league had better keep their goggles on—these Bees are looking to swarm.
