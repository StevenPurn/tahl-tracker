# The Drought is Over: Hoes Edge Tuskens in a Nail-Biter at the Ice Box

**By: "Hat-Trick" Harry, Rinkside Reporter**
**July 16, 2026**

If you wanted a masterclass in desperation hockey, the 7:15 puck drop at the arena was the place to be. With the Average Hoes and the Tulsa Tuskens both looking to climb out of the basement and shed their winless skins, the air was thick with the scent of Zamboni fumes and nervous energy. It wasn’t always pretty—in fact, at times it was about as clean as a fresh sheet of ice during a snowstorm—but for the Average Hoes, a 2-1 shootout victory is exactly what the doctor ordered.

### First Period: Finding the Back of the Onion Bag
The Hoes came out skating like their hair was on fire, desperate to end that 0-6-0 nightmare. They controlled the early rhythm, forcing the Tuskens into a defensive shell. The dam finally broke at the 11:25 mark. Abigail Nisbet threaded a needle through the neutral zone, finding Jessica Stoops, who went top-shelf where mom keeps the cookies, leaving the Tuskens' netminder grasping at thin air. 

Just over a minute later, the Tuskens handed the Hoes a golden opportunity when Matthew Patrick got caught in a communication breakdown, leading to a "Too Many Men" penalty. The Tuskens’ penalty kill, however, stood tall, acting like a brick wall against a wrecking ball to keep the deficit at a single goal heading into the break.

### Second Period: The Tuskens Strike Back
The middle frame turned into a defensive tug-of-war. The Tuskens, realizing their season was slipping through their fingers, tightened their laces and turned up the physical intensity. 

At 7:10, the Tuskens found the equalizer. Dylan Walker, riding a one-game goal streak, proved why he’s the danger man for Tulsa. He intercepted a breakout pass and danced through traffic in front of the net before snapping a wrister home. That goal turned the momentum on its head, turning the rest of the period into a high-stakes chess match where both goalies stood on their heads to keep things knotted at 1-1.

### The Shootout: Ice in the Veins
After a scoreless stalemate in the closing minutes of regulation, we went to the skills competition. Shootouts are the ultimate heartbreak for some and a coronation for others, and tonight, the Hoes had the magic touch.

Suzanne Hawkins stepped up first, deking with the grace of a ballerina before sliding the puck home to put the pressure on Tulsa. When the Tuskens failed to respond, Abigail Nisbet—the architect of the Hoes’ first goal—stepped onto center stage. With nerves of steel, she buried her shot to seal the deal. 

### Standing Tall
For the Average Hoes (1-6-0), tonight wasn’t just about the two points; it was about the exorcism of a season-long demon. They finally put a "1" in the win column and have officially leapfrogged the Tuskens. 

As for the Tulsa Tuskens (0-6-0), they fall to the bottom of the pile with a 0-6-0 record. It’s a bitter pill to swallow, especially after such a gritty effort. But in a league as competitive as this, tonight proved one thing: the gap between the cellar and the rest of the pack is closing. 

The Hoes head home tonight with a little extra spring in their step, while the Tuskens retreat to the locker room to regroup. One thing is for sure—the standings are shifting, and the race for points is only going to get faster from here. Stick with us, folks; this season is just starting to cook!
