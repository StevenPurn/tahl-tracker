# Blizzard Blows Through Havok in 12-6 Scoring Frenzy

If you showed up to the rink on Friday night expecting a defensive clinic or a tight-checking affair, you clearly haven’t been paying attention to this league lately. In a game that featured more red lights than a city intersection at rush hour, the Blizzard dismantled Havok 12-6 in a regular-season tilt that had the scoreboard operator praying for mercy.

The Blizzard came out of the gate looking like they’d had an extra cup of coffee, and Joshua Moyer set the tone early. Moyer struck with a mere seven seconds left in the opening period, followed by an encore goal just over four minutes later. By the time the final buzzer mercifully sounded, the Blizzard had turned a standard matchup into a full-scale offensive barrage.

The undisputed architect of the chaos was Gary Hennenfent, who put on a clinic that would make a statistician dizzy. Between his own scoring touch and his playmaking vision, Hennenfent was involved in almost every sequence that mattered, effectively acting as a one-man wrecking crew against the Havok defensive shell. Nicklas Clark was equally troublesome for the Havok goaltender, finding the back of the net three times and proving that if you give this Blizzard squad an inch, they’ll take the whole ice sheet.

"We told the boys to keep it simple, but I think they heard 'keep it aggressive,'" said one Blizzard staffer, nursing a suspiciously large Gatorade after the game. "When the puck is finding the net that often, you stop asking questions and just keep skating."

Havok, to their credit, didn’t roll over, even as the deficit ballooned. Matthan Hillberg provided a glimmer of life in the third period, completing a personal hat-trick with a flurry of goals that briefly made the crowd wonder if a miracle comeback was in the cards. Zack Wade added a late marker as well, but the hill was simply too steep to climb against a team that was clicking with such frightening efficiency.

It was a tough night for the Havok bench, who spent the better part of the middle frame chasing shadows. "We were doing a lot of watching," a noticeably winded Havok defenseman muttered as he headed to the locker room. "Usually, we’re the ones finding the gaps, but tonight, those gaps were about as wide as the parking lot."

The win is a much-needed shot in the arm for the Blizzard, who have been struggling to find consistency in a crowded middle-to-lower tier of the standings. For Havok, it’s back to the drawing board to tighten up the defensive zone. If they want to climb out of the bottom half of the table, they’ll need to figure out how to stop the bleeding before they find themselves on the wrong side of another scoreboard massacre.

As for the Blizzard, they’ll head home tonight enjoying the rare luxury of a double-digit offensive output—a night where even the bounces that usually go wide seemed to find the twine. It was the kind of game that reminds you why we play, even if it’s the kind of game that ruins a goalie's save percentage for the entire month.
