# Eagles Fly Past Danglers in Gritty Tuesday Night Scrap

If you like your hockey served with a side of desperate, mid-table clawing, the rink at 8:45 PM on a Tuesday was the place to be. In a battle between two teams looking to jumpstart their sluggish seasons, the Lite Beer Eagles managed to edge out the Dirty Danglers, 3-2, in a game that felt like it was played through a layer of wet cement.

The Eagles and the Danglers entered the contest sharing the same 0-1-1 record and a collective hunger for their first win of the season. It wasn’t always pretty—the kind of game where the puck seems to have a personal vendetta against the players' sticks—but it was tight, and for the most part, disciplined.

The Eagles drew first blood late in the opening frame when Tyler Waller found the back of the net unassisted at 14:38. It was a classic "keep it simple" play that frustrated a Danglers defense that had spent much of the period killing off a penalty to LC III Sinor. While the Danglers looked to settle in after the break, they found their rhythm in the second period when Daniel Maxwell capitalized on a loose puck to tie the game at 10:47. 

By the third period, the fatigue—and the stakes—began to show. The game opened up, and the momentum swung toward the Danglers early when Stephen Jeffery buried a feed from Dylan Calhoun with less than two minutes left in the period to give the Danglers a 2-1 lead. It looked for a brief moment like the Danglers had finally found the cure for their early-season blues.

"We thought we had the wind in our sails," one Danglers veteran muttered near the locker room, shaking his head. "Then we just stopped watching the back door."

He wasn't wrong. The Eagles didn't panic. Just under two minutes later, James DePriest knotted the game back up at 2-2, thanks to some heads-up vision from Waller. With the clock winding down and the crowd—a mix of tired spouses and friends who were likely just waiting for the bar to open—expecting a stalemate, Corey Phillips provided the heroics. Phillips tucked away the go-ahead goal at 9:52, effectively sucking the air out of the Danglers' bench.

While neither team is going to be mistaken for the ‘80s Oilers, both goaltending squads kept things respectable. The Eagles’ defensive shell in the final seconds proved to be just enough to survive the Danglers' last-ditch flurry.

For the Eagles, the win is a necessary psychological boost, pushing them out of the basement of the standings and giving them a bit of breathing room. The Dirty Danglers, meanwhile, will head back to the drawing board. They had their chances, but in a league this crowded, you don’t get points for effort—you get them for finishing. Tonight, the Eagles were just a little better at finding the finish line.
