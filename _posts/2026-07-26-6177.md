# McCormick’s Hat-Trick Heroics: Dirty Danglers Snuff Out Fossil Hockey in Sunday Morning Clash

**By: The Rinkside Reporter**
*July 26, 2026 – 10:15 AM Puck Drop*

There’s nothing quite like the smell of fresh-cut ice and stale locker room air on a Sunday morning. For the Fossil Hockey squad and the Dirty Danglers, this 10:15 AM tilt was more than just a skate—it was a battle to climb out of the basement. Entering the contest, Fossil Hockey was looking to stop a heart-wrenching five-game slide, but it was the Danglers who found their rhythm, skating away with a decisive 4-1 victory.

### Period 1: The Brief Flicker of Hope
Fossil Hockey came out with some pep in their step, despite the heavy cloud of their recent losing streak. The physicality was apparent early, with Brandon Johnson earning a two-minute vacation to the sin bin for high-sticking at 3:49. 

However, Fossil managed to draw first blood at the 8:00 mark. Dave Griffiths threaded a beautiful pass to Andrew Lamparter, who hammered it home to put the Fossils up 1-0. It looked like the streak might be coming to an end, but the lead was short-lived. Just over a minute later, the Danglers' Jonathan McCormick decided he had other plans, burying a feed from Stephen Jeffery to knot things up 1-1 heading into the first intermission.

### Period 2: The McCormick Takeover
If the first period was a test of wills, the second was a masterclass in efficiency by the Dirty Danglers. They turned the screws, and McCormick proved to be an absolute menace in the offensive zone. 

At 12:11, McCormick struck again, this time off a dish from Brett Mooney, giving the Danglers their first lead of the morning. Less than three minutes later, Daniel Maxwell capitalized on a Travis Moore setup to extend the gap. By the time McCormick completed his natural hat-trick at 2:55—courtesy of a slick Arthur Baisley helper—the momentum had shifted entirely toward the visitor's bench.

Fossil Hockey’s frustration began to boil over as the period wore on. Discipline became a ghost, with Brandon Johnson, David Muckala, and Dave Griffiths all finding themselves whistled for hooking. The constant parade to the penalty box sucked the oxygen out of the Fossil rally, leaving them chasing the game for the final frame.

### Period 3: Keeping the Ship Steady
Trailing by three, Fossil Hockey needed a miracle, but the Danglers’ defense—and their netminder—were in lockstep. Even when David Muckala picked up his second hooking penalty of the game at 9:05, the Danglers’ penalty kill unit stood tall, refusing to yield an inch of ice. The game ended in a clinical fashion, with the Danglers suffocating any Fossil momentum and sealing the 4-1 result.

### The Aftermath
For the Dirty Danglers, this win is a massive breath of fresh air. They’ve been hovering in the lower rungs of the standings, but a performance like this—built on the back of McCormick’s three-goal outburst—might just be the spark plug they need to ignite a climb up the leaderboard.

For Fossil Hockey, the road gets steeper. With their losing streak now extended, the squad is searching for answers. Discipline is clearly an issue, and they’ll need to stop the revolving door to the penalty box if they want to leapfrog their rivals in the standings.
