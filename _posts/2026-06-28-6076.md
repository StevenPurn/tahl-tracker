# Mojo Magic: A Late-Game Surge Secures the W Over Gold as Ice Bronze

**By: The Rinkside Reporter**
**Date: June 28, 2026**

If you weren’t at the barn this afternoon, you missed a classic. What started as a tactical chess match on skates devolved into a back-and-forth, heart-pounding barn burner that kept the fans on the edge of their seats until the final buzzer echoed off the rafters. When the dust settled, Mojo had edged out Gold as Ice Bronze 4-3 in a contest that had more momentum swings than a pendulum in a hurricane.

### First Period: Trading Punches
The game started with both squads feeling each other out, looking to establish some heavy traffic in front of the net. Mojo drew first blood at the 2:21 mark when Bradley Ruttman cashed in on a beautiful feed from Jarred Butler. It was exactly the kind of gritty, blue-collar play we expect from Mojo. 

However, Gold as Ice Bronze wasn’t about to let them skate away with it. With the momentum clearly shifting, the dangerous duo of Christopher Chapin and Triston Wilson went to work. At 4:28, Wilson found the back of the net off a Chapin dish, knotting the game at 1-1 and silencing the Mojo faithful. 

### Second Period: The Blitz
The second frame saw a flurry of action that would make any hockey purist’s head spin. With the clock winding down, Austin Lowry decided to take matters into his own hands. At 12.7 seconds—yes, you read that right, a literal buzzer-beater—Lowry scorched one past the keeper to put Mojo up 2-1. 

Coming out of the gate for the restart, Mojo looked possessed. Just thirty seconds later, at the 12:40 mark, Lowry turned playmaker, setting up Stephen Jeffery for a strike that sent the Mojo bench into a frenzy. Just when it looked like Mojo might run away with it, Triston Wilson struck again at 14:52, pulling Gold as Ice Bronze back within striking distance at 3-2.

### Third Period: The Wilson Hat Trick and the Heroics
The third period was all about Triston Wilson. He completed his hat trick at 12:38, knotting the score at 3-3 and putting the pressure squarely on Mojo’s shoulders. The tension reached a boiling point at 6:01 when Mojo’s Jarred Butler was whistled for "Too Many Men"—a classic case of having too many cooks in the kitchen. 

But Mojo’s penalty kill stood tall, fueled by some absolute robbery in the crease. They neutralized the threat, and that grit paid off big time at 9:29. Brandon Chouinard stepped up and buried the game-winner, a clutch marker that brought the roof down and regained the lead for good. 

### The Aftermath
It was a gut-wrenching loss for Gold as Ice Bronze, despite Wilson’s heroic three-goal performance. They remain hungry at 1-3-1, looking for a way to break out of the basement of the standings. 

For Mojo, this 4-3 victory is a massive morale booster. By moving to 3-2-0 (6 points), they climb the ladder and join the thick of the conversation near the top of the standings. They’ve proven they can survive a slugfest, and with the middle of the pack tightening up, every point from here on out is precious.

The skates are back in the locker, the ice is being resurfaced, but make no mistake: Mojo earned every bit of that ice time tonight. See you at the next drop!
