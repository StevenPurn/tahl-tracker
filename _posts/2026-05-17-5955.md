# Riveting Offense: Tulsa Storms Back to Sink the Valkyries in 7-5 Thriller

**By: Your Rinkside Reporter**
**Date: May 17, 2026**

If you didn’t have your coffee yet this morning, the 8:45 a.m. puck drop between our Valkyries and the Tulsa Riveters provided a wake-up call louder than a goal horn at center ice. It was a classic "barn burner" that saw the lead change hands like a hot potato, but in the end, it was the Riveters who walked away with the two points, handing the Valkyries a heartbreaking 7-5 defeat.

### Period 1: The Valkyries Come Out Flying
The Valkyries looked like they’d eaten lightning bolts for breakfast. Jessica Hopwood set the tone early, silencing the skeptics at the 10:16 mark with an unassisted beauty. Not long after, Makenzie Dillard showcased some serious wheels, extending the lead to 2-0. 

However, the Riveters aren’t a team you can keep in the hangar for long. Ivy Emerson managed to carve through some heavy traffic in front of the net to put Tulsa on the board at 7:42, reminding everyone that this was going to be a long, physical morning.

### Period 2: The Momentum Swings
The second frame was all about the gritty, blue-collar play that hockey fans live for. Kalea Maxwell got the Valkyries back on track at the 13:00 mark, finding the back of the net to restore the cushion. But then, the tides turned. 

Tulsa’s Gabrielle Gibson decided it was her time to shine, and shortly after, Hannah Atkins buried a gorgeous feed from Taylor Soucy. Suddenly, the Valkyries' two-goal lead had evaporated. You could feel the air in the arena shift; the Riveters had the momentum, and they were skating like they owned the ice.

### Period 3: A Wild West Shootout
If you blinked, you missed a goal. The third period was absolute carnage in the crease. 

Gabrielle Gibson completed a monstrous effort by finding the net at 10:48, followed immediately by an Amanda Long strike just 30 seconds later. Just when it looked like the Valkyries might be drowning, Makenzie Dillard—who was clearly on a mission today—sniped one home at 12:47 to keep the dream alive.

But Gibson, playing like she had magnets in her stick, buried another one, and Amanda Long hammered the final nail in the coffin at 6:43 off a nifty setup from Stacey Burgess and Hannah Atkins. Despite a late-period heater from Dillard to complete her hat trick, the hill was just too steep to climb. 

### The Bottom Line
When the dust settled and the final buzzer mercifully cut through the tension, the scoreboard read 7-5 in favor of the visitors. 

For the Tulsa Riveters, this win is a massive shot in the arm. Moving to 4 points in the standings, they showed the grit required to battle back from an early deficit—a trait that wins championships. 

For the Valkyries, it’s back to the drawing board. Still sitting at 0 points, the squad showed flashes of brilliance and elite individual skill—especially from Dillard—but they’ll need to clean up the defensive zone if they want to stop these high-scoring affairs from slipping away.

The ice might be cold, but the rivalry is heating up. We’ll see if the Valkyries can lace ‘em up and find their defensive footing when they hit the ice next. Stay tuned, puckheads!
