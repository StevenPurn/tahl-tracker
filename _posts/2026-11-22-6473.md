# Fossil Hockey Outlasts Ice Breakers Plus in Eight-Goal Offensive Outing

If you were looking for a defensive masterclass this Sunday, you surely took a wrong turn at the rink. In an 8-5 victory for Fossil Hockey over the Ice Breakers Plus, we witnessed the kind of high-event hockey that makes scorekeepers reach for extra lead and makes statisticians like myself question the very nature of structural integrity in the defensive zone.

Fossil Hockey, currently sitting mid-table, did exactly what they needed to do against a winless Ice Breakers squad, though they certainly made it more interesting than their scouting report suggested they would. The tone was set early by Eron Richards, who extended his personal goal-scoring streak to three games with a pair of opening-period tallies. With Frederic Bassett acting as the primary distributor—notching three assists on the afternoon—Fossil managed to establish a cushion that, in hindsight, they desperately needed.

The narrative of the first period was simple: Fossil Hockey controlled the pace, capitalized on defensive lapses, and built a 3-0 lead. Adam Donley added his first of two goals at the 7:58 mark, forcing the Ice Breakers to spend the first intermission rethinking their entire approach to tracking opponents in the slot.

The second period, however, offered a glimpse of life from the league’s cellar-dwellers. After Jerry Larue sat for a hooking minor, the Ice Breakers somehow found their rhythm. Gage Walker put the visitors on the board, and while Donley responded to restore a three-goal lead, Tyler Waller began what would eventually become a one-man crusade against the Fossil goaltender. Waller’s second-period strike, aided by Joshua Pula, kept the Breakers within striking distance, heading into the final frame trailing 5-2.

The third period turned into a chaotic scoring carousel. Fossil Hockey saw Michael Hoey, Ethan Hilton, and Dave Griffiths—the latter keeping his goal-scoring streak alive—all find the back of the net to balloon the lead to 8-2. At this point, the spreadsheets suggested this was a closed case. Then, the discipline evaporated.

A double-penalty situation involving David Muckala and Daron Ross left Fossil Hockey shorthanded, and Tyler Waller decided he wasn’t going home without a statement. He buried two rapid-fire goals in the final minutes to complete a four-goal performance, a Herculean effort that gave the Ice Breakers a late-game surge that, while numerically impressive, proved too little, too late.

"I think we just got a little comfortable with the geometry of the lead," said one anonymous Fossil player, likely while shaking off the shock of those final four minutes. "When you’re up by six, the math says you win. When you’re up by two, the math says you need to start back-checking again."

For Fossil Hockey, the win moves them to 5-3-1, keeping them firmly in the hunt and capitalizing on their recent form. For the Ice Breakers Plus, who remain at 0-8-1, the search for that first elusive win continues. They may have lost the game, but watching Tyler Waller hunt down an empty net (and then some) was, if nothing else, a testament to playing until the final buzzer sounds—regardless of what the goal differential tells you.
