# Lodge Fires Up, Trims the Leafs in a 6-4 Barn Burner

**By: The Rinkside Reporter**
*July 13, 2026*

If you weren’t at the barn last night for the 8:45 puck drop, do yourself a favor: grab a tape of the game, find a beverage, and settle in. The Lodge and the Tulsa Leafs went to war under the bright lights, and by the time the final horn sounded, the ice was just as shredded as the nerves of the fans in the stands. In a display of pure, unadulterated grit, the Lodge clawed their way to a 6-4 victory, proving that when the chips are down, they know exactly how to cash in.

### First Period: A Tentative Start
The game opened with the intensity of a double-espresso at midnight. Both squads spent the first ten minutes testing the waters, trading hits and playing a physical game along the boards. The Leafs’ Dan Munson found himself cooling his heels in the sin bin for slashing at the 11:40 mark, but the Lodge couldn't capitalize on the power play. Instead, it was the Leafs who drew first blood. At 7:03, Jason Fennell found the back of the net off a sweet dish from Curtis Connors and Braden Trinka, silencing the Lodge faithful momentarily. 

### Second Period: The Lodge Awakens
If the first period was a cagey dance, the second was a full-blown mosh pit. Louis Luongo broke the seal for the Lodge at 13:56, courtesy of a Dustin Giebel feed, and the floodgates were officially open. 

The momentum swung like a pendulum in a hurricane. Jonathon Anderson—riding high on a point streak—ripped one home at 2:41, but the Leafs answered back just forty-one seconds later when Curtis Connors notched his second point of the night. Not to be outdone, Anderson responded at 6:30 with his second of the night, followed closely by Christopher Fleming, who buried a beauty set up by Scott Dorough. By the time Ben Gartner headed to the box for tripping at 9:21, the Lodge had successfully snatched the momentum, heading into the final frame with a lead that felt like it had legs.

### Third Period: The Closing Chaos
The final stanza was a masterclass in heart-attack hockey. The Lodge extended their lead early on the power play thanks to a clutch individual effort from Matthew Seddelmeyer at 14:14. 

The Leafs, however, refused to roll over. Douglas Moore proved he’s got a nose for the net, banging in two late goals to narrow the gap and force the Lodge to play some desperate defensive hockey. But with the clock bleeding out and the Leafs threatening to ruin the party, Jonathon Anderson put the game on ice—and himself in the history books for the night—with a highlight-reel hat trick goal with just 39.5 seconds left on the clock. 

A late interference call on Garrett Williams kept the Lodge on their toes until the bitter end, but the clock eventually bailed them out. 

### The Bottom Line
With this 6-4 win, the Lodge (3-2-2) climbs to 8 points, shaking off their recent skid and sliding into a much more comfortable position in the standings. They’ve got the wind at their back now, and if Anderson keeps playing like a man possessed, the rest of the league better take notice.

For the Tulsa Leafs (1-4-2), the result is a tough pill to swallow. They showed plenty of heart, especially from Fennell and Connors, but defensive lapses proved costly against a hungry Lodge squad. As the standings tighten, the Leafs are going to need to find a way to lock down the middle of the ice if they want to climb out of the basement.

**Final Score:** the Lodge 6, Tulsa Leafs 4.
