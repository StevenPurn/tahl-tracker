# The Mafia Establishes Order with 6-3 Victory Over Rohan Riders

It took until late September, but The Mafia has finally shed the "all ties, no wins" label that had defined their season. In a dominant display at the rink on Wednesday night, The Mafia dismantled the Rohan Riders 6-3, proving that their high-octane offensive production was far more than a statistical anomaly.

The tone was set early, even if the scoreboard didn’t reflect it until midway through the first period. After surviving an early trip to the penalty box courtesy of Myriah Karo, The Mafia’s offense went nuclear. Brett Lamb, riding the momentum of a hot stick, opened the scoring at 14:09, assisted by Joshua Hendrickson. Just 30 seconds later, Hendrickson decided one point wasn't enough, burying an unassisted goal to double the lead. 

The Riders, who entered the contest looking to break a stalemate streak of their own, were clearly rattled. They conceded again at 6:24 to Tracy Wilmes, and before the crowd could even settle in, Brian Maher found the back of the net off a nifty feed from Scott Hellard and Brett Lamb. By the time the dust settled on the first frame, The Mafia held a commanding 4-0 lead, leaving the Riders’ goaltending shell-shocked.

"When you look at the shot volume and the transition efficiency in that first period, it was essentially a masterclass in puck possession," noted one rink-side observer, clutching a clipboard with enough data points to confuse a NASA engineer. "The Mafia simply didn't let the Riders breathe, let alone organize a breakout."

The second period saw Brett Lamb continue his dominant campaign, netting his second goal of the night at 13:09 to push the lead to 5-0. However, the Riders finally found a flicker of life when Aivar Kant took an interference penalty. While one might expect the power play to favor the offense, the Riders used the transition to set up Glenn Prouse, who capitalized on a pass from Robert Millea and Mitchel Pierce to break the shutout at 9:12.

The Mafia responded with their usual clinical efficiency. Joshua Hendrickson grabbed his second goal of the night at 9:54, assisted by Gage Walker, effectively slamming the door on any hopes of a Riders comeback. 

To their credit, the Riders refused to disappear in the third period. Glenn Prouse notched his second goal of the game at 6:44 off a feed from Alexander Rowin, and an anonymous contribution from a "Not Signed In" player—aided by William Coover—narrowed the gap to 6-3 with 8:02 remaining. Despite the late push, the deficit proved too steep to overcome, particularly against a Mafia squad that tightened their defensive shell after Scott Hellard’s third-period interference penalty.

For The Mafia, the win moves them to 1-0-2, catapulting them into the thick of the mid-table pack. The Riders drop to 0-1-2, leaving them searching for their first win of the campaign. While the "Stickler" notes that the Riders showed resilience in the final frame, the analytics don't lie: you cannot spot a team like The Mafia a 5-0 lead and expect the spreadsheets to balance in your favor by the final buzzer.
