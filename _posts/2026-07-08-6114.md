# A Scoring Storm at the Rink: Shooting Blanks Put on a Masterclass in 14-7 Romp

**By: Your Hometown Rink-Side Reporter**  
**Date: July 7, 2026**

If you didn’t get to the arena by 10:15 PM last night, you missed a total barn burner that turned into a full-blown bonfire. In a game that saw more rubber flying than a tire factory on a Friday night, the undefeated juggernaut Shooting Blanks dismantled the Tornadoes, 14-7. 

Coming in, the Blanks were sitting pretty at 5-0-0, while the Tornadoes were fighting to find their wind. For a hot minute in the first, it looked like we were in for an upset.

### Period 1: The Calm Before the Crimson Tide
The Tornadoes came out buzzing. Matthew Rankin opened the scoring early, followed by a snipe from Josh Moyer and a beautiful goal from Christian Alvegran to put the Tornadoes up 3-0. It was the kind of start that makes the home crowd stand up and take notice. But then, Bret Kurth—a man who seems to be allergic to *not* scoring—found the back of the net for the Blanks right before the buzzer. That goal was the spark that ignited the powder keg.

### Period 2: The Blanks Open the Floodgates
If the first period was a light breeze, the second was a Category 5 hurricane. The Tornadoes tried to stay in it, with Josh Moyer and Matthew Rankin adding to their tallies, but the Shooting Blanks went on an absolute tear. 

Aaron Best turned into a one-man wrecking crew, scoring back-to-back goals in less than twenty seconds. The momentum shift was palpable; the ice felt tilted, and the Tornadoes were gasping for oxygen. Between the blistering pace of Austin Tyler, the relentless pressure from Hunter Best, and Kurth continuing to find twine, the Blanks rattled off a scoring flurry that left the Tornadoes’ defense looking like they were skating in quicksand. 

By the time the dust settled on the second frame, the scoreboard was glowing with a staggering number of tallies. 

### Period 3: The Best Show in Town
The third period was all about the Blanks cementing their status as the kings of the league. Hunter Best and Aaron Best continued to punish the net, while the playmaking chemistry between Austin Tyler, David Best, and Bret Kurth was nothing short of symphony-level synergy. 

The Tornadoes continued to fight, but the Blanks’ transition game was relentless. Every time the Tornadoes poked their heads up, the Blanks buried another one "top shelf where mom keeps the cookies." When the final buzzer rang, it was 14-7 in favor of the visitors.

### What This Means for the Table
For the **Shooting Blanks (6-0-0)**, this is a statement win. They aren’t just winning; they are putting the entire league on notice that they are playing a different version of hockey. With Bret Kurth extending his point streak and the Best brothers essentially playing their own game out there, they are the team to beat.

For the **Tornadoes (2-5-0)**, it’s a tough night at the office. They showed flashes of brilliance early on—they can clearly put the puck in the net—but they’ll need to tighten up their defensive zone coverage if they want to stop the bleeding. They’ll look to regroup, but the league standings are tightening up, and they’ll need to find their rhythm before the playoff picture gets even murkier.

Keep your skates sharp, folks. If the rest of the season is half as chaotic as last night, we’re in for one heck of a ride.
