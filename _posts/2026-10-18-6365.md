# Riveters Edge Bad Bees in Seven-Goal Thriller as Gibson Goes Nuclear

If you were looking for a defensive masterclass on Friday night, you picked the wrong sheet of ice. The Tulsa Riveters and the Bad Bees turned the arena into a shooting gallery, eventually settling for a 7-6 nail-biter that saw the Riveters cling to their spot at the top of the standings.

The headline tonight is, unequivocally, Gabi Gibson. If you didn’t know her name before, you’d have to be living under a Zamboni not to know it now. Gibson put on a clinic, registering an staggering five goals in regulation and then casually stepping up to bury the deciding goal in the shootout. She wasn't just finding the back of the net; she was auditioning for a highlight reel that doesn't exist yet.

The Riveters wasted no time signaling their intent, with Gibson opening the scoring at the 11:18 mark of the first. But the Bad Bees, riding a strong run of form themselves, weren't about to roll over. Whenever the Riveters looked to pull away—like when Theresa Rose Rabara doubled the lead midway through the first—the Bees buzzed right back. The first period alone was a back-and-forth affair that left the timekeepers dizzy, ending with the Riveters nursing a slim lead.

The second period was where the game truly descended into glorious, structured chaos. The two teams traded goals like they were trying to fulfill a collective quota. Grace Wolff and Lauren A. Mincey kept the Bees aggressive, while Gibson continued her personal crusade against the opposing goaltender. By the time the buzzer sounded on the middle frame, the scoreline looked more like a mid-season baseball game than a hockey match.

"I think we forgot that goaltenders are allowed to make saves," one Riveters bench staffer joked as the players headed to the locker room for the intermission. "It’s hockey, not a tennis match."

The intensity peaked in the third. With the Riveters clinging to their lead, the Bees' Grace Wolff struck again at 11:03 to keep the pressure mounting. Discipline became an issue late in the game, with both sides trading trips to the box for tripping and hooking, forcing both benches to shorten their rotations and lean on their tired veterans to close the gap.

Despite the defensive lapses—which I’m sure both coaches will be dissecting with plenty of coffee and frustration this weekend—the finish was pure theater. With the regulation clock showing a 6-6 deadlock, the game moved to a shootout. It felt almost scripted that the puck found its way to Gibson’s stick for the clincher. She made it look like a casual Tuesday morning practice, sliding it home to secure the win and the extra point.

For the Riveters, this win keeps them at the summit of the league, defending their 3-0-2 record with a victory that was as messy as it was effective. The Bad Bees walk away with a loss, but they proved that they can hang with the league leaders punch-for-punch. It wasn't pretty, it wasn't textbook, but it was exactly the kind of local hockey that keeps the seats filled.
