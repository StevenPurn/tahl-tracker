# Fossils Turn Back the Clock in 9-3 Rout of Danglers

If you were looking for a defensive masterclass on Tuesday night, you were at the wrong rink. Fossil Hockey decided that the best way to spend a Tuesday evening was to turn the ice into their personal playground, hanging nine goals on the Dirty Danglers in a 9-3 lopsided affair that left the Danglers looking like they were skating in a wind tunnel.

The tone for the evening was set early—and I mean early. Frederic Bassett wasted no time finding his rhythm, netting two goals in the first three minutes of play. By the time the first period buzzer rang, the Fossils had already built a 4-0 lead, with Logan Pardue and Enoch Cox chipping in to essentially put the game out of reach before most of the spectators had settled into their seats with their lukewarm rink coffee.

The Danglers, to their credit, didn’t roll over, though they did spend a good portion of the middle frame flirting with the penalty box. Ethan Hilton’s slashing penalty was just the start of a scrappy, frustrated stretch for the visitors. However, once the dust settled, the Danglers finally found some daylight. Brett Mooney and Stephen Jeffery managed back-to-back goals late in the second period, momentarily breathing some life into a bench that had looked like it was mourning a lost cause.

"We figured if we just kept shooting, eventually one would have to go in," Mooney joked after the game. "Unfortunately, we decided to wait until we were already halfway to a blowout before we started doing it."

Any hope of a miraculous comeback was swiftly extinguished in the third. Logan Pardue, who seemed to have a magnetic connection with the back of the net all night, completed his hat trick—and then some—finishing the night with four goals. His relentless pace, combined with Alan Dyke’s pair of third-period markers, turned the latter half of the final frame into little more than a victory lap for the Fossils.

The Danglers’ defense, which has struggled with consistency all season, looked disjointed, leaving their netminder to face a barrage of high-quality chances. On the flip side, the Fossils played a disciplined, if offensively greedy, game, only handing the referee a reason to blow the whistle once—a holding call against Bassett that proved to be entirely inconsequential given the scoreboard.

For Fossil Hockey, the win is a welcome boost, pushing them to 3-2-1 and keeping them firmly in the middle-of-the-pack conversation. As for the Dirty Danglers, they’ll need to do some soul-searching before their next outing. Falling to 2-4-1, the Danglers are currently stuck in a cycle of inconsistency that would make a weather vane dizzy. 

If they want to climb out of the basement of the standings, they’ll need to find a way to stop the bleeding—literally and figuratively—before they find themselves completely written out of the narrative for the rest of the season.
