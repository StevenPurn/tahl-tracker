### Period 1: The Tornadoes Strike First, But Can’t Hold the Gale
The Tornadoes entered the game looking to build on their early-season momentum, and things started perfectly. Despite Nicklas Clark heading to the sin bin for slashing at the 11-minute mark, the Tornadoes struck shorthanded—or rather, just as the power play fizzled—when Christopher Cieslak hammered one home off helpers from Michael Alfaro and Steven Purn.

But Oklahoma Precast wasn’t rattled. They found their groove quickly, flipping the ice with a vengeance. Emil Hinkefent, Matthew Rankin, and James Stuckey rattled off three consecutive goals to close the first, turning a 1-0 deficit into a 3-1 lead. Michael Bergstrom managed to stem the tide for the Tornadoes with a gritty, unassisted individual effort, but the Precast headed into the locker room holding the momentum and a 3-2 lead.

### Period 2: A Scoring Carousel
If the first period was a spark, the second was a forest fire. Hinkefent and Stuckey opened the frame with back-to-back tallies, putting the Tornadoes in a deep hole. Wolfgang Baran offered a glimmer of hope with a beautiful sequence from Cieslak and Clark, but the Precast offense—led by a relentless Matthew Rankin—refused to let the Tornadoes breathe.

By the time the buzzer ended the second period, the scoreboard was glowing with a dizzying array of numbers. Bergstrom grabbed his second of the night for the Tornadoes, but Rocky Ricci’s late-period strike ensured the Precast kept the Tornadoes at arm's length. It was a track meet, and the Tornadoes were struggling to keep up with the Precast’s heavy-handed forecheck.

### Period 3: Closing the Curtain on a Wild One
The final frame saw both teams trading penalties as the frustration—and fatigue—began to set in. James Stuckey hit his hat-trick mark early in the period, and while the Tornadoes made a valiant push with late goals from Baran, Brandt Mueller, and a second from Cieslak, the hole was just too deep.

Matthew Rankin and Rocky Ricci punctuated the night with late goals for Oklahoma Precast, icing the game and silencing the hometown crowd. Despite some stellar individual efforts—namely Cieslak and Bergstrom doing everything in their power to keep the Tornadoes afloat—the sheer offensive depth of Oklahoma Precast was the difference-maker.
