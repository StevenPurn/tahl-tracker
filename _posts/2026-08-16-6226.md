# Birds Fly High in 7-6 Barnburner Over Havok

If you like your hockey served with a side of defensive indifference and enough scoreboard movement to give a timekeeper carpal tunnel, then the 11:15 a.m. matinee at the rink was exactly what the doctor ordered. The league-leading Dirty Birds extended their winning streak, besting a desperate Havok squad 7-6 in a game that felt less like a tactical matchup and more like a high-speed game of dodgeball on ice.

The Birds came into the building looking every bit like the top-ranked team in the standings, treating the first two periods like a private shooting gallery. By the time the dust settled on a frantic second period, the Birds had established a lead that seemed insurmountable. Kyle Rachal opened the scoring, but the real story was the middle frame, where Nicklas Clark went on a one-man wrecking crew mission, potting a hat trick in a span of time that left the Havok coaching staff staring blankly at the ceiling tiles in search of divine intervention.

Down 6-1, most teams would have opted to pack their bags and beat the traffic out of the parking lot. To their credit, Havok—who have been struggling through a rough stretch of late—decided to stop playing like spectators and started acting like hockey players. 

Emil Hinkefent sparked the resurrection, and suddenly, the ice tilted. Hinkefent was a man possessed, finishing the afternoon with a hat trick of his own. As the third period wore on, Havok’s bench grew louder, and the Dirty Birds’ defense began to look a bit like a screen door in a hurricane. Zachary Wade kept the pressure on, and when Hinkefent buried his third of the game with just minutes remaining, the Dirty Birds’ comfortable cushion had withered to a single goal.

However, the late-game momentum shift came with a price. Havok’s Ethan Charny spent nearly as much time in the box as he did on the ice, culminating in a 10-minute unsportsmanlike conduct penalty in the final minute that effectively stifled Havok’s last-gasp effort to find an equalizer. Even with Logan Singleton cutting the lead to one late in the frame, the clock proved to be the ultimate defender for the Birds.

"We played about forty-five minutes of excellent hockey and about fifteen minutes of 'let's see if we can give the coach a heart attack,'" remarked one Dirty Birds skater while untying his skates. "It wasn't pretty, but in this league, a win is a win, even if you leave a few years off your life expectancy in the process."

The Dirty Birds continue their reign atop the standings, remaining the team to beat as they march through the season. For Havok, the loss marks another tough result in a difficult stretch, though their third-period surge provides a glimmer of hope that they can find their form before the end of the season. 

It was a reminder that even when the defensive structures are optional, local hockey remains the most unpredictable, frustrating, and exhilarating show in town. The rink lights might have dimmed, but the defensive coaches are surely still having nightmares about those thirteen total goals.
