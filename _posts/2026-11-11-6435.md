# Joes Edge Greasers in Shootout Thriller

If you like your hockey served with a side of chaos and a complete disregard for defensive structure, Monday night’s tilt between the Average Joes and the Tulsa Greasers was exactly the kind of mess that makes beer league hockey the beautiful disaster it is. When the dust finally settled at the rink, the Average Joes skated away with a 5-4 victory, courtesy of a Ryan Martinez shootout winner that sent the Greasers packing with another frustrating loss.

It was a night where the goaltenders were likely left wondering if their defensemen had actually decided to take a permanent sabbatical in the neutral zone. The Greasers, desperate to snap a three-game skid, came out swinging, with Dylan Walker effectively deciding he was going to carry the entire team on his back. Walker netted a hat trick, starting with a shorthanded unassisted effort just 19 seconds into a James Caldron penalty. He’d strike again in the first and add a third in the final frame, looking like the only guy in the building who had actually eaten a pre-game meal.

The Joes, however, have a habit of staying in games longer than their opponents would prefer. Joshua Hendrickson was the man of the hour for the winners, orchestrating a personal three-goal clinic that spanned the first and second periods. His chemistry with Ryan Martinez—who extended his goal streak to five games in regulation before playing hero in the shootout—kept the Joes afloat while the Greasers were busy trading punches.

The turning point? It wasn't a tactical masterclass or a bench-clearing brawl, though tempers flared momentarily when Tracy Wilmes headed to the box for roughing in the first. Instead, it was the sheer exhaustion of two teams who seemed to realize midway through the second period that they were trading scoring chances like they were playing in an open-ice pond hockey tournament. Mitchell Buckmaster did his part to keep the Greasers in it with a second-period marker, but for every step forward the Greasers took, Hendrickson seemed to have a counter-punch ready.

"I’m just glad we don't have to play these guys every night," said one Joes bench warmer, clutching an oversized water bottle after the game. "I think both teams combined for about four actual 'defensive plays' all night. It was high-event, high-blood-pressure stuff."

The shootout, mercifully, didn't last long. With the tension thick enough to cut with a dull skate blade, Martinez stepped up and buried the deciding shot, effectively securing the two points for the Joes. 

For the Average Joes, the win keeps them firmly in the middle of the pack, hovering at 4-2-1 and maintaining a respectable rhythm in what has been a topsy-turvy season. The Greasers, now sitting at 1-5-2, have to be kicking themselves. They fought hard, they got the scoring, but when you look at the defensive miscues, you can see exactly why they’re struggling to find the win column. 

It wasn't pretty, it wasn't disciplined, and it certainly wasn't going to be taught in any coaching clinics across the country. But for those of us watching from the stands? It was just another night at the rink, and honestly, you couldn't ask for much more.
