# Goats Graze Past Hitmen in Third-Period Thriller

If you like your hockey served with a side of high-octane drama and a dash of late-game chaos, the Killer Goats and Hitmen Silver delivered the goods on Sunday night. In a back-and-forth battle that kept the fans at the edge of their seats until the final buzzer, the Goats managed to edge out the Hitmen 5-4, effectively putting a halt to the Hitmen’s four-game heater.

The game started with a controlled burn, as Nelson Pina opened the scoring for the Goats midway through the first period off a crisp feed from the league’s hottest hand, Dylan Criquet Danielson. The Hitmen, playing with the confidence of a team that hadn't tasted defeat in nearly a month, fired back when Nicklas Clark extended his personal goal streak to five games, knotting things up at 1-1 before the first intermission.

The second period turned into a penalty-box parade, but it was the Goats who capitalized on the mess. With the Hitmen’s discipline wavering, Lucas Blanchard found a seam to put the Goats back on top. 

"It was like trying to herd actual goats out there at times," joked one Goats supporter after the game. "Everything was moving, nobody was standing still, and somehow we kept finding the back of the net."

The real fireworks, however, were saved for a wild third period. After trailing 2-1, the Hitmen surged with goals from Calvin Baker and Brett Barrick to flip the script, taking a 3-2 lead and silencing the Goats' bench. But just when it looked like the Hitmen were poised to cruise, the Goats decided to put their horns down and charge. 

Michael Doyle answered the Hitmen’s rally just over a minute after Barrick's tally, sparking a frantic offensive flurry. With the Hitmen suddenly facing a parade of whistles—including a pair of simultaneous penalties for Bobby Pickett and Josh Moyer—the Goats’ power play finally hummed to life. Dylan Criquet Danielson extended his impressive point streak by finding the back of the net, and Brian Smith added what would prove to be the deciding goal just moments later.

Though Josh Moyer managed to claw one back for the Hitmen with less than 20 seconds remaining on the clock—keeping his own four-game goal streak alive—it was too little, too late to derail the Goats.

"They're a tough squad, but we kept our composure when the game threatened to turn into a demolition derby," said a visibly relieved Goats locker room source. 

The win is a massive morale booster for the Killer Goats, who remain firmly planted in the second seed, while the Hitmen Silver see their impressive winning streak snapped. As the season approaches the home stretch, this victory proves that the Goats are more than ready for a fight to the finish.
