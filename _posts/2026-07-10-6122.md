# Blue Bombers Blow the Doors Off in 14-6 Goal-Fest Against Tuskens

**By: The Rink-Side Reporter**  
*July 9, 2026 – Late Night at the Rink*

If you were looking for a quiet, defensive chess match on this Tuesday night, you were at the wrong barn. If you were looking for enough offense to make a scoreboard operator contemplate retirement, you were in the right place. The Blue Bombers (4-2-0) absolutely dismantled the Tulsa Tuskens (0-5-0) tonight, lighting the lamp 14 times in a 14-6 blowout that felt more like a batting practice session than a hockey game.

### First Period: The Bombers Set the Fuse
From the opening puck drop, the Bombers looked like they had been mainlining espresso. Hayden Sixsmith kept his hot streak alive, opening the floodgates at 10:39, and by the time the first frame was in the books, the Bombers had hung a massive six-spot on the board. 

Daron Ross, Frank Post, and Aaron West all joined the party, but it was Samuel Northup—who clearly decided he didn’t want to go home early—who buried a beauty at the 5:50 mark. Sixsmith added his second of the night in the waning seconds of the period, proving that "traffic in front of the net" is only dangerous if you’re the one trying to clear the bodies.

### Second Period: Pure Chaos
If the first period was a light shower, the second was a monsoon. The Bombers opened up the scoring again, but the Tuskens finally found a pulse. Matthew Patrick broke the seal for Tulsa, sparking a frantic sequence of back-and-forth action.

Midway through the second, we witnessed a legendary stretch of hockey. Between 1:15 and 6:04 on the game clock, the teams traded goals like they were playing Pong. Samuel Northup was the story here; he was hovering around the crease like a ghost, putting home goal after goal. By the time he netted his fourth of the game, he had effectively turned the Tulsa crease into his own personal living room. 

Not to be outdone, Tulsa’s Dylan Walker decided he was going to turn this into a one-man show, racking up a hat-trick of his own in the blink of an eye. Despite the Tuskens’ late-period spark, the sheer depth of the Bombers' lineup—with Travis Shuck and Chris Olsen chipping in—was simply too much for the Tuskens' beleaguered netminder to handle.

### Third Period: Coasting to the Finish
After a 20-goal total through two periods, the pace predictably slowed in the final frame. Dylan Walker, clearly refusing to give up, added one final tally at 2:41, but the game was already well out of reach. The Bombers’ defense tightened up, and the clock ticked down on a game that will surely be remembered by the folks in the press box as a true "barn burner."

### What This Means
For the **Blue Bombers**, this win moves them to 4-2-0 on the season, helping them keep pace with the upper echelon of the standings. With momentum building and the top-line guns like Northup and Sixsmith finding the back of the net with terrifying consistency, they are starting to look like a team that could climb into that top-three spot sooner rather than later.

As for the **Tulsa Tuskens**, the search for that elusive first win continues. Dropping to 0-5-0 is a tough pill to swallow, especially when the scoreboard gets lit up like a Christmas tree. However, seeing Dylan Walker put up four goals is a glimmer of hope. They’ll need to stop the bleeding in their own end, but they’ve got a long season ahead to find their footing.

**Final Score:** Blue Bombers 14, Tulsa Tuskens 6.
