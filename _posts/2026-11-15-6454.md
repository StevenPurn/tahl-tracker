# Trinka’s Triple-Threat Leads Leafs Past Laser

If you were looking for a highlight reel on a Saturday afternoon, you didn't have to look further than the ice at the local rink, where Braden Trinka put on a masterclass in scoring to lead the Tulsa Leafs to a 5-3 victory over the Laser. 

The Laser actually struck first, finding the back of the net at 10:43 in the first period when David Mansfield tucked one home off a sweet feed from Nic Poverud. But if the Laser thought they were in for a quiet day at the office, Braden Trinka had other plans. Trinka went on an absolute tear, firing off a hat trick that spanned the first period and into the third. By the time the dust settled, Trinka had single-handedly shifted the momentum of the game.

"I think the puck just wanted to hang out with me today," Trinka said after the game, still catching his breath. "I wasn't doing anything fancy, just finding those soft spots and hoping the twine would hold up to the pressure."

The Leafs weren't just a one-man show, though. Curtis Connors kept his hot streak alive with an unassisted laser—pun intended—at the 8:09 mark of the second period. Connors has been living in the highlight reel lately, extending his goal-scoring streak to four games. Even when the Laser tried to claw their way back in the third period with a goal from Chad Pancoast, the Leafs responded with a "anything you can do, I can do better" attitude. 

Trinka notched his fourth goal of the game at 2:11 of the third, silencing a brief Laser rally. The game got a little chippy late in the third with double-minors to Ronald Herwig and Nathan Hogue, adding some salt to the Laser’s wounds, but the damage was already done. 

Despite the loss, the Laser showed some late-game grit when Timothy N See found the back of the net at 8:41, proving that this squad refuses to go home without a fight. See’s goal extended his own impressive point streak, serving as a bright spot for a team that has been searching for consistency.

For the Tulsa Leafs, this win is a massive confidence booster. Moving to 3-3-2 on the season, they’ve snapped their recent slump and proven that when their offense is clicking, they can skate with anyone in the league. As for the Laser, they fall to 1-6-2, but with individual efforts like Pancoast and See lighting the lamp, they’ll be looking to turn those close calls into points sooner rather than later. 

It wasn't a pretty game for the Laser’s defensive core, but for the fans in the stands, it was a high-octane affair that reminded everyone exactly why we love this game: you never know whose stick is going to get hot next.
