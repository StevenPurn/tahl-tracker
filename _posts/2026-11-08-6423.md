# Mooseknuckles Run Wild in 10-1 Rout of Tulsa Murder

If you’re a fan of high-scoring affairs, the 12:45 p.m. matinee at the rink was exactly what the doctor ordered. If you’re a fan of the Tulsa Murder, however, you likely spent the better part of the afternoon wishing you’d stayed in bed. In a game that quickly spiraled into a statement of dominance, the Mooseknuckles dismantled the Murder by a final score of 10-1, proving that when the wheels fall off, they tend to bounce all the way into the parking lot.

The Mooseknuckles came out looking like they’d had their morning coffee, while the Murder seemed to be skating through wet cement. Despite an early hooking penalty by Donny Griffin that might have given Tulsa a glimmer of hope, it was actually the Mooseknuckles who drew first blood—while shorthanded. Jasen Lawwill opened the account just over a minute into the first period, and from there, the floodgates didn't just open; they were ripped off the hinges.

By the time the horn sounded on the first, the Mooseknuckles held a comfortable 3-0 lead thanks to goals from Scott Bartkus and Benjamin Savory. For the Murder, the first period was a masterclass in how to give up high-quality chances. 

The Murder finally found a pulse early in the second period when Kurtis Henderson buried a feed from Spencer Charles to make it 3-1. For about twenty seconds, it felt like a game. Then, reality set in. KYLE OKUROWSKI, who evidently decided that the goal crease was his personal real estate, proceeded to dismantle the Murder’s defensive structure single-handedly. 

Okurowskis’s hat trick performance—which eventually grew into a four-goal effort—was the story of the afternoon. He was everywhere the puck was, and the Murder’s defensemen seemed content to offer him a guided tour of the slot rather than challenging him for the disc. 

"I think we just forgot that you're supposed to cover the guy who keeps putting the puck in the net," one frustrated Murder player muttered toward the bench halfway through the second. His coach didn't have much to say, mostly because he was too busy watching the scoreboard climb into double digits.

The discipline issues didn't help matters for Tulsa either. Trips and holds peppered the second period, keeping the Murder short-handed and exhausted while the Mooseknuckles’ Garrett Williams racked up assists like he was padding a resume. By the time Michael Pacheco and Garrett Williams added their own tallies in the third to round out the 10-1 scoreline, the atmosphere in the rink had shifted from competitive hockey to a lighthearted curiosity about how high the number would actually go.

For the Mooseknuckles, the win is a massive morale booster that keeps them hovering in the mix of the top four teams in the standings. They played a disciplined, opportunistic game that turned a mid-day regular season matchup into a lopsided affair. As for the Murder, they’ll need to do some serious soul-searching before their next outing; giving up ten goals is the kind of math that keeps goalies awake at night and keeps coaches staring at the ceiling tiles during film study. 

It wasn't a clinic in defensive hockey, but for the Mooseknuckles, it was a clinic in capitalizing on every lapse in judgment the opposition could provide. And in this league, that's usually all you need.
