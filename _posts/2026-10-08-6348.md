# The Mafia Hand Mighty Drunks Their First Loss of the Season

In a rink that smelled of stale coffee and industrial-strength disinfectant, The Mafia proved that slow and steady can indeed win the race, knocking off the Mighty Drunks 3-1 on Wednesday night. While the Drunks came in riding high as the number two seed, they left the ice looking like a team that had finally run into a brick wall—or at least a very organized defense.

The Drunks drew first blood early, with Cian Slattery cleaning up a dish from Connor Slattery at the 10:29 mark of the opening period. For a moment, it looked like business as usual for the Drunks, who have been flirting with the top of the standings all season. However, Jose Herrick had other plans for the Mafia, evening the score just over two minutes later after a smart setup from Mark Blevins. 

It was a scrappy, no-nonsense affair from there. The middle frame was defined by a shift in momentum that caught the Drunks flat-footed. At 2:13, Jacob Meyer buried a feed from Brett Lamb, giving the Mafia a 2-1 lead they seemed determined to guard with their lives. Lamb, who entered the night on a two-game goal streak, showed he’s more than happy to play the role of provider when the lanes open up.

"We knew we couldn't out-run them all night, so we decided to just out-wait them," said one Mafia veteran after the game, declining to be named but noted for having a particularly nasty stretch of tape on his twig. "They like to play fast; we like to play hockey."

The third period turned into a masterclass in tension management. The Drunks attempted to manufacture some urgency, but their efforts were largely stymied by a Mafia defensive unit that played with the kind of discipline you rarely see in a late-night beer league. Nathan Fahland’s trip to the box for roughing at 10:46 of the third was the death knell for the Drunks' comeback hopes, as it forced them to burn precious time killing off a penalty rather than pushing for the equalizer.

With just 59.8 seconds left on the clock, Gage Walker put the game on ice with an unassisted empty-netter. It was a cold-blooded finish that extended Walker’s point streak to three games and officially snapped the Drunks' momentum.

For the Mighty Drunks, the loss is their first of the season, a reality check that sends them back to the drawing board to reconsider their defensive structure. For The Mafia, this win pushes them to a 2-0-2 record and keeps their undefeated streak alive—a quiet, grinding success that reminds everyone why you don’t overlook a team just because they aren’t making a lot of noise in the standings. 

If they keep playing with this kind of composure, The Mafia might just be the team nobody wants to see in the late stages of the season. For now, they’ll enjoy the win, and the Drunks will look to wash the taste of this one out of their mouths—preferably with something other than the locker room water cooler.
