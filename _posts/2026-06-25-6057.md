# From a Tight Start to a Total Rout: Renegades Run Wild Over Oklahoma Precast

**OKLAHOMA CITY** – If you’re a fan of defensive stalemates, you probably left the rink at 10:30 PM last night wishing you’d stayed home to watch paint dry. But if you like your hockey served with a side of absolute offensive chaos, the Renegades put on a clinic that would make a goal judge’s arm sore. 

In a late-night tilt that escalated from a competitive scrap into a 14-5 landslide, the Renegades dismantled Oklahoma Precast, proving that while Precast might have the concrete in their name, their defense was anything but solid on Wednesday night.

### Period 1: The Illusion of a Game
The opening frame was a tease. Nicholas Power opened the account for Oklahoma Precast at the 13:44 mark, and for a fleeting moment, it looked like we were in for a classic barn burner. 

However, the Renegades found their legs as the period wore on. LOGAN SINGLETON kicked off the comeback party, and despite a gutsy response from MATTHEW RANKIN—who extended his goal-scoring streak to three games—the Renegades began to turn the screws. By the time Adam Carter buried a helper from Power to keep it close, the writing was on the wall. The ice was tilting, and the Precast goaltender was starting to see more rubber than a tire factory.

### Period 2: The Floodgates Burst
The second period was where the wheels came off the Precast wagon. The Renegades came out of the locker room looking like they were shot out of a cannon. 

Bobby Watson and the ever-dangerous ALEX BATEMAN began carving up the zone. BATEMAN, riding a serious hot streak, netted his second of the night at 11:02, and suddenly the Renegades were playing keep-away. PHILLIP CAPUTO joined the buffet, scoring two quick ones to turn a competitive game into a runaway train. By the time the buzzer sounded on the second, the scoreboard read like a lopsided math problem.

### Period 3: Total Offensive Saturation
The third period was essentially a Renegades victory lap. JOSHUA FARRELL, Hunter Bemies, and Alex Crowder took turns finding the back of the net, reminding everyone in the arena why they sit second in the standings. 

Even when Oklahoma Precast managed to find the twine—thanks to a late effort from CARLEE TURNER and another marker from Adam Carter—the Renegades responded with a flurry of goals that erased any hope of a comeback. Hunter Bemies was particularly clinical, completing a multi-goal performance that left the Precast defenders looking like traffic cones in a construction zone.

### The Breakdown
This was a performance defined by high-octane chemistry. ALEX BATEMAN was the architect of destruction, consistently finding open ice, while PHILLIP CAPUTO acted as the primary distributor, picking apart the Precast defensive coverage with ease. 

**The Standings Impact:**
With this 14-5 shellacking, the Renegades (3-2-0) firmly entrench themselves in the second seed, keeping the pressure on the top-ranked Shooting Blanks. They’ve proven that when their offense clicks, they are arguably the most dangerous team in the league. 

For Oklahoma Precast (1-5-0), it’s back to the drawing board. After a tough run of games, they’ll need to shore up the gaps in their defensive zone before their next outing. They showed flashes of offensive potential tonight, but in this league, you can’t win by trading blows when the other team is throwing haymakers.

The breeze from the skates tonight? It felt a lot like a whirlwind. The Renegades aren't just playing for points; they’re playing for style points, and they’re winning in that category, too.
