# Yetis Run Wild in 9-1 Shellacking of Mojo

If you blinked on Saturday night, you might have missed a goal. The Yetis turned the ice into their personal playground at the 8:15 tilt, steamrolling Mojo in a 9-1 performance that had the home crowd buzzing and the Mojo defense searching for answers that just weren’t there.

The Yetis came into this regular-season clash looking to capitalize on their recent hot streak, and they didn't waste a second. STEVEN BELANGER opened the floodgates early in the first, setting the tone just over 12 minutes in. Michael Pacheco, who was arguably the star of the show, added his first of two goals shortly after, ensuring the Yetis headed into the first intermission with a commanding 2-0 cushion.

If the first period was a warm-up, the second was a full-blown blizzard. The Yetis offense clicked with clockwork precision, rattling off four unanswered goals in the middle frame. KIRK NIELSEN got the party started, followed by Jason Bahr—who extended his impressive point streak to five games—along with RICHARD RETKOWSKI and a second marker from Belanger. By the time the horn sounded for the second intermission, the Yetis held a staggering 6-0 lead.

Mojo finally broke the goose egg late in the third when David Lawrence found the back of the net to avoid the shutout, but by then, the Yeti train had already left the station. Belanger completed a massive hat-trick performance midway through the third, while Retkowski and Pacheco tacked on insurance goals to seal the 9-1 final.

"I think we just found a rhythm early," said a grinning Pacheco, who finished the night with two goals and two assists. "Sometimes the puck just finds the back of the net, and tonight it felt like it had a magnet in it."

For Mojo, it was a night to forget, but they’ll have to shake it off quickly. The loss keeps them at 2-3-3 on the season, struggling to find consistency in a crowded middle of the standings. Meanwhile, the Yetis continue their climb, moving to 4-2-1 and proving that when their transition game is firing on all cylinders, they are one of the most dangerous squads in the league.

The Yetis’ discipline was solid throughout, outside of a brief stint in the box for Belanger in the third, but they largely stayed out of their own way. As for Mojo, they’ll look to reload and regroup after a defensive performance that coach described as "trying to stop a hurricane with a hair dryer." 

The Yetis now ride a three-game winning streak into their next outing, while Mojo will be looking for a much-needed bounce-back performance to get their season back on track.
