# Bad Bees Sting Power Puck Girls in 4-2 Victory

It was a chilly Saturday morning at the rink, the kind where the coffee is lukewarm and the Zamboni tracks are still fresh, but the Bad Bees didn't seem to mind the early start. They buzzed past the Power Puck Girls 4-2 on November 1st, proving that while their recent form had been a bit erratic, their competitive edge remains sharp.

The Bees came out swinging early in the first period, with Jessica Harper finding the back of the net at the 14:08 mark, courtesy of a tidy setup from Lauren A. Mincey. It was a textbook start for the Bees, who seemed content to play a puck-possession game. However, the Power Puck Girls—currently searching for a rhythm to lift them out of the basement of the standings—refused to roll over. 

The middle frame turned into a tactical grind. The Power Puck Girls managed to level the score at 13:08 when Lindsey Smith buried a feed from Addison Gaut. Just when it looked like the momentum might swing toward the Girls, Remisha Blation stepped up for the Bees. With 3:27 left in the second, she managed to poke home an unassisted goal, a gritty individual effort that reminded everyone why you never leave a forward unchecked in the slot.

The turning point of the morning came early in the third. With Andrea Evans sitting in the box for a roughing minor, the Bees’ penalty kill unit did something unexpected: they scored. Blation notched her second of the day just 14 seconds into the power play, assisted by Harper, effectively taking the wind out of the Power Puck sails.

To their credit, the Power Puck Girls kept clawing back. Suzanne Hawkins capitalized on a scramble to score an unassisted goal at 5:31, bringing the score to 3-2 and putting a flicker of doubt into the Bees’ bench. But the Bees shut the door for good at the 6:56 mark. Kristin Brooks hammered home the final goal of the game, set up by Grace Wolff, to secure the 4-2 win.

"We played okay in patches," one anonymous bench observer noted, "but if we keep giving up odd-man rushes, we’re going to be spending our Saturday afternoons chasing ghosts instead of the puck."

For the Bad Bees, the win is a crucial correction for their mid-season record, moving them to 4-2-1 and keeping them firmly in the hunt among the top three. The Power Puck Girls drop to 1-6-1, and while the effort was there, they’ll need to tighten up their defensive zone coverage if they hope to climb out of the bottom spot as the season grinds on. It wasn't always pretty, but in this league, four goals are four goals—and for the Bees, that’s all that matters.
