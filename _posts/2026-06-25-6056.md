# The Blanks Keep Firing: Tornadoes Swept Away in 6-1 Rout

**By: "The Rink Rat" Reporter**

If you came to the arena on the evening of June 24th hoping to see the Tornadoes disrupt the league leaders, you might have left feeling a bit chilly. The Shooting Blanks rolled into town with their sights set on perfection, and let me tell you, they didn’t miss. In a 6-1 display of pure offensive efficiency, the Blanks proved exactly why they are sitting pretty at the top of the food chain.

### First Period: The Watson Warning
The game barely had time to get cold before Bobby Watson decided to make his presence known. At the 1:50 mark, Watson buried a beauty off feeds from Bret Kurth and Brett Allen to put the Blanks on the board early. 

The Tornadoes tried to find their footing, but the Blanks’ defense was playing like a locked vault. When Tobias Alvegran headed to the sin bin for a trip at 8:08, the Tornadoes managed to kill the penalty, but the momentum remained firmly planted in the offensive zone of the visitors.

### Second Period: The Tug-of-War
The second frame was where the real fireworks started. Bobby Watson doubled his pleasure at the 14:29 mark, going solo and snaking one past the tender to make it 2-0. 

For a brief, shining moment, the Tornadoes looked like they might stir up a storm. Christopher Cieslak lit the lamp at 1:57, finishing off a slick tic-tac-toe sequence from Colton LeVasseur and Zachary Stidham. The crowd surged, the horn blared, and for sixty seconds, it felt like a game. 

That feeling died exactly one minute later. Brett Allen answered back at 2:57, reclaiming the two-goal cushion and effectively taking the wind out of the Tornadoes' sails. It was a back-breaker, the kind that makes a goalie hang his head and the defense look for answers in the rafters.

### Third Period: The Closing Statement
The third period was less of a hockey game and more of a clinic. Despite handing the Tornadoes a two-man advantage following penalties to Hunter Best and Phillip Caputo, the Blanks didn’t just survive—they thrived. 

Once back at full strength, the floodgates opened. Bret Kurth hammered one home at 5:49, followed by Bobby Watson—who was putting on a vintage hat-trick performance—at 7:26. To put an exclamation point on the evening, Nicholas Power added a sixth tally at 8:52, putting the game entirely out of reach.

### The Aftermath
Bobby Watson was the undisputed king of the castle tonight, ending his night with an absolute stat-sheet stuffer: three goals and three assists. You can’t ask for much more than that unless you’re the Tornadoes’ goaltender, who likely wants to burn the game tape and find a very long, very dark room.

**What does this mean for the standings?**
The Shooting Blanks remain the league’s boogeyman, moving to a pristine 4-0-0 record. They are playing a brand of hockey that is fast, physical, and frankly, lethal. For the Tornadoes, a 2-3-0 record leaves them reeling in the middle of the pack. They’ll need to find a way to plug the holes in their defense if they want to make a run at the top-tier clubs.

The Blanks are currently firing on all cylinders, and right now, the rest of the league is just trying to stay out of the crosshairs. See you at the next drop of the puck!
