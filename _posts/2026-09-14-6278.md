# Puckadelics Find Their Groove, Hand Mojo a 4-1 Reality Check

If you’re a fan of hockey played with a bit of rhythm and a whole lot of finish, the late-night tilt at the rink on September 13th was exactly what the doctor ordered. The Puckadelics came into this matchup looking to shake off the rust from their previous draw, and they did just that, cooling off Mojo by a decisive 4-1 scoreline.

The game started with the kind of urgency you rarely see at 9:45 PM on a Sunday. Before most of the fans in the stands had finished adjusting their glasses, David Guajardo put the Puckadelics on the board just 66 seconds into the first period, set up nicely by Joshua Smith. It was the shot in the arm the Puckadelics needed, and they doubled down at the 8:40 mark when John White buried a feed from Kirk Nielsen. 

Mojo, who came into the contest sitting higher in the standings, looked a step behind during those opening minutes. Their frustration boiled over just seconds after White’s goal when Tom Mortimer headed to the sin bin for tripping. While Mojo managed to kill off the penalty, they spent most of the period chasing shadows rather than playing the puck.

The second period was a clinic in patience from the Puckadelics. Despite a tripping minor assessed to David Lawrence, the Mojo offense couldn't find a way to crack the code. Instead, it was the Puckadelics who continued to press their advantage. Triston Wilson added a goal at the 4:34 mark of the period, assisted by Dylan Calhoun, and James DePriest capped off a productive frame with a goal of his own at 14:37, courtesy of another helper from Wilson. 

By the time the third period rolled around, the Puckadelics had settled into a defensive shell that would make a turtle envious. Mojo finally broke the goose egg at 10:18 thanks to an unassisted effort from Austin Lowry. It was a gritty, hardworking goal, the kind that shows a bit of character, but it was far too little and far too late to spark a comeback.

"We just didn't have the legs," one Mojo player grumbled in the hallway after the game, looking like he’d rather be anywhere else than in a locker room. "They came out buzzing, we came out napping. You can't spot a team three goals and expect a trophy."

For the Puckadelics, the win is a massive confidence booster after a lackluster start to the season. They looked crisp, moved the puck with intent, and—crucially—didn't let Mojo build any momentum. 

For Mojo, this is a wake-up call. They’ll need to figure out their transition game before their next outing if they want to maintain their spot near the top of the standings. As for the Puckadelics? If they play like this next week, they’re going to be a headache for every team in the league. Sometimes, the late game brings out the best in a group, and tonight, the Puckadelics were simply the better bunch.
