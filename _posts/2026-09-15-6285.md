# Blue Bombers Run Up the Score in 11-4 Rout of Greasers

If you were looking for a defensive masterclass on Monday night, you clearly wandered into the wrong rink. The Blue Bombers took the ice at the local barn and turned what started as a competitive affair into an absolute demolition, skating away with a convincing 11-4 victory over the Tulsa Greasers.

It didn't look like a blowout early on. The Greasers actually dictated the tempo for much of the first period. Robert McAnally drew first blood at the 14:39 mark, and when Mitchell Buckmaster extended the lead to 2-0 late in the frame, the Bombers looked like they were still nursing their post-game coffee from last week.

Then, something snapped. 

Maybe the Bombers remembered they were supposed to be playing hockey, or maybe the Greasers just decided that defense was merely a suggestion. In a frantic closing sequence to the first period, the Bombers flipped the script entirely. Jacob Meyer ignited the fuse, followed quickly by Robert Millea, Dylan Walker, and a second from Meyer. In the span of about four minutes, a two-goal deficit transformed into a 4-2 lead. It was a dizzying display that left the Greasers’ bench looking like they’d just been through a spin cycle.

"I’m not sure what happened there," said one weary-looking Greasers fan behind the glass. "It was like watching a guy try to hold back a flood with a cocktail napkin."

The second period belonged entirely to Dylan Walker, who seemed to be playing a different sport than everyone else. Walker was an absolute menace, finding the back of the net three times throughout the contest, including a relentless stretch in the middle frame. By the time the dust settled on the second period, the scoreboard read 9-3 in favor of the Bombers. 

The Greasers did manage to scrape together a few goals—Brent Beck and Colin Roohan added their names to the scoresheet—but they were little more than footnotes in a blowout. The Bombers’ offense, anchored by the playmaking vision of Walker and the steady finishing of Jacob Meyer and Christopher Olsen, was simply too much to handle. 

The third period was essentially a formality, with Meyer and Olsen adding late markers to finalize the 11-4 scoreline. It was the kind of game that makes a goaltender consider early retirement and a coach start eyeing the nearest exit. 

For the Blue Bombers, the win is a massive statement of intent after an opening-week draw. They move to 1-0-1, finally finding their scoring touch in a big way. The Greasers, meanwhile, fall to 0-1-1 and will certainly be spending their next practice session working on the concept of "marking your man"—a defensive fundamental that appeared to be entirely optional on this September night.

It’s only the second week of the regular season, and while nobody is hoisting a trophy tonight, the Blue Bombers certainly made it clear that if you aren't ready to play for sixty minutes, they are more than happy to make it a very long night for you.
