# Paleolithic Power: Fossil Hockey Dethrones the Reapers in 5-2 Victory

It turns out that fossils have plenty of life left in them. On a crisp September night at the rink, Fossil Hockey stormed onto the ice and effectively turned the clock back, securing their first win of the season with a commanding 5-2 victory over the Reapers. While the Reapers were looking to climb the standings, they instead found themselves buried under an avalanche of Fossil momentum that didn't stop until the final buzzer.

The early stages of the game were a lesson in discipline—or rather, the lack thereof. Both squads spent the first period wrestling with the sheer physics of line changes, resulting in matching "Too Many Men" penalties for both sides. It was a chaotic dance of too many skates on the ice, suggesting that perhaps the teams were trying to play with a "more the merrier" philosophy that the referees, quite rudely, didn't share.

Once the dust settled, the Fossil Hockey engine roared to life in the second period. Michael Hoey struck first at 2:13, assisted by Enoch Cox, setting the tone for a team that suddenly looked like they had been caffeinated by a prehistoric thunderstorm. Frederic Bassett followed suit at 4:46, and just when the Reapers thought they might catch a breath, Alan Dyke went rogue at 7:33, weaving through traffic for an unassisted beauty that rattled the Reapers' confidence.

"I think we just forgot how to move," one Reapers bench observer whispered, clearly stunned by the Fossils' sudden offensive explosion. By the time Eron Richards buried a feed from Daron Ross at 12:30 in the third, it looked like the Reapers were headed for a shutout disaster.

However, the Reapers weren’t ready to head to the morgue just yet. A spark of life ignited at 4:52 when Lucas Jameson finally found the back of the net, thanks to some slick puck movement from Dickie Williams and Kelly Rundle. Just 35 seconds later, Matt Burkhart capitalized on a Jose Herrick setup, bringing the score to 4-2 and momentarily making the Fossil bench sweatier than a woolly mammoth in July.

Any hope of a miraculous Reapers comeback was summarily extinguished at 8:36, however. Frederic Bassett, clearly feeling the magic of the evening, struck for his second goal of the night, sealing the deal with assists from Eron Richards and Calder Gann. 

For Fossil Hockey, this victory is a massive injection of adrenaline, moving them from the bottom of the pack and proving that their early-season struggles were merely a warm-up. The Reapers, meanwhile, fall to a 1-2-1 record and will have to head back to the drawing board to figure out how to stop the extinction of their own momentum. As the ice clears, one thing is certain: in this league, the only thing more dangerous than a Reaper is a Fossil that remembers how to score.
