# Riders Stampede Past Ice Breakers in 7-2 Offensive Clinic

The Rohan Riders came into Friday night’s tilt looking like a team possessed, dismantling the third-place Ice Breakers in a 7-2 rout that has the rest of the league checking their rearview mirrors. While the Ice Breakers have enjoyed a respectable season, they had no answer for a Riders squad that turned the rink into their personal playground for three periods of high-octane hockey.

The fireworks started early and never really let up. Samuel Northup set the tone just over two minutes into the opening frame, burying an unassisted beauty that seemed to catch the Ice Breakers flat-footed. Though Dylan Walker managed to briefly stem the tide for the visitors, the Riders responded with a ferocious barrage. Gage Walker, Greg Barnes, and Joshua Hendrickson all found the back of the net in quick succession, turning a tight game into a runaway train before the first intermission even hit.

Hendrickson was the undisputed conductor of the Riders' offensive symphony, picking up a pair of goals in the first period alone—the second of which came during a power play sparked by a Ryan Major tripping penalty. "We just decided that the puck looked better in their net than anywhere else," Hendrickson joked after the game, still dripping with sweat and sporting a grin wide enough to fit a hockey puck through. "Abigail [Nisbet] was feeding me absolute apples all night. I just had to make sure I didn't trip over my own skates."

The Ice Breakers, clearly frustrated by the lack of defensive structure, found themselves chasing shadows for the duration of the second period. A hooking penalty by Jason Rosengard provided the breathing room for Samuel Northup to bag his second of the night, assisted by Nathan Lesnick, effectively putting the game on ice before the final frame even began.

The third period served as the victory lap for the Riders. Greg Barnes notched his second goal of the contest halfway through the period, once again finding chemistry with the ever-reliable Hendrickson and Alexander Rowin. 

To their credit, the Ice Breakers didn't go down without a final act of defiance. Joshua Pula, riding a stellar four-game goal streak coming into the night, managed to beat the Riders' goaltender just as the final buzzer loomed at 15:00. While the goal was too little, too late to salvage the result, it kept Pula’s impressive scoring streak alive—a small consolation prize in an otherwise forgettable evening for the Breakers.

For the Riders, this win is a massive statement. Sitting at #6, they’ve struggled to find consistency this season, but a dominant win over a top-three opponent like the Ice Breakers suggests they are finally finding their rhythm. The Ice Breakers, meanwhile, will look to regroup after a performance that saw their defensive shell cracked wide open. If tonight was any indication, the middle of the pack is about to get a lot more interesting.
