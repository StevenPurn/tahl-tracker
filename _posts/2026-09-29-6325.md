# Mortimer’s Multitool Effort Powers Skate Goats Past the Lodge

If you look at the spreadsheet, you’ll see that hockey is a game of probability. But if you were at the rink for Monday night’s tilt between the Skate Goats and the Lodge, you saw that sometimes, math just gets out of the way to make room for a good old-fashioned shootout. The Skate Goats managed to edge out the Lodge 5-4, securing a much-needed two points in a crowded middle-of-the-pack logjam.

The story of the night was undeniably the Tom Mortimer show. Entering the game on a two-game point streak, Mortimer decided he wasn't interested in just padding his stats; he wanted to dominate the box score. His two goals—one in the first period to equalize Christopher Brooks’ unassisted opener, and a second in the middle frame—provided the backbone for the Goats' offense. 

"I don't know what to tell you," Mortimer said after the game, while adjusting his shin guards with the focus of a bomb technician. "The puck just seemed to have an affinity for my stick tonight. It was like I was playing a game of catch, but the puck kept ending up in the net."

The game was a chaotic affair, characterized by back-and-forth momentum shifts that would give a defensive coach an ulcer. After a scoreless power play for the Skate Goats following an early hooking penalty by Alex Ruiz, the Lodge struck first via Brooks. The Goats responded quickly, and by the end of the second period, it felt like the scoreboard was struggling to keep up with the pace. 

A critical turning point occurred midway through the second. After Mortimer potted his second of the night at 10:14, Jamie Halverson fired back for the Lodge just 29 seconds later. It was a classic "momentum swing" scenario that usually deflates a bench. Instead, the Goats stayed vertical, with Dustin Giebel finding the twine at 12:05 to regain the lead. 

The Lodge showed impressive grit, particularly on their power play. Despite a series of penalties that threatened to derail their night—including two separate infractions by Jess Herd—the Lodge managed to keep things within reach. Christopher Brooks’ second goal of the night at 13:50 of the second period brought them within striking distance, keeping the Goats' goaltender under constant, high-stress pressure.

The final frame saw the Skate Goats rely on Joshua Gonya to provide the ultimate insurance policy. His goal at 9:42 proved to be the difference-maker, neutralizing Jered Pieschke’s early third-period effort for the Lodge. 

"We’re looking for consistency," noted a Skate Goats staffer, clutching a clipboard like it contained the secrets of the universe. "When you take that many penalties, you’re essentially asking the hockey gods to throw a wrench in your gears. Tonight, the gears held."

With the win, the Skate Goats (2-1-1) jump to 5 points, effectively climbing out of the cluster at the bottom and pulling even with the league leaders. The Lodge (1-2-1) will walk away from this one wondering what might have been had they capitalized on their transition opportunities just a fraction more efficiently. 

For the statisticians in the crowd, it was a night of high-octane efficiency. For everyone else, it was just a reminder that in this league, the only stat that really matters is the one in the final column.
