# A Jurassic Juggernaut: Fossil Hockey Rips Through the Wendigos 7-3

**Byline: The Rinkside Reporter**
**Date: June 7, 2026**

If you didn’t believe in the staying power of Fossil Hockey, you’d better start looking at the history books—or better yet, the scoreboard. In a display of offensive firepower that would make a paleontologist blush, Fossil Hockey dismantled the Wendigos 7-3 on a balmy Saturday night, proving that this squad is anything but ancient history.

### Period 1: Shaking Off the Rust
The game opened with both teams feeling each other out, looking for a clean breakout. Fossil Hockey found themselves in a bit of a bind early when Ethan Peterson caught a whistle for hooking at the 2:42 mark. The Fossil penalty kill unit stood tall, turning the Wendigos away and keeping the sheet clean. 

Just when it seemed we were headed to the first intermission at a stalemate, Ethan Hilton—a man currently riding a heater hotter than the sun—found the twine with a mere 38.1 seconds left in the frame. Assisted by Bryan Simms, Hilton’s goal extended his impressive goal streak to three games and served as a wake-up call for the visitors.

### Period 2: The Fossil Firing Squad
If the first period was a light appetizer, the second was an all-you-can-eat buffet. Fossil Hockey came out of the locker room with the bit between their teeth. Robert Norris turned into a one-man wrecking crew, lighting the lamp twice—first off a dish from Michael Hilton, and again shortly after with help from Jose Medina and Bryan Simms.

The Wendigos, struggling to keep their skates under them, saw Mark Smith head to the box for tripping at 5:01. Fossil made them pay. Jose Medina joined the party at the 8:33 mark, followed quickly by Ethan Peterson—redeeming his earlier penalty—who wired one past the tender off a Michael Hoey setup. By the time the buzzer sounded for the second break, Fossil had built a lead that looked more like a mountain than a scoreline.

### Period 3: A Late Frostbite Attempt
Leading 6-0 heading into the final frame, Fossil Hockey’s Dave Griffiths kept the pressure on, adding his own tally with just over nine seconds gone in the third. It was a statement goal, but to their credit, the Wendigos didn’t go quietly into the night.

Michael McLaughlin finally broke the goose egg for the Wendigos at 3:07, and following a slashing minor to Jerry LaRue and a tripping call on Mark Smith, the Wendigos capitalized on the open ice. Zac Hughes and Aden Ingram rattled off back-to-back goals in the final two minutes to make things interesting. However, any hopes of a miracle comeback were swiftly extinguished at 9:42, when Jose Medina put his second of the night into the top shelf, right where the goalie keeps his pride. 

### The Bottom Line
With a 7-3 victory, Fossil Hockey (now 2-0-0) continues their climb up the standings. They are looking like a bonafide contender, playing with the kind of chemistry that usually takes a whole season to cultivate. Bryan Simms was the architect tonight, acting as a one-man playmaking factory, while the offense proved they can strike from every line on the roster.

For the Wendigos, tonight is one to put in the rearview mirror. Sitting at 0-2-0, they’ll need to find some defensive cohesion if they want to stop the bleeding. But for tonight, the rink belonged to the Fossils, and they left the ice looking like a team that’s ready to leave a permanent mark on this league.

**Final Score:** Fossil Hockey 7, Wendigos 3.
