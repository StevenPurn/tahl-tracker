# Reapers Harvest Two Points in Decisive Win Over Syndicate

If you caught the 9:30 p.m. puck drop on Tuesday night, you might have expected a back-and-forth grind between a Syndicate team looking to find their footing and a Reapers squad trying to climb the standings. What you actually got was a clinical demonstration of why you don't leave the slot unattended, as the Reapers cruised to a 5-1 victory.

The Reapers came out with the kind of focus that’s rare for a late-week nightcap. By the middle of the first period, they’d already dictated the tempo, drawing first blood at the 5:56 mark when Dennis Jackson found the back of the net unassisted. Dickie Williams followed that up with a helper from Paul Sanders just a few minutes later, leaving the Syndicate scrambling to find their defensive structure.

For the Syndicate, the first period was mostly spent chasing shadows and dodging whistles, highlighted by a Logan Pardue trip that put them on their heels early. Pardue, to his credit, was the only one to find a glimmer of hope for his side. Midway through the second, he finally solved the Reapers' goaltending, burying a feed from Ethan Hilton at the 13:03 mark to cut the deficit to 2-1.

It was the only spark they’d get. Just as the Syndicate looked like they might claw their way back into the contest, Patrick Flannery killed the momentum with a cold-blooded goal at 7:44 of the second. A frustration penalty from Travis Moore shortly after served as the final nail in the coffin, effectively ending any hopes of a comeback before the third period even began.

The final frame belonged entirely to Lucas Jameson, who treated the net like a target in a practice drill. Jameson notched his first goal at 13:21 and followed it up with a second at 8:40, putting the game well out of reach. 

"Sometimes the puck just finds you, and sometimes it just finds the net," Jameson joked after the match, probably grateful his teammates didn't ask him to share the credit for the puck luck.

For the Reapers, the win is a necessary correction to their recent uneven form, moving them up to 4-3-1 and proving they have the teeth to compete with the middle-of-the-pack teams. The Syndicate, meanwhile, drops to 2-3-1, heading back to the locker room with plenty of tape to review and a reminder that in this league, if you give up five goals, you’re rarely going to find yourself in the winner's circle. 

It wasn't pretty, and it wasn't particularly close, but for the Reapers, it was exactly what the doctor ordered on a Tuesday night.
