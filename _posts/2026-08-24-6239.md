# Valkyries Edge Bad Bees in Hard-Fought Championship Thriller

If you subscribe to the theory that hockey championships are won by the team that controls the transition game, Tuesday night at the rink provided a masterclass in efficiency. The top-seeded Valkyries cemented their dominant season by dispatching the underdog Bad Bees 3-2 to claim the league title, proving that while Cinderella stories are fun for the history books, elite consistency remains the gold standard.

The Valkyries entered the contest riding a five-game win streak, and they wasted no time imposing their will. Gabrielle Gibson, who entered the night riding a four-game goal-scoring streak, continued her personal heater just 2:35 into the contest. Fed by Barbara Lepe Briones, Gibson’s opening tally set the tone, quickly followed by an Emily Adamic strike at the 18:26 mark—thanks to a sharp setup from Carson See—to put the Valkyries up 2-0.

However, anyone who has watched the Bad Bees knows they are allergic to moral victories. With 1:04 remaining in the first, the Bees’ leading scorer, known only as "Unknown," notched an unassisted marker. It was a classic "stat-sheet disturber"—a reminder that when a player is on an eight-game goal-scoring streak, they don't necessarily need a play drawn up to find the back of the net. 

The middle frame turned into a tactical chess match, punctuated only by a Natalie Myers goal at the 7:44 mark. Assisted by Gibson and See, the goal was a highlight-reel example of puck movement. By putting the Valkyries up 3-1, Myers effectively forced the Bees to abandon their defensive structure and chase the game. 

"We told the girls at the break: if you give them time and space, they’ll turn a boring Tuesday night into a stressful science experiment," said one Valkyries bench staffer, clearly unimpressed by the frantic pace of the third period.

The Bees didn't make it easy. The mysterious "Unknown" struck again with 4:13 remaining in the third, once again going unassisted to pull the Bees within one. The final minutes turned into a defensive scramble for the Valkyries, especially after Makenzie Dillard was whistled for a slashing penalty with only 43.4 seconds left on the clock. 

The Bad Bees threw everything they had at the net, but the Valkyries’ defensive shell held firm until the final buzzer. The victory caps off a stellar campaign for the Valkyries, who finished the regular season at 9-1-0 and validated their top seeding with a disciplined championship performance. 

For the Bad Bees, the loss is a bitter pill, but there is no shame in falling to a squad that played with the mechanical precision of a spreadsheet all season. As for the Valkyries, they proved that when you combine a four-game goal streak with an relentless offensive transition, you don't just win games—you collect hardware.
