# Mooseknuckles Grind Out Gritty Shutout Win Over Skate Goats

If there’s one thing they don’t teach you in those fancy modern skating clinics, it’s how to win a game when the whistles are blowing more often than a steam engine in a coal mine. Monday night at the rink, the Mooseknuckles and the Skate Goats treated us to a classic display of "recreational" hockey: plenty of sweat, a heap of penalties, and enough frustration to keep the penalty box attendant gainfully employed for the duration of the 9:30 p.m. start. 

When the dust settled, the Mooseknuckles walked away with a 2-0 victory, proving that sometimes, staying out of the box is better than being the flashiest skater on the ice.

The first period was less of a hockey game and more of a scenic tour of the penalty box, particularly for the Skate Goats. Josh Mosley seemed determined to test the structural integrity of the referee’s arm, picking up a hooking minor in the final seconds of the opening frame, following earlier trips to the box by Joshua Gonya and a repeat trip for Mosley himself. The Goats spent a significant portion of the period trying to find their rhythm while perpetually down a man, a strategy that—much like my attempts at a balanced diet—didn't end well.

The deadlock finally shattered early in the second. With the Goats struggling to find their defensive structure, Pete Fleming found the back of the net off a clean feed from Garrett Williams. It was the kind of goal that makes an old-timer smile; simple, effective, and executed without the unnecessary flair that seems to infect the game these days. 

The Mooseknuckles weren’t entirely angelic, mind you. James Rozell spent some time cooling his heels for roughing, but the Goats simply couldn't capitalize on the man-advantage. The middle frame became a slog of whistles and frustrated glares, including a late unsportsmanlike call on Mosley that effectively deflated any remaining tire the Goats had left on their wagon.

The third period was a defensive stalemate until Mark Hodges took matters into his own hands with about four minutes remaining. In a beautiful display of individual effort, Hodges managed to bury an unassisted goal, effectively slamming the door on any hopes of a Goats comeback. Even a late holding penalty on Rozell couldn't crack the Mooseknuckles' defensive shell, as they held fast to secure the shutout victory.

For the Mooseknuckles, the win is a tidy bit of business that keeps them firmly in the hunt at third place. For the Skate Goats, who came in looking to climb the standings, tonight was a lesson in discipline. They weren't necessarily out-skated, but they were certainly out-managed. 

As the ice crew pulled the nets and the late-night crowd shuffled toward the exits, it was a reminder of why we watch this stuff: it’s never as pretty as you want it to be, and it’s rarely as smooth as you expect, but a win is a win. And in this league, you take the two points however you can get them.
