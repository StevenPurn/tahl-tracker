# A Stalemate in the Trenches: Jackalopes and Mojo Grind to a Draw

If you like high-octane, highlight-reel hockey, Tuesday night’s tilt between the Jackalopes and Mojo wasn’t for you. If, however, you enjoy the smell of damp equipment, the sound of the boards taking a beating, and a game that refused to be decided by a single bounce, then this 1-1 draw was just what the doctor ordered.

Entering the night tied in the standings with identical 2-2-1 records, both teams played like they were terrified of giving the other an inch. It was a classic "four-point game" that ended with everyone walking away with one, leaving the middle of the pack just as crowded as it was when the puck dropped at 8:00 PM.

The Jackalopes drew first blood late in the first period. At the 2:58 mark, Bradley Fidler found the back of the net, cleaning up a tidy setup from Ronald Herwig and Kyle Foster. For a moment, it looked like the Jackalopes might run away with the momentum, especially considering they’d successfully killed off a Toby Temple hooking penalty earlier in the frame.

But Mojo isn't the type of squad to fold up their tents, even when the offense is harder to find than a parking spot at the rink. They clawed their way back in the second period, with Brandon Chouinard knotting the score at 14:37. Assisted by Doug Lowry, the play was a workmanlike effort—the kind of goal that doesn't make it to the "Top 10" reels, but makes a coach sleep a little better at night.

The third period devolved into what can only be described as "recreational extracurriculars." With both teams frustrated by the lack of breathing room, the whistles began to dictate the pace. A flurry of penalties at the 11:17 mark—involving Kyle White, Thomas White, and Tyler Wick—turned the ice into a bit of a circus. When the dust settled on the roughing calls and a fighting major for Thomas White, the officials had their work cut out for them, but neither side could capitalize on the chaos.

"It was one of those nights where the puck felt like it was made of granite," said one observer near the benches, and they weren't wrong. 

Despite the late-game discipline issues—including a slashing call on Wick and a cross-checking minor for Foster—the defenses held firm. Goaltending was the unsung hero of the night, as both nets remained virtually impenetrable once the score was tied. 

For the Jackalopes and Mojo, the point doesn't do much to shift them up the standings, but it keeps them within striking distance of the leaders. The Puckedelics and the Bone Zone are still setting the pace at the top, but nights like this remind us that in a league this tight, every shift—and every single point—eventually carries the weight of a championship. 

Both teams head back to the locker rooms with a draw, a few sore shoulders, and the realization that they’ll have to find a way to finish more effectively next time out. For now, the Jackalopes and Mojo remain deadlocked, both in the standings and on the scoreboard.
