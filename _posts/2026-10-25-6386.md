# Valkyries Sting the Bees in High-Octane 6-4 Affair

In a clash between two of the league’s top contenders, The Valkyries flexed their offensive muscle on Friday evening, holding off a frantic Bad Bees comeback to secure a 6-4 victory. The win keeps The Valkyries comfortably in second place, maintaining their momentum as they remain one of the few squads yet to lose in regulation this season.

The first period was a tactical affair, played at a pace that suggested both teams were feeling out the ice. Elisabeth Schroeder opened the scoring for The Valkyries at the 12:17 mark, set up by a sharp feed from Haley Griffin. The Bees responded late in the frame when a mystery skater—listed simply as "Unknown" in the scorebook—buried a pass from Suzanne Hawkins to knot the game at 1-1 heading into the first intermission.

If the first period was a chess match, the second period was a barroom brawl. The Valkyries looked to pull away early, with Schroeder netting her second of the night, followed by unassisted goals from Haley Griffin and Makenzie Giove. At 4-1, it looked as though the Bees were going to be swatted out of the rink.

But the Bees, never a team to roll over, found their groove in the final six minutes of the second period. A surge of energy turned the tide when the Bees’ "Unknown" skater potted their second of the game, followed shortly by a strike from Grace Wolff, who extended her goal-scoring streak to three games. Before the Zamboni drivers could even start checking their watches, the Bees found the net yet again, pulling within a single goal at 4-3.

"We stopped moving our feet for about five minutes and let them back into the kitchen," said a Valkyries coaching staff member, adjusting a clipboard that looked like it had been through a shredder. "You give a team like the Bees an inch, and they’ll try to take the whole ice."

The third period settled back into a defensive grind, with The Valkyries tightening up their defensive zone coverage. Jessica Hopwood padded the lead to 5-3 with a patient, unassisted effort midway through the final frame. Even a late roughing penalty assessed to Rachel Colombo couldn't spark the Bees' power play, and Makenzie Giove sealed the deal with her second goal of the night, putting the game out of reach at 6-4.

For The Valkyries, the win extends their impressive run, proving they have the depth to handle high-pressure situations. The Bees, meanwhile, will look to regroup after seeing their three-game winning streak snapped in a game that proved they can hang with anyone, even when the scoreboard isn't in their favor.

It was the kind of hockey that makes you appreciate the basics: clean passes, grit in the corners, and the occasional blast from the blue line that reminds you why we spend our Friday nights in a cold rink instead of on the couch. The Valkyries march on, and the Bees are left with a loss—but if you’re a fan of this league, you’re just happy to see both teams still swinging for the fences.
