# Puckheads Feast as Honey Badgers Remain Winless in 5-2 Rout

If you looked closely at the ice on the night of November 15, you might have sworn the Puckheads were trying to turn the rink into a personal buffet. In a commanding 5-2 victory over the winless Honey Badgers, the Puckheads didn’t just play hockey; they orchestrated a symphony of offense that left the Badgers chasing shadows for the better part of forty minutes.

The game started with the Honey Badgers looking to snap their cold streak, but discipline proved to be a bitter pill. When Cole Rush headed to the sin bin for slashing midway through the first, it seemed to wake the sleeping giants in white and black. Gregory Douglas opened the account at the 3:22 mark with a solo effort that had the Badgers’ defense looking like they were trying to solve a Rubik’s cube in the dark. Shortly after, Cameron Sartin extended the lead, burying a feed from Douglas and Steven Ramos to head into the intermission with a comfortable 2-0 cushion.

If the first period was a warm-up, the second was a full-blown Puckheads party. WILLIAM HOFMEISTER decided he hadn't seen enough of the back of the net, netting his first of the night just three minutes in, then adding another marker at 7:43—this time with help from Jake Sartin and the ever-reliable Steven Ramos. Before the Badgers could even check the scoreboard, Gregory Douglas notched his second of the game at 9:03, pushing the lead to a staggering 5-0.

"I think we were moving the puck like it was a hot potato that nobody wanted to hold," joked Puckheads bench boss after the game, clearly enjoying the offensive explosion. "When you're up by five, the hardest part is remembering to keep your skates moving and not start thinking about dinner reservations."

To their credit, the Honey Badgers refused to roll over and play dead. Despite the lopsided scoreline, they clawed back in the third period. Michael Bergstrom finally broke the shutout bid for the Badgers at 5:59, assisted by Andrew Sleezer, before Sleezer himself lit the lamp at 8:39 with help from Jared Sewell and Bergstrom. It was a valiant late-game surge, but with the Puckheads’ massive lead, it was a case of too little, too late.

The win sees the Puckheads (5-3-1) inching closer to the top tier of the standings, maintaining a steady pace that suggests they aren't going to let the leaders run away with the season. For the Honey Badgers, it’s back to the drawing board as they remain at the bottom of the table, still searching for that elusive first victory.

As for the Puckheads? They’ll head home tonight knowing that when they play with this kind of synergy, they are a nightmare for any goaltender in the league. They didn't just win; they left the rink looking like a team that has finally found its rhythm—and isn't planning on dropping the beat anytime soon.
