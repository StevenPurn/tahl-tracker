# Dirty Birds Ground the Wolfpack in 10-4 Rout

If there’s one thing they don’t teach you in those fancy hockey academies, it’s that you don’t leave your goaltender out to dry while you’re busy daydreaming about post-game pizza. The One Man Wolfpack learned that lesson the hard way on Friday night at the rink, as the Dirty Birds systematically dismantled them in a 10-4 shellacking that felt every bit as lopsided as the final score suggests.

The tone for the evening was set early—very early. Before most of the fans had finished settling into their seats, Kyle Rachal opened the scoring just 65 seconds into the first period. Gabriel Kierath added another just 34 seconds later, and by the time the dust settled on the opening frame, the Birds had hung a five-spot on the scoreboard. 

The Wolfpack seemed to be playing with the structural integrity of a wet paper bag. Alexander Hajduczok’s trip to the sin bin for unsportsmanlike conduct midway through the first only served to pour gasoline on the fire, as the Birds capitalized with a James Smith marker shortly after. By the end of the first, it was 5-0, and the only thing missing for the Wolfpack was a white flag.

To give credit where it’s due, the Wolfpack managed a pulse in the second period. Kyle Okurowski and Michael Bergstrom finally broke the seal on the Birds' net within forty seconds of each other, momentarily silencing the chirping coming from the Dirty Birds' bench. 

"We got a little complacent, sure," said one Dirty Birds player during the second intermission, wiping sweat from his brow. "But we realized if we let them back in this, we’d never hear the end of it in the parking lot."

The third period turned into a full-blown showcase for the Gabriel Kierath and Nicklas Clark show. Kierath completed a stellar night with a hat trick, while Clark buried two of his own in the final frame to effectively put the Wolfpack out of their misery. James Smith was the silent assassin of the night, racking up a mountain of assists—four in total—by simply finding the open man while the Wolfpack defenders were busy watching the puck like it was a hypnotist's pendulum.

Despite a late-game surge from James Whitacre, who managed to put two past the Birds' defense, the result was never in doubt. The Wolfpack’s struggles in the defensive zone were glaring, and the Birds were more than happy to exploit them at every turn. 

For the Dirty Birds, the win keeps the momentum rolling as they improve to 2-0-0 on the young season. They looked sharp, disciplined, and—perhaps most importantly—they didn't rely on luck to do their heavy lifting. As for the One Man Wolfpack, they’ll have to go back to the drawing board to figure out how to stop the bleeding before they find themselves cemented at the bottom of the standings. 

If tonight’s performance is any indication, the Birds aren’t just looking to play hockey; they’re looking to dominate the ice. And frankly, for those of us watching from the sidelines, it’s about time someone did.
