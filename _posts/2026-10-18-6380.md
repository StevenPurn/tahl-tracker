# Greasers Grind Out First Win in Thriller Against Riders

The Tulsa Greasers finally kicked the monkey off their back—and maybe a few ghosts along with it—securing their first win of the season in a heart-pounding 5-4 victory over the Rohan Riders this Saturday morning. It was a classic "don't look at the standings" affair that had everything: early dominance, a furious comeback, and a nail-biting finish that kept the early-bird crowd on the edge of their bleachers.

The Greasers came out of the gates like they’d just discovered the fountain of youth. Dylan Walker drew first blood at the 12:18 mark, setting the tone for a period where Tulsa simply refused to let the puck settle. By the time the dust cleared on the first, Mitchell Buckmaster and Colin Roohan had added two more to give the Greasers a commanding 3-0 lead. It looked like the Riders were going to be run right out of the building, but as any local hockey fan knows, a three-goal lead is the most dangerous lead in the game.

The second period turned into an absolute barnburner. The Riders, clearly waking up from a collective slumber, found their legs and started putting the pressure on. An unassisted tally from a player who must be too humble to sign the scoresheet—listed simply as "Not Signed In"—broke the seal for Rohan. 

Buckmaster notched his second of the game early in the frame to briefly restore the three-goal cushion, but the Riders weren’t going down without a fight. Alexander Rowin ignited the comeback train with a beautiful helper from Glenn Prouse, and suddenly the energy in the rink shifted. Two more quick strikes—another mystery goal from the "Not Signed In" squad and a clutch finish from Frank Brandon Smith—pulled the Riders even at 4-4 by the end of the second.

The third period was a defensive stalemate, a gritty, high-stakes game of "who blinks first." Both teams traded penalties and frustrated sighs as the clock ticked down. Just when it felt like we were headed for a stalemate, Robert McAnally stepped up to play the hero. With John White feeding him the biscuit, McAnally buried the eventual game-winner at the 8:55 mark.

"We finally stopped playing like we were wearing concrete skates," joked a Greasers team staffer after the buzzer. "It wasn't pretty, it wasn't quiet, but it’s a W, and we'll take it to the bank."

The win is a massive morale boost for the Greasers (1-4-1), who have been looking for a spark all season. For the Rohan Riders (0-3-2), it’s a tough pill to swallow after such a spirited second-period rally. Both teams look to build on the intensity of this matchup as they continue their respective campaigns in a league that, clearly, is still wide open for anyone willing to put in the work.
