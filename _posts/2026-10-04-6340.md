# Yetis Avalanche Overpowers Skate Goats in 7-2 Rout

If there is one thing I’ve learned from years of watching the local circuit, it’s that momentum is a fickle beast. Yet, on Saturday afternoon, the Yetis didn’t just grab the momentum; they put it in a headlock and refused to let go. In a decisive 7-2 victory over the Skate Goats, the Yetis demonstrated that their 1-2-1 start was perhaps a statistical anomaly rather than a reflection of their true offensive ceiling.

The game began with a flurry of activity that left the scorekeepers scrambling. Jason Bahr set the tone early, opening the scoring at the 12:49 mark of the first period with an unassisted effort. The Skate Goats, looking to claw back into the contest, found an equalizer through Robert Hoffmann, who capitalized on a dish from the ever-productive Dustin Giebel. Giebel, carrying a two-game point streak into the matchup, continued his consistency, but the Skate Goats simply couldn't find an answer for the sustained pressure of the Yetis' forecheck.

The turning point of the afternoon wasn't a tactical maneuver, but rather a discipline breakdown. Travis Miller’s 10-minute unsportsmanlike conduct penalty in the first period served as a significant anchor on the Skate Goats' rotation. As the penalty box became a revolving door, the Yetis began to tighten their grip on the game’s geometry. Kirk Nielsen added his name to the scoresheet shortly after, and the Yetis entered the first intermission with a lead they would never relinquish.

The second period saw the emergence of Alexander Hajduczok as the game’s primary offensive catalyst. Hajduczok found the back of the net twice in the middle frame, effectively neutralizing the Skate Goats' attempts to bridge the gap after Andrew Vandiver—set up again by Giebel—narrowed the lead to 3-2. By the time Brendan Burkman extended the Yetis' lead to 5-2 at the 8:03 mark, the Skate Goats appeared to be running on fumes.

"We were chasing the puck all afternoon," said one anonymous Skate Goats defenseman while untying his skates with a weary grimace. "Every time we looked up, there was a Yeti. It felt like we were playing against six skaters and a calculator."

The third period served as a clinical exercise for the Yetis. Jason Bahr tallied his second of the night, followed by a late marker from Kirk Nielsen to seal the 7-2 final. The Yetis' ability to generate offense without needing heavy assist volume—notably Bahr and Nielsen both scoring unassisted goals—speaks to a high level of individual skill that finally clicked in unison.

For the Skate Goats, this result is a bitter pill, dropping them from their fifth-place standing into a pack of teams searching for answers. Meanwhile, the Yetis move to a 2-2-1 record, signaling that they are finally ready to shed their slow start. If they can replicate this level of efficiency on the transition, the top half of the league standings might look very different by the time we hit the mid-season point. For now, the stats favor the Yetis, who proved that even when the ice tilts, you still need to be the one to put the puck in the net.
