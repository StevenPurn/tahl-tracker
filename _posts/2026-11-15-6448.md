# Power Puck Girls Upset Riveters in Early Morning Shootout Thriller

The Power Puck Girls arrived at the rink at an hour when most sensible people are still deciding whether to hit the snooze button, but they were wide awake when it counted. In a 4-3 shootout victory over the Tulsa Riveters this Saturday morning, the league’s bottom-dwellers finally found a reason to smile, snapping a four-game skid against one of the toughest squads in the standings.

It didn’t take long for the Power Puck Girls to signal their intentions. Just 1:27 into the opening frame, Addison Gaut found the back of the net off a feed from Maggie Marcum. It was the kind of quick start that usually makes a coach breathe a little easier, though as any veteran of this league knows, breathing easy is a luxury that rarely lasts sixty minutes.

The momentum continued into the second period when an unnamed goal-scorer doubled the lead, assisted by April Belanger. But the Riveters, who entered the match riding a wave of four wins in their last five, weren’t about to roll over. Ivy Emerson kept her goal-scoring streak alive at 4:45 of the second, clawing one back for Tulsa to keep the pressure firmly on.

The third period turned into a proper dogfight. While Holly Hutto and Stacey Burgess both sat for tripping—a reminder that skates are meant for gliding, not gymnastics—the Riveters found their rhythm. Hannah Atkins, currently the hottest stick on the Riveters' bench, took matters into her own hands. She clawed back a goal at 14:45 and then leveled the score at 6:06 with her second of the night, assisted by Elizabeth Johnson. It looked like the Riveters were poised to complete the comeback, but hockey has a funny way of punishing the side that assumes the game is already won.

Macy Alford silenced the Riveters' surge at 8:32, netting an unassisted goal to reclaim the lead for the Power Puck Girls. While Tulsa managed to push the game into the shootout, Liliena Storer played the hero, burying her chance to secure the two points.

"We finally stopped watching the puck and started chasing it," noted one rinkside observer, a sentiment that seemed to ring true for the Power Puck bench. 

For the Tulsa Riveters, who remain at 4-1-2, it’s a frustrating stumble, but they’ve played enough smart hockey lately to know one bad morning won’t sink the season. As for the Power Puck Girls, moving to 2-6-1, the win is a massive morale boost. They’ve spent the better part of the season learning the hard way, but on Saturday, they learned that even the league’s basement can host an upset. Sometimes, all you need is a little cold ice and a willingness to finish the play.
