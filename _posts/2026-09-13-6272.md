# Tornadoes and Precast Deadlock in Back-and-Forth Thriller

If you showed up to the rink at 8:15 on a Sunday morning looking for a snooze-fest, you clearly picked the wrong game. The Tornadoes and Oklahoma Precast battled to a 3-3 draw this morning, a result that felt less like a stalemate and more like a tactical arm-wrestling match that neither side wanted to concede.

The Tornadoes looked like they were intent on blowing the doors off the place early, thanks largely to the heavy lifting of Christian Alvegran. Alvegran opened the scoring at the 12:57 mark and doubled the damage just over four minutes later, assisted by the duo of Tyler Schulz and Tobias Alvegran. For a brief moment, it looked like the Tornadoes might coast, but Nicholas Power had other ideas, answering for Oklahoma Precast before the first intermission to keep things interesting.

The second period saw the Precast tighten up their defensive structure, forcing the Tornadoes to earn every inch of ice. Emil Hinkefent brought the game back to even at 5:39, showing a bit of grit in the crease to put it home. However, the Tornadoes managed to reclaim the lead shortly after when Zachary Stidham found the twine off a clean feed from Nicklas Clark and Alvegran. 

"It was one of those games where you’re skating hard, but the puck just keeps finding a way back to the middle," said one unidentified veteran defender after the buzzer. "It’s honest work, but my lungs aren't as young as they used to be."

The back-and-forth nature of the tilt continued into the final frame. Nicholas Power, who was clearly the most dangerous man on the ice for Oklahoma Precast, netted his second of the morning with just over 11 minutes remaining to force the 3-3 tie. 

The waning minutes provided plenty of anxiety for the folks in the stands. With the game tied, a bit of desperation crept into the play, resulting in an Emil Hinkefent trip at 11:51. The Tornadoes had a prime opportunity to ice the win on the power play, but the Precast penalty kill stood tall, clearing the zone and protecting the draw until the final whistle.

The Tornadoes move to 0-0-2 on the season, continuing their trend of tight contests. Oklahoma Precast, meanwhile, leaves the rink with a hard-earned point and the satisfaction of knowing they can hold their own against the league’s top-seated squad. It wasn't always pretty, and there were a few blown assignments that would make a coach pull their hair out, but for an early Sunday morning, it was exactly the kind of hockey that keeps us coming back to the rink.
