# Hot Garbage Puts the Hammer Down on Lite Beer Eagles

If you were looking for a high-octane offensive clinic this Saturday evening at the rink, you didn’t have to look much further than the matchup between Hot Garbage and the Lite Beer Eagles. By the time the final buzzer echoed through the rafters, Hot Garbage had lived up to their name—if the name implies an absolute dumpster fire for the opposition—skating away with a dominant 7-2 victory that left the Eagles scrambling for answers.

From the opening puck drop, it was clear that Hot Garbage came to work. Aaron Whisner continued his hot streak, finding the back of the net just four and a half minutes into the first period off a crisp dish from Brett Mecham. Not long after, Nicholas Brewer doubled the lead, putting the Eagles in a hole they simply couldn't dig their way out of. 

"We just couldn't find our footing," one frustrated Eagles bench hand muttered while unlacing his skates. "Every time we thought we had a lane, someone in a Hot Garbage sweater was there to shut the door."

While the Eagles managed to put a pair of dents in the scoreboard—Adam Strange finding the twine in the second and Michael Hilton doing the same in the third—it was mostly a one-way street. The second period was particularly illustrative of the night, as Hot Garbage effectively countered any momentum the Eagles tried to build. Just after Strange sparked a glimmer of hope with his unassisted goal, Brett Mecham snatched it right back with a goal of his own just under a minute later. 

Mecham was undoubtedly the star of the show tonight. Between his pair of goals and his playmaking, he seemed to be everywhere at once. Joshua Hall was another thorn in the Eagles' side, putting on a passing display that kept the stat sheet humming. Joe Baker and John Bewley also got in on the action, padding the lead in the final frame and turning the third period into something of a victory lap for the squad in the Hot Garbage colors.

For the Lite Beer Eagles, it’s a tough pill to swallow. After entering the night looking to find some consistency in their form, they ran into a buzzsaw that was firing on all cylinders. Conversely, Hot Garbage looks to be finding their rhythm in a crowded middle-of-the-pack standings race. 

As the two teams headed for the parking lot, the contrast was stark: one team looking for a quick exit, and the other wondering how they can bottle up this offensive explosion for their next tilt. If tonight was any indication, Hot Garbage isn't just looking to compete this season—they're looking to make the rest of the league sweat every time they lace them up.
