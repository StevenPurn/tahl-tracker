# Yetis Storm Back to Deny Jackalopes in 5-3 Thriller

If you were looking for a masterclass in "never say die" hockey, the ice at the local barn was the place to be on Tuesday night. In a regular-season clash that started with the Jackalopes looking like they might run away with the show, the Yetis staged a gritty, late-game surge to secure a 5-3 victory and climb up the standings.

The Jackalopes came out of the gate with enough momentum to power a Zamboni for a week. Kyle Foster drew first blood just under two minutes into the opening frame, and when Robert Verity doubled the lead midway through the second period off a slick feed from James Whitacre, the Yetis looked colder than their namesake.

But then, the momentum shifted like a sudden change in wind direction. Brendan Burkman sparked the Yetis' heartbeat late in the second period, burying a feed from Christopher Copp and Kirk Nielsen to cut the lead to 2-1. Though Christopher King fired back for the Jackalopes just under three minutes later to restore the two-goal cushion, the Yetis were already finding their groove. A solo effort by Kirk Nielsen at the 6:08 mark of the second served as a warning shot: this game wasn’t over.

"We knew we were playing a bit of 'catch-me-if-you-can' hockey early on," said a grinning Yetis player in the locker room post-game. "Once the first one rattled the back of the net, the boys realized we had plenty of fuel left in the tank."

The third period was pure, unadulterated chaos—the kind that makes local hockey fans reach for their second cup of concession-stand coffee. The Yetis turned up the heat, and the Jackalopes simply couldn't find the fire extinguisher. Brendan Burkman struck again at 11:31, thanks to some stellar vision from Nielsen, knotting the game at 3-3.

With the crowd buzzing and the atmosphere thick enough to slice with a skate blade, Alexander Hajduczok decided it was his time to shine. In a blistering span of less than a minute, Hajduczok played hero, lighting the lamp twice—once with help from Dillon King and Jered Pieschke, and again off a dish from Brett Mooney—to turn a one-goal deficit into a 5-3 lead that the Yetis would not relinquish.

For the Yetis, the win is a massive momentum builder. After entering the night tied in the standings with a handful of other clubs, this victory pushes them into the upper echelon of the league table. The Jackalopes, meanwhile, will be left wondering how a commanding lead slipped through their fingers, though they showed plenty of teeth in those first two periods.

The Yetis proved tonight that in this league, the game isn't over until the final horn sounds. And for the fans in the stands? They got exactly what they paid for: a comeback for the ages and enough highlights to keep the group chat buzzing until the next puck drop.
