# Renegades Storm Past Tornadoes in 4-2 Victory

The Renegades finally found the win column on Sunday night, cooling off the Tornadoes with a gritty 4-2 victory that proved that hard work still beats fancy footwork in this league. While the Tornadoes arrived at the rink sporting a pair of ties and a top-seed status, the Renegades looked like a team that had spent the week sharpening their skates and their attitudes.

The tone was set early in the first period. Logan Singleton opened the scoring at the 11:47 mark, courtesy of a clean feed from Dominic Najjar, and Caeleb Burch padded the lead with a late-frame strike with just 1:32 left on the clock. The Tornadoes managed to scrape one back thanks to a solo effort from Alex Crowder at 8:17, but it was clear from the opening buzzer that the Renegades were committed to winning the battles along the boards.

"We just stopped playing like we were skating in mud," said one Renegades skater, who requested anonymity because he still had a shift-change related headache. "We put the puck on net and quit waiting for the ref to blow the whistle for us."

The second period turned into an exchange of haymakers. Singleton struck again just over a minute into the frame off a helper from Joey Pachuta, re-establishing the two-goal cushion. Once again, Crowder answered for the Tornadoes, converting a pass from Derek Smith at the 3:52 mark to keep his side within striking distance. However, any hopes of a Tornadoes comeback were stifled when Burch netted his second of the night at 9:11, capitalizing on some slick playmaking from Pachuta and Joshua Farrell.

The third period settled into a tighter, grittier affair. The ice got a bit chippy late in the game, as evidenced by a brief, albeit unnecessary, dust-up that landed Derek Smith and Matthan Hillberg in the box for roughing. It was the kind of scrappy finish that reminds you why we’re all here: a little bit of sweat, a little bit of frustration, and a whole lot of chasing a frozen piece of vulcanized rubber around a cold building.

With the win, the Renegades move to 1-0-1, successfully shaking off the draw from their season opener. For the Tornadoes, the loss is a reality check; sitting at 0-1-2, they’ll need to stop settling for single points if they want to hold onto that spot at the top of the standings. 

At the end of the day, the Renegades simply looked hungrier. They finished their checks, they hit the open man, and they sent the Tornadoes home with their first regulation loss of the season. It wasn't always pretty, but in this league, a win is a win—and it beats a tie every day of the week.
