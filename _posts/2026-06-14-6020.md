# Valkyries Soar, Puckers Roar: A Mid-June Thriller at the Rink

**DATE:** June 14, 2026  
**LOCATION:** The Barn  

If you weren’t at the arena tonight, you missed a classic. June hockey usually brings a bit of a summer slump, but the Mother Puckers and The Valkyries decided to ignore the calendar and treat us to a bona fide barn burner that had the ice steaming. In a game that saw lead changes, hard-nosed board battles, and enough drama to fill a penalty box, The Valkyries managed to hold off a charging Mother Puckers squad, escaping with a narrow 5-4 victory.

### First Period: The #97 Show
The Valkyries entered the night sitting pretty at the top of the standings, and they looked every bit the part early on. Just over a minute into the frame, the enigmatic #97 found the back of the net, drawing first blood with a laser beam. 

But the Puckers aren't a team to go down without a scrap. Malori Christian, showing the hands of a surgeon, clawed one back at the 3:09 mark, undressing the defense to tie it up. However, the period ended with a sting; after Taylor Skorupski headed to the sin bin for hooking, that pesky #97 struck again on the power play, putting the Valkyries back in the driver’s seat.

### Second Period: Trading Blows
The second period turned into a game of "anything you can do, I can do better." Nicole Baker spent two minutes in the box for hooking—a tough break for the Puckers—and wouldn't you know it, #97 was there again to capitalize, netting her hat trick and extending the Valkyries' lead.

The Puckers showed their resilience midway through the period. Nicole Baker, fresh out of the box, turned playmaker, setting up an absolute heater to cut the lead to 3-2. The crowd was roaring, the energy was electric, but the Valkyries’ mysterious #2 answered the bell almost immediately, finding a pocket of space to keep the momentum squarely in the visitors' corner.

### Third Period: The Pucker Rally
Trailing 4-2 heading into the final frame, it would have been easy for the Mother Puckers to fold up the lawn chairs and go home. Instead, they channeled their inner comeback kids. 

Malori Christian snagged her second of the night, once again assisted by the relentless Nicole Baker, making it a one-goal game. The pressure in the Valkyries' zone was thicker than a blizzard, and moments later, Amanda Gonya tied it all up, lighting the lamp off a beautiful feed from Theresa Rose Rabara. 

The building was shaking. The Puckers had all the momentum, the ice was tilted, and it felt like a potential upset was written in the stars. But hockey is a cruel mistress. With the clock winding down, that unstoppable force—#97—found the twine for her fourth of the night at 9:29. It was a dagger to the heart of the Puckers, silencing the home crowd as the Valkyries hung on for dear life to secure the 5-4 win.

### The Aftermath
For the Valkyries (4-0-0), this was a statement win. They proved they can handle pressure and possess the firepower to win a shootout-style affair. They remain the team to beat, sitting comfortably atop the standings.

For the Mother Puckers (1-1-1), it’s a tough pill to swallow, but there is plenty to build on. They played the league leaders to a one-goal game and proved they can light the lamp against anyone. If they can tighten up the defensive zone and stay out of the penalty box, this team is going to be a nightmare for the rest of the league in the second half of the season.
