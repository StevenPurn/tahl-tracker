# The Bone Zone Rattles the Lodge in 5-3 Victory

If you’re a fan of hockey that keeps your heart rate somewhere north of “leisurely stroll,” Tuesday night’s tilt between The Bone Zone and the Lodge was exactly what the doctor ordered. By the time the final buzzer rang out at 9:30 PM, The Bone Zone had secured their first win of the season, walking away with a 5-3 victory that proved they aren't just here to fill space in the standings.

The Lodge actually drew first blood early in the opening frame, with their number 79 finding the back of the net just over a minute into the contest. It was a wake-up call for The Bone Zone, who took a moment to shake off the rink-fog before mounting a serious response. Cody Kern managed to equalize things midway through the first, and before the Lodge could even get comfortable, Jeffrey Greco tucked one home off a beauty of a feed from James DePriest to take a 2-1 lead into the intermission.

The second period was a scrappy affair. The Lodge’s number 4 managed to knot it up at 45.6 seconds, but The Bone Zone responded with a goal from Jerry Bates at the 5:30 mark. The tension was palpable; the Lodge spent the latter half of the period trying to dig out of a deficit, and things got chippy enough that number 79 found himself cooling his heels in the box for a slashing minor. 

By the time the third period rolled around, the pace was frantic. The Lodge refused to roll over, pulling within one again at the 12:50 mark thanks to their number 92. It looked like we might be headed for a nail-biter finish, but Tyler Wimpee decided he’d seen enough. With 30.6 seconds remaining, Wimpee found the back of the net unassisted—a dagger of a goal that practically shut the lights out on the Lodge’s comeback hopes. 

Jeffrey Greco, who was arguably the most industrious skater on the ice tonight, put the final exclamation point on the evening. Despite a late hooking penalty and a slashing call on Garrett Williams that had the Bone Zone bench sweating, Greco struck again at the 8:27 mark, set up by Brian Smith and Williams, to effectively bury the competition.

"We knew we had to keep our sticks on the ice and our heads out of the clouds," said a breathless member of The Bone Zone post-game, reportedly while eyeing a cooler of refreshments. "The Lodge is a tough squad, but we found our rhythm when it mattered most."

For the Lodge, it’s back to the drawing board to see why the defensive zone coverage broke down during critical shifts. Meanwhile, The Bone Zone leaps up from their early-season stalemate with a much-needed two points. They entered the night tied for fifth, and after tonight’s performance, they’ve firmly established themselves as a team you don’t want to meet on a Tuesday night when the ice is slick and the pride is on the line.
