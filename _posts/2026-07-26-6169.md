# A Morning Barn Burner: Shooting Blanks Erase the Precast to Stay Perfect

**By: The Rinkside Reporter**
**Date: July 26, 2026**

If you had any plans to sleep in this Sunday morning, you missed a total heater at the rink. The Oklahoma Precast (#4) played host to the undefeated league-leaders, Shooting Blanks (#1), and what we witnessed wasn’t just a hockey game—it was an absolute fire alarm of an offensive clinic. When the dust settled on the 8-11 scoreline, the fans were breathless, and the ice was surely feeling the heat.

### First Period: Trading Punches
The game started with the intensity of a coffee-deprived morning skate, but James Stuckey quickly woke everyone up, burying a beauty just nine seconds in to put the Precast on the board. The Blanks, however, are top of the table for a reason; they don’t get rattled. 

After a Hunter Best hooking penalty gave the Precast a power play, Marshall Nunneley capitalized with a crafty strike assisted by Matthew Rankin and Nicholas Power. Down 2-0, the Blanks finally woke up. Bret Kurth found the twine, and Hunter Best—who would go on to have a morning for the history books—ripped one home to knot it up. Stuckey grabbed his second of the period before the buzzer, sending the Precast into the intermission with a narrow 3-2 edge.

### Second Period: The Blanks Open the Floodgates
If the first period was a chess match, the second was a bar fight. The Precast started with an interference penalty, and the Blanks’ special teams smelled blood in the water. Hunter Best tied the game on the man advantage, and while Joseph Pachuta answered for the Precast, the Blanks began to pull away.

The momentum shifted violently toward the visitors as Phillip Caputo and Elliott Langford began dancing through traffic in front of the net. Hunter Best added two more in the frame, including one of his classic snipes, and by the time Nicholas Power tallied a late goal for the Precast, the scoreboard read 8-6 in favor of the visitors. It was end-to-end action, with both goaltenders facing more rubber than a tire factory on a Tuesday.

### Third Period: The Closing Act
Trailing by two, the Precast came out swinging. Alex Bateman brought them within one early in the third, but the Shooting Blanks proved they have a "top shelf where mom keeps the cookies" kind of efficiency. 

Hunter Best continued his absolute masterclass, punctuating his performance with yet another goal to keep the Precast at arm's length. Bret Kurth hammered home his second of the game, and just as the Precast tried to manufacture a comeback, Hunter Best struck again—this time with Michael Bergstrom joining the party to put the game out of reach. Corey Christian managed a late consolation prize for the Precast, but the clock eventually ran out on their gallant effort.

### The Aftermath
Final Score: **Oklahoma Precast 8, Shooting Blanks 11.**

For the Shooting Blanks, it’s win number eight. They remain the undisputed kings of the hill, sitting pretty at 8-0-0 and looking like a freight train that nobody in this league can derail. Hunter Best’s multi-goal performance keeps his point streak alive and sends a loud message to the rest of the pack.

The Oklahoma Precast (2-8-0) might be disappointed with the result, but they shouldn't hang their heads. They traded blows with the best team in the league for 45 minutes of pure hockey chaos. They’ll look to regroup, tighten up the defensive zone, and find some consistency as they head into the next stretch of the season.

The ice has melted, the coffee is gone, and if you weren’t there to see it, you truly missed a wild one. Keep your sticks on the ice, folks—the season is just getting good!
