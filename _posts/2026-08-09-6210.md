# A Royal Shellacking: Mooseknuckles Blitz Laser in 12-1 Romp

**By: "Rusty" Blade, Rinkside Beat Reporter**

**August 9, 2026** – If you were hoping for a defensive struggle or a nail-biting, low-scoring affair to start your Sunday morning, you clearly weren't at the barn today. The Mooseknuckles, currently sitting pretty at the top of the standings, didn't just walk into the rink—they absolutely steamrolled the Laser in a display of offensive firepower that left the ice smoking.

When the dust settled at the final buzzer, the scoreboard read **Mooseknuckles 12, Laser 1**. It wasn't so much a hockey game as it was a clinical exhibition of puck movement that would make a geometry teacher blush.

### Period 1: Opening the Floodgates
The Mooseknuckles came out of the gate looking like they’d had three extra espressos in the locker room. Pete Fleming struck first at the 10:04 mark, burying a feed from Michael Pacheco and Jess Herd. That seemed to open the floodgates. By the time the dust settled on the first frame, Pacheco, Scott Bartkus, and Mark Hodges had all found the back of the net.

The Laser tried to spark a comeback when the red-hot Robert Verity—keeping his point streak alive—snuck one past the Mooseknuckles’ tendy at the 7:52 mark. But honestly? It was the only light in the tunnel. The Laser found themselves chasing the play all period, struggling to clear the zone while the Mooseknuckles dominated the dirty areas.

### Period 2: The "Hat Trick" Machine
If the first period was a drizzle, the second was a monsoon. Mark Hodges turned the game into his personal highlight reel, racking up goals with a relentless, north-south style of play. Jess Herd was the puppet master all night, dishing out assists like he was handing out candy on Halloween.

By the time Nicholas Strle and Ben Savory added their names to the scoresheet, the Laser looked like they were skating through wet cement. The Mooseknuckles were firing lasers of their own, finding corners with such surgical precision that it felt like they were picking the cookies off the top shelf while the Laser goalie was still trying to find his bearings.

### Period 3: Finishing the Job
To their credit, the Laser kept skating, but the Mooseknuckles weren't taking their foot off the gas. Garrett Williams kept his goal-scoring streak alive, and Jess Herd—not content with just being the league’s most generous playmaker—put one home himself at the 8:01 mark to put the exclamation point on a 12-goal masterclass.

### The Aftermath: What Does it Mean?
For the Mooseknuckles, this win is a statement. They aren't just winning games; they are asserting dominance. With a 7-2-0 record, they are officially the team to beat in this league. Their chemistry, particularly the connection between Herd and his wingers, is firing on all cylinders.

As for the Laser (4-5-1), this is a tough pill to swallow. They’ll need to hit the reset button and shore up their defensive zone coverage. Getting blown out by an 11-goal margin is a sobering wake-up call, but if they want to climb out of the bottom half of the standings, they’ll need to find a way to stop the bleeding and tighten up their transition game before their next tilt.

The Mooseknuckles are heading home to rest their sticks, and the Laser are heading to the drawing board. One thing is for sure: after a 12-1 performance, the Mooseknuckles have put the rest of the league on high-octane notice.
