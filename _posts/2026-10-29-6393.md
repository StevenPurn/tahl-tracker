# Kraken Cold Ones Stun Berzerkers in 6-1 Rout

If you looked at the standings heading into Tuesday night’s tilt, the Kraken Cold Ones were statistically adrift, anchored in 12th place with a dismal 1-4-1 record. Meanwhile, the third-place Berzerkers were humming along with the confidence of a team that had only tasted defeat once in five outings. But hockey, as it often does, decided to treat the spreadsheet like a suggestion rather than a rulebook, as the Kraken dismantled the Berzerkers 6-1 in a performance that turned the league’s power rankings on their head.

The early stages of the game suggested the script would proceed as expected. The Berzerkers drew first blood at the 11:39 mark of the first period when Stephen Jeffery found the back of the net, assisted by Hayden Wheeler. Extending his point streak to four games, Jeffery looked to be the catalyst for another Berzerkers victory. 

Then, the narrative shifted. 

At 4:49 of the opening frame, Gregory Boyer struck for the Kraken, evening the score and effectively decapitating the Berzerkers’ momentum. It was the first of what would become an offensive barrage, as the Kraken found an extra gear that has been suspiciously absent throughout their early-season slump.

The second period was a masterclass in efficiency for the Kraken. Tommy Manning broke the tie just under four minutes in, set up by Edgar Solorio, and Chase Stubblefield followed up with an unassisted tally at 7:01 to extend the lead to 3-1. By the time Justin McKinney cleaned up a feed from Jeremy Wentworth and Randy Hatfield at 9:27, the Berzerkers’ bench was looking visibly rattled. 

"I think we forgot to account for the fact that a cornered Kraken is still a cephalopod with eight tentacles," remarked one bewildered Berzerkers observer during the second intermission. "We were playing checkers; they were playing, well, I don't know what they were playing, but it was working."

While the Berzerkers struggled to regain their composure, the Kraken simply refused to slow down. Gregory Boyer, having already opened the scoring, turned his night into a highlight reel. He added a goal at the very buzzer of the second period—credited at 00:00—and then capped off his hat trick at 9:33 of the third period. Boyer’s three-goal effort served as the definitive nail in the coffin, turning a competitive game into an emphatic statement.

The Berzerkers’ frustration boiled over in the third when Hayden Wheeler headed to the box for hooking at 2:26, but the Kraken’s special teams unit wasn't even needed to put this one away. The Berzerkers were thoroughly outworked in every zone, their typically reliable offensive flow stifled by a Kraken squad that seemed to play with a newfound urgency.

For the Berzerkers, who drop to a 3-2-1 record, this loss is a sobering reminder that there are no "gimme" games in this league. For the Kraken, the win marks their first victory since the season opener and provides a much-needed morale boost. Whether this is a statistical anomaly or the start of a genuine climb back up the standings remains to be seen, but for one night, the 12th-place underdogs proved that the scoreboard has the final say.
