# Murder Steals the Show in Shootout Thriller Over Mojo

In a matchup that won’t be confused with the high-octane offensive displays of The Bone Zone or the Puckedelics, the Tulsa Murder managed to grind out a 3-2 shootout victory over Mojo this Saturday afternoon. While the spreadsheet may not have been painted with highlight-reel passing plays, the game was a masterclass in stubbornness, with the Murder snapping their alternating win-loss streak pattern by securing a hard-fought two points.

The game began with a disciplined, albeit cautious, pace. Mojo’s Jesse Stockley broke the deadlock at the 7:34 mark of the first period, finding the back of the net unassisted. The Murder, who have struggled to find consistent chemistry this season, responded at 9:30 when Andrew Foote converted a feed from Jarred Butler. It was a rare moment of offensive fluidity in a period otherwise defined by Tulsa’s trips to the penalty box; Spencer Charles and Triston Wilson both sat for minor infractions, forcing the Murder’s penalty kill to work overtime just to keep the score knotted at one heading into the intermission.

If the first period was about containment, the second period was about controlled chaos. Juan Padilla reclaimed the lead for Mojo at 5:49, but the lead lasted less than two minutes. Mark Hodges, showing the kind of situational awareness that scouts dream about, equalized for the Murder at 7:18 with an assist from Triston Wilson. The middle frame was further marred by a flurry of extracurricular activity, including coincidental roughing and unsportsmanlike conduct penalties that saw the ice crowded with tension but short on scoring chances.

By the third period, the legs were heavy and the systems had effectively neutralized one another. Despite a late roughing penalty to Gilbert Collard that had the Murder bench pacing nervously, the defensive structure held firm. The goaltenders for both sides deserve a nod for their resolve; in a league where puck possession is often a suggestion rather than a strategy, they kept the scoreboard from ballooning during several high-pressure sequences.

With the deadlock unbroken after 36 minutes of regulation play, the game shifted to a shootout. It was here that Mark Hodges took center stage, providing the decisive goal that secured the win for Tulsa.

For the Tulsa Murder, the victory is a much-needed shot in the arm as they attempt to climb out of the bottom third of the standings. While they currently sit at 2-4-1, the two points tonight represent a vital correction in their trajectory. Mojo, meanwhile, finds their momentum stalled; coming off a stretch where they had secured points in three straight games, this loss will undoubtedly sting. 

"We didn't win by being fancy," remarked one anonymous source close to the Murder locker room, perhaps gesturing toward the team’s penchant for finding themselves in the penalty box. "We won by being the last ones standing." 

If the Murder want to challenge the upper echelon of this league, they’ll need to clean up the discipline issues that saw them shorthanded five times on Saturday. For now, they’ll settle for the two points and the satisfaction of knowing that, at least for today, the math fell in their favor.
