# Yetis Stomp Out Murder in 4-1 Victory

If you were looking for a high-octane offensive showcase at the rink on Friday night, you might have been disappointed by the first period, which felt like watching two guys try to solve a Rubik’s Cube with hockey gloves on. But once the Yetis finally found their groove in the second, they didn’t just turn up the heat—they turned the Tulsa Murder into a block of ice. The Yetis skated away with a commanding 4-1 win, proving once again that when this team clicks, they’re as cold and unforgiving as their name suggests.

The first frame was a parade to the penalty box, with both teams trading trips and hooks like they were passing out candy at Halloween. Kirk Nielsen and Benjamin Gartner both spent time in the sin bin for the Yetis, but the Murder couldn’t capitalize on the power play, effectively letting the Yetis off the hook early. It was a scoreless stalemate heading into the intermission, leading one fan in the stands to shout, “I’ve seen more action at a local library!”

The deadlock finally broke at the 10:03 mark of the second period when Skylar Miller took matters into his own hands. Miller snagged the puck and fired it home unassisted, finally giving the Yetis some breathing room. It was the spark the team needed, and by the third period, they were playing like they had a dinner reservation they were desperate to make.

The floodgates officially opened in the final frame. Steven Belanger extended the lead at 13:06, tapping in a beautiful feed from Brett Mooney. Not long after, Jason Bahr—clearly hungry to keep his scoring streak alive—added another at the 1:46 mark, thanks to a crisp setup from Timothy N See. By the time Aidan Doyle tacked on an unassisted beauty with under a minute left, the Yetis were essentially doing victory laps.

To their credit, the Tulsa Murder didn't just fold up the chairs and go home early. With just over eight minutes left on the clock, Mark Hodges managed to ruin the Yetis' shutout bid, converting on a nice play orchestrated by Jarred Butler and Pete Fleming. It was a small consolation prize in a game that largely belonged to the visitors.

The Yetis look like a team finding their identity, moving to 3-2-1 and solidifying their spot in the upper echelon of the league standings. For the Tulsa Murder, who drop to 1-3-1, it’s back to the drawing board to find some offensive consistency. As I always say, you can’t win games if you’re only checking the scoreboard in the final ten minutes. The Yetis head home with the points, and the rest of the league would be wise to take note—the frost is settling in.
