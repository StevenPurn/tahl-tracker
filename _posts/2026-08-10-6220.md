# Mayhem Wreck+ Steamrolls Hot Garbage in Decisive 5-1 Victory

If you were looking for a masterclass in puck possession and sustained pressure, you likely weren’t at the rink on August 9th. But if you were looking for a textbook example of how to exploit defensive gaps, the Mayhem Wreck+ provided exactly that, dismantling Hot Garbage in a 5-1 rout that was significantly more lopsided than the final scoreboard suggests.

Entering the contest, both teams were looking to establish momentum, but it was the fourth-place Mayhem Wreck+ who looked like the title contenders. Despite a respectable season, Hot Garbage (#7) simply couldn't find an answer for the relentless transition game of the Wreck+.

The game remained a tense, scoreless stalemate until the early stages of the second period. Alan Dyke momentarily sparked some hope for Hot Garbage at the 12:06 mark, breaking the ice with an unassisted effort that surely had the Garbage bench thinking they had the momentum. That optimism lasted exactly 22 seconds. John Saltsman answered back at 13:44, effectively resetting the game state and stifling any "garbage time" momentum before it could fully take root.

From that point on, the math began to look ugly for the visitors. Justin Demoss added a goal at 5:02 of the second, and by the time the teams headed to the locker rooms, Mayhem Wreck+ had seized total control.

The third period served as a brutal reinforcement of the Wreck+'s superiority. A 13-second flurry midway through the frame saw John Saltsman collect his second of the night—thanks to a crisp setup from Jake Jewson—followed immediately by a Stephen Jeffery strike assisted by Spencer Hodgson. Any lingering thoughts of a Hot Garbage comeback were systematically dismantled, and Demoss punctuated his multi-goal night with a final tally at 2:53, assisted by Justin McKinney.

"We just stopped looking at the scoreboard and started looking at the open ice," remarked one Wreck+ observer after the final whistle. It’s a simple philosophy, but when executed with this level of efficiency, it’s nearly impossible to defend against.

For Mayhem Wreck+, the victory is a massive boost, keeping them firmly in the hunt at 8-2-1 (17 points). They’ve now secured three wins in their last five, showing a level of consistency that will be vital as the season rounds the corner. Conversely, Hot Garbage sees their momentum stalled after a run of three straight wins earlier this month; at 5-4-1, they will need to recalibrate their defensive structure if they hope to climb back into the top half of the standings.

Hockey, as we know, is a game of inches—or in this case, a game of 5-1. The spreadsheet doesn't lie: Mayhem Wreck+ controlled the lanes, maximized their opportunities, and effectively sent Hot Garbage to the curb.
