# Mighty Drunks and Blue Bombers Trade Leather in 5-5 Deadlock

If you came to the rink on Tuesday night expecting a defensive clinic, you picked the wrong night to leave your house. The Mighty Drunks and the Blue Bombers locked horns in a mid-table showdown that ended in a 5-5 stalemate, a result that felt less like a tie and more like two prize fighters forgetting to keep their hands up for sixty minutes.

It was a night defined by a singular, persistent headache for the Drunks: Blue Bombers forward Dylan Walker. Walker decided the back of the net was his personal mailbox, hammering home an incredible four goals on the evening. He was everywhere—finding soft spots in the coverage, winning puck battles, and reminding anyone watching that when you give a shooter that much room, he’s going to make you pay.

The Drunks, to their credit, didn’t roll over. They countered with a more balanced attack, led by the Slattery-Awad connection. Justen Monroe kicked off the festivities early, and by the time the first period whistle blew, the Drunks had managed to claw their way to a 3-2 lead. Omar Awad continued his recent tear with a goal of his own, looking comfortable with the puck on his stick and, quite frankly, like the only guy on the ice who remembered that backchecking is a part of the game.

The second period, however, saw the momentum shift firmly into the Bombers' corner. Walker went on a scoring tear that had the scorekeepers sweating, netting two quick ones to erase the deficit and put the Bombers in the driver’s seat. Just when it looked like the Drunks might crumble, Cian Slattery hammered one home, followed by a late-period response from Aaron West—who continues to pad his point streak—that kept the Bombers feeling good heading into the final frame.

The third period turned into a chippy, whistle-heavy affair. Between the trips and the slashes, it felt like the refs were getting more cardio than the skaters. With the clock winding down, Gage Walker provided the heroics the Drunks needed, scoring an unassisted goal while the teams were scrambling, effectively forcing the game into the shootout.

In the skills competition, it was the Walker Show all over again. Gage Walker potted one for the Drunks, and naturally, Dylan Walker responded for the Bombers, ensuring the game ended on the same chaotic note it started with.

For the standings, this draw keeps the Bombers and the Drunks deadlocked in a tight race for second place, both sitting at 10 points. If they keep playing like this, the only thing they’ll be competing for is who needs more Advil in the locker room after the game. It wasn't pretty, it wasn't disciplined, but for the crowd braving a Tuesday night, it was exactly the kind of local hockey madness that keeps us coming back.
