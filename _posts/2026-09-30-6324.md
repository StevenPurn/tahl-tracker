# Mojo Finds Their Groove, Topples Leafs in 4-2 Victory

Stop the presses and call the neighbors—the Mojo have finally stopped being just a name on the stat sheet and started acting like a force of nature. In a late-night regular season tilt that saw enough back-and-forth action to give a tennis umpire whiplash, the Mojo stormed out of their winless slump to dismantle the Tulsa Leafs 4-2. 

The hero of the hour was undeniably Andrew Armendariz, who treated the ice like his personal playground. Armendariz notched a spectacular hat trick, proving that if you give him an inch, he’ll take a mile—or at least three goals. He opened the scoring at the 14:38 mark of the first, courtesy of a slick feed from Austin Stauffer, setting the tone for a night where the Mojo refused to be silenced.

The Leafs, not ones to let a party get started without their input, answered back quickly. Zac Hughes, currently riding a heater of a scoring streak, buried an unassisted goal at 4:54 to equalize. It was a scrappy, physical affair, with both benches chirping enough to wake the ghosts of rinks past. 

As the second period unfolded, the momentum shifted like a pendulum in a hurricane. Armendariz restored the lead for the Mojo midway through the frame, but the Leafs clung on with the tenacity of a barnacle. When the Mojo's Kyle White headed to the box for interference, the Leafs capitalized on the man-advantage. It wasn’t a standard power play goal, however; it was Andrew Foote finding the twine at 3:03, assisted by Jonathan Lee and Dillon King, knotted up at 2-2. 

"We were playing like we were skating on butter out there," quipped a Mojo bench staffer post-game. "But once Andrew [Armendariz] found his rhythm, the whole locker room felt like they’d just drank five espressos. It was electric."

The decisive blow came in the third, and it was a sequence that will surely be replayed in the locker room for weeks. With time ticking away and the tension thick enough to cut with a skate blade, Armendariz struck again at the 07.7 mark. As if adding insult to injury, Douglas Moore was sent to the penalty box on the same play, unraveling the Leafs' defensive composure. 

With the Leafs scrambling to regain their footing, Austin Stauffer put the final nail in the coffin at 8:44, icing the game and sending the Mojo faithful—all dozen or so of them—into a frenzy. 

For the Mojo, this 4-2 result is more than just a notch in the win column; it’s a total reset. They entered the night at the bottom of the standings, but after tonight, they’ve proven that the basement is no place for a team with this much snarl. The Leafs, meanwhile, will look to shake off the loss and find the form that made them a mid-table threat. 

The ice has thawed, the puck has settled, and for one night at least, the Mojo have all the moves.
