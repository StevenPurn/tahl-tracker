# Goats Find Their Stride in 5-3 Win Over Puckheads

The Killer Goats climbed a few rungs up the standings ladder on Friday night, proving that sometimes you don’t need fancy systems to win—you just need to keep your nose to the grindstone and hit the back of the net when it counts. In a 5-3 decision that had enough momentum shifts to make a rink manager dizzy, the Goats managed to outlast the Puckheads in a regular-season tilt that reminded everyone why we still lace ‘em up.

The opening frame was a chaotic affair that looked more like a track meet than a hockey game. The Puckheads struck first at the 10:36 mark when William Hofmeister capitalized on a tidy setup from Jamie Halverson and James Rozell. But if you’ve watched this league long enough, you know the Goats aren’t the type to fold their cards early. Just twenty-one seconds later, Samuel Michaud answered back to knot things up, turning the tide before the scoreboard operator could even catch his breath.

The Goats caught fire late in the first, fueled by some gritty individual efforts. After killing off a couple of minor penalties, the team found a rhythm. Erik Chillingworth managed a solo effort at 13:54 to put the Goats ahead, and shortly after, Dylan Criquet Danielson extended the lead with help from Lucas Blanchard. By the time the buzzer sounded on the first, the Goats had established a cushion that, frankly, they needed.

The second period proved that "comfortable" is a dangerous word in beer league hockey. The Puckheads tightened their laces and started crashing the crease. A costly tripping penalty by Aaron Quinton gave the Puckheads a man-advantage, and Hofmeister didn’t need a second invitation, burying his second of the night just eight seconds into the power play. It was the kind of goal that swings the energy in the building, leaving the Goats looking a bit rattled as the frame wore on.

However, the final period saw the Goats re-assert their physical presence. Samuel Michaud notched his second of the game at 12:07, again working in tandem with Dustin Giebel, before Lucas Blanchard added some insurance with an unassisted marker. Kellen Nitz pulled one back for the Puckheads late in the game off a dish from Cameron Sartin and Gregory Douglas, but it was a case of too little, too late.

"It wasn't pretty, and there were times we were skating like we had cinderblocks in our skates," one anonymous Goats veteran joked in the parking lot after the buzzer. "But a win’s a win. We’ll take the two points and worry about the style points later."

For the Goats, the victory is a much-needed shot in the arm as they look to distance themselves from the middle of the pack. The Puckheads, meanwhile, will be looking at the game tape wondering how they let this one slip away after clawing back within one in the second. In a league where the standings are tighter than a pair of brand-new skates, every goal matters, and tonight, the Goats simply had one more in them than their neighbors.
