# Concrete Confidence: Precast Edges Tornadoes in a Sunday Morning Barn Burner

**OKLAHOMA CITY** – If you were hoping to sleep in this Sunday, you clearly weren't at the rink. The 9:45 a.m. puck drop between the Tornadoes and Oklahoma Precast turned into a full-blown hockey clinic, featuring enough lead changes and bad blood to keep the coffee brewing and the heart rates elevated. In an 11-goal thriller, Oklahoma Precast managed to pour the foundation for their second win of the season, edging the Tornadoes 6-5.

### Period 1: Rankin Takes the Reins
The Tornadoes struck first when Alex Crowder buried one past the netminder at 10:55, with help from Michael Bergstrom and Justin Gibson. But if there’s one thing we’ve learned, it’s that you don’t poke the Precast bear. Matthew Rankin, riding a white-hot three-game goal streak, decided he wasn’t done yet. He leveled the score at 13:55 with a solo effort that had the goalie reaching for thin air. 

Even after Nicholas Power found himself sitting in the sin bin for a hook, Precast didn't blink. They killed the penalty and immediately pushed back, with Rankin netting his second of the morning at 8:28, courtesy of a slick setup from Power and Christopher Cahill. 

### Period 2: The Goal-Scoring Carousel
If the first period was a tactical chess match, the second was a back-alley brawl. Justin Gibson knotted it up at 13:14, and Tobias Alvegran quickly followed suit to give the Tornadoes a 3-2 lead. 

But this wasn't a day for defensemen to pad their stats. Christopher Cahill answered the call at 3:59 to tie it up, only for Christian Alvegran to restore the Tornadoes' lead moments later. Just when it looked like the Tornadoes were blowing away with the momentum, Joseph Pachuta stepped up for Precast, going unassisted to hammer one home. By the time the horn sounded for the intermission, we were knotted at 4-4 in a game that felt like it had been played in a phone booth.

### Period 3: Power Trips and Pure Grit
The final frame was defined by Nicholas Power—for better and for worse. Power found himself in a parade to the penalty box, eventually getting hit with an Unsportsmanlike Conduct major and an early exit to the showers after an intense sequence of penalties. 

Despite being down a man and dealing with the chaos of the officiating, Precast kept their cool. With just over two minutes remaining, Matthew Rankin—the undisputed MVP of this morning tilt—completed his hat trick, feeding off a perfect pass from Power (who, despite his penalty woes, was the playmaker of the game).

The Tornadoes made a desperate final push, with Alex Crowder finding the twine with 20 seconds left to cut the lead to one, but Christopher Cahill’s earlier insurance goal at the 8:24 mark proved to be the difference-maker. The Precast squad clamped down in the dying seconds, sealing the 6-5 victory.

### The Big Picture
For Oklahoma Precast (2-5-0), this win is a massive shot in the arm. Climbing out of the cellar requires grit, and beating a team ahead of them in the standings is exactly the kind of momentum that turns a season around. They’ve proven they can hang with the middle of the pack; now, the challenge is consistency.

The Tornadoes (2-4-0) will be looking at this one in the rearview mirror and shaking their heads. They had the chances, they had the goals, but they couldn't contain the Rankin-Cahill tandem when it mattered most. 

As for the fans? We saw a 9:45 a.m. game that had more action than most primetime showdowns. Grab your breakfast sandwiches and hold onto your hats—if this is how these two play on a Sunday morning, the rest of the season is going to be one wild ride.
