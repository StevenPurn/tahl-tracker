# Puckheads Upset Hitmen Silver in Offensive Seesaw

If you were looking for a defensive masterclass on a Tuesday night at the rink, you came to the wrong place. If, however, you enjoy seeing the scoreboard operator get a workout, the Puckheads’ 7-5 victory over Hitmen Silver was exactly the kind of chaotic, end-to-end hockey that keeps the beer league lights humming.

The Puckheads walked into this one sitting fifth in the standings, while the second-place Hitmen Silver were riding a four-game heater. But as any veteran of these leagues knows, form usually goes out the window the moment the puck drops.

The game started with the Puckheads’ Gregory Douglas and Steven Ramos putting their side up by two early in the first. The Hitmen, clearly annoyed by the early deficit, decided to play a little “anything you can do” drill. In a blink-and-you-missed-it span of 37 seconds, Lukas Vodicka scored twice, sandwiching a goal by Keegan Guthrie to turn a 2-0 hole into a 3-2 lead before the ice had even settled. It was the kind of momentum shift that usually deflates an opponent, but the Puckheads refused to fold their tent.

The second period saw the Hitmen extend their lead to 5-3, thanks to a second-period goal from Vodicka—his third of the night—and a tidy finish from Bobby Pickett. At that point, it looked like the Hitmen were going to cruise, but someone must have spiked the Puckheads' water bottles during the second intermission.

The third period turned into a nightmare for the Hitmen and a showcase for the Puckheads’ resilience. Jake Sartin took matters into his own hands, potting two consecutive unassisted goals to knot the score at 5-5. Suddenly, the bench that had been quiet all night was buzzing. Andrew Retkowski added a third for the Puckheads shortly after, and Gregory Douglas put the exclamation point on the comeback with his second of the game, coming on a shorthanded effort despite the Puckheads playing down a man due to a hooking penalty.

"I’m not saying we planned to give them a five-goal head start in terms of rhythm, but we certainly made it interesting," one Puckhead skater joked in the parking lot, though he probably wasn't entirely joking.

For the Hitmen Silver, the loss serves as a harsh reality check. After winning four straight, they looked a step slow when the Puckheads turned on the jets in the final frame. The Puckheads, meanwhile, proved that their 2-2-1 record was perhaps a bit deceiving; when they decide to play a full 45 minutes of aggressive, north-south hockey, they can hang with the top of the table.

It was an ugly, beautiful mess of a hockey game—the kind that leaves the goalie wondering why he ever volunteered for the position and the rest of us wondering if we should’ve ordered an extra round at the pub. The Puckheads head home with the two points, while the Hitmen are left to ponder how they let a three-goal lead evaporate into thin air.
