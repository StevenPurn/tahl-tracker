# Blue Bombers Offense Explodes in 7-3 Rout of Tuskens

If you’re a fan of high-scoring affairs and seeing a defense completely abandon the concept of backchecking, the late-night tilt between the Blue Bombers and the Tulsa Tuskens at the rink on Sunday was exactly what the doctor ordered. The Blue Bombers walked away with a dominant 7-3 victory, effectively snapping their uneven form and leaving the Tuskens searching for answers to a leaky blue line.

The game started as a frantic, back-and-forth affair that suggested a much closer contest. Tuskens forward Dylan Walker continued his absolute tear through the league, netting a hat trick that accounted for all of Tulsa’s offense. Walker has been playing like a man possessed, and for the first period, he looked like the only skater who had finished his morning coffee. 

However, the Bombers possess a depth that the Tuskens simply couldn't contain. While Walker was busy padding his stats, Austin Smith was busy dismantling the Tuskens' structure. Smith was the star of the show, delivering a clinical performance that saw him tally three goals, effectively breaking the game open midway through the second period. 

"I think we just stopped watching the puck and started watching the clock," Tuskens coach "Mumbles" McGee joked after the final whistle. "When you give a guy like Smith that much space, you might as well hand him a goal-scoring trophy at center ice and save everyone the trouble of skating."

The turning point came in the second period. After Walker’s third goal of the night made it 3-3, the Tuskens seemed to deflate. The Bombers responded immediately with Robert Millea putting one home just seconds later, followed by a pair of goals from Smith to pull away. Once the lead hit 6-3 early in the third, the Bombers tightened up their defensive shell, limiting the Tuskens to the perimeter and letting the clock do the heavy lifting.

Aaron West also chipped in with a pair of goals for the Bombers, including a late-third-period marker that sealed the deal. Aside from a minor hooking penalty for Jordan Hawkins Baker and a tripping call on Tobias Roeding, the Bombers played a surprisingly disciplined game for a team that spent most of their time in the offensive zone.

For the Blue Bombers, the win is a much-needed morale booster as they sit in the middle of the pack. They played with an urgency that the Tuskens—who remain winless on the season—lacked for large stretches of the game. If the Bombers can replicate this kind of offensive output while keeping their defensive assignments as tidy as they were in the final frame, they’ll be a tough out for anyone in the league. 

The Tuskens, meanwhile, have plenty of film to review. Relying on Dylan Walker to carry the entire offensive load is a fine strategy for a highlight reel, but it’s a losing formula for a hockey game. They’ll need to find some secondary scoring and a bit more grit on the backend if they want to climb out of the bottom tier of the standings.
