# Perfect Season: Shooting Blanks Secure Championship Crown

If you were looking for a fairytale upset on Saturday afternoon, you picked the wrong rink. The Shooting Blanks capped off an impeccable, undefeated season by dismantling the Renegades 5-2, proving once and for all that a perfect record isn’t just a statistical anomaly—it’s a habit.

The game started with the Renegades playing the role of the spoiler with gusto. Alex Bateman opened the scoring just over five minutes into the first period, unassisted, signaling that the underdog wasn't just there to hand over the trophy. Even when the Blanks’ Brett Allen leveled the score later in the frame, the Renegades answered back through Joshua Farrell, assisted by Joseph Pachuta and Bateman. As the teams headed to the second period with the Renegades holding a 2-1 lead, there was a genuine sense in the bleachers that the Blanks might finally be sweating.

That sweat didn't last long. The second period was a gritty, whistle-heavy affair that turned into an absolute circus of penalties. Between the slashing, tripping, and holding calls, the ice felt more like a courtroom than a hockey rink. But in the midst of the chaos, the Blanks' depth proved to be the difference-maker.

Bret Kurth ignited the turnaround at the 5:29 mark of the second, and from there, the floodgates swung open. Hunter Best followed up less than a minute later, and by the time James Stuckey buried a pass from Elliott Langford, the Blanks had rattled off three unanswered goals in a blistering span of action.

"We spent more time in the box than a grumpy teenager," remarked one Blanks veteran on the bench, wiping sweat from his brow. "But when we actually managed to keep five guys on the ice, we remembered why we haven't lost a game all year."

The Renegades, who had played well to earn their spot in the final, simply couldn't find an answer for the sustained pressure of the league leaders. David Best put the final nail in the coffin early in the third, stretching the lead to 5-2 and effectively ending any hope of a Renegades comeback.

The Shooting Blanks finish their championship run at 11-0-0, a dominant statement in a season where they were clearly playing a different game than the rest of the pack. The Renegades, while disappointed to finish as runners-up, proved they were the best of the rest, showing flashes of brilliance that kept a powerhouse team on their toes for nearly half the match.

As for the Blanks, they’ll head into the off-season with the hardware and an undefeated record to brag about at the local watering hole. In this league, where the coffee is usually cold and the Zamboni is always a bit late, perfection is a rare commodity. Today, at least, the Shooting Blanks made it look easy.
