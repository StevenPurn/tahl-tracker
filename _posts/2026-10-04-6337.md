# A Bone-Chilling Shutout at the Rink

If you were looking for the Tulsa Leafs on Sunday afternoon, you might have checked the wrong ice surface, because for sixty minutes, they were essentially ghosts. The Bone Zone rolled into the rink like a storm front and didn’t stop until they’d put a decisive 7-0 walloping on the Leafs, proving exactly why they’re sitting pretty at the top of the standings.

The tone for the afternoon was set early. Tyler Wimpee continued his scorching run, potting the opener just over eight minutes in. Before the Leafs could even lace their skates tight enough to respond, Brian Smith cleaned up a feed from Jeffrey Greco only thirty-three seconds later to double the lead. By the time the horn sounded for the first intermission, the Leafs looked like they were trying to solve a Rubik’s cube while wearing mittens.

The second period was where the wheels didn't just fall off the Leafs' wagon—the whole carriage disintegrated. The Bone Zone turned the ice into their personal playground, racking up four goals in the middle frame alone. Jeffrey Greco kept his hot streak alive, and Jerry Bates and Richard Looney piled on the misery for the Tulsa defense. When Wimpee notched his second of the game at the 6:03 mark, it was clear that the only thing the Leafs were going to be heading home with was a sore neck from watching the puck cross their own goal line.

Credit where it’s due: The Bone Zone didn't play like a team satisfied with a comfortable lead; they played like a team allergic to letting the opposition breathe. Even when Brian Smith spent two minutes in the sin bin for high-sticking, the Leafs couldn't find a spark. "We were chasing shadows out there," one frustrated Leafs fan remarked while clutching a lukewarm coffee near the glass. "I think the only thing we successfully defended was the penalty box."

Jerry Bates put the final nail in the coffin midway through the third, burying his second of the day off a nice setup from Bryce Perkins and Austin Stauffer. 

For The Bone Zone, this 7-0 demolition is a loud statement to the rest of the league. Sitting at the top of the heap with a 3-0-1 record, they’ve got all the momentum in the world. As for the Tulsa Leafs, who drop to 1-2-1, they’ve got some soul-searching to do. They’ll need to shake off the dust and find their footing fast, because after a performance like that, the only way to go is up—or at least, anywhere but back to that particular brand of misery.
