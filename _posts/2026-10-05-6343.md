# Tulsa Murder Stave Off Laser Comeback in Nail-Biter

If you were looking for a high-octane offensive display on a Friday night, the Tulsa Murder and the Laser certainly delivered the goods. In a back-and-forth thriller that had the folks in the stands gripping their programs a little tighter than usual, the Murder finally shook the monkey off their back, narrowly edging out the Laser 5-4 to secure their first win of the season.

The game started at a frantic pace, with the Murder’s "Not Signed In"—who, for the record, might have the most mysterious jersey in the league—opening the scoring just 81 seconds into the opening frame off a helper from Jarred Butler. The Laser’s Robert Verity, showing exactly why he’s currently on a scoring tear, leveled things up midway through the period. 

The first period was a bit of a chaotic carousel, featuring a flurry of goals and a couple of trips to the sin bin for Laser’s Timothy N. See. After "Not Signed In" netted his second of the night and Mark Hodges hammered one home on the power play, you could tell the Murder were hungry. But the Laser weren’t about to roll over; Chad Pancoast kept the tension high with a gritty unassisted goal just before the intermission.

By the time the second period rolled around, it felt less like a hockey game and more like a backyard brawl. MARK HODGES, who was clearly the architect of the Murder's offense tonight, spent two minutes in the box for interference, only for his team to flip the script immediately. Despite being shorthanded, Jonathan P. Sanders found the back of the net to extend the lead, showing the kind of opportunistic hustle that makes a locker room hum. DAVID MANSFIELD answered back for the Laser shortly after, narrowing the deficit to one.

The third period was a true test of nerves. James Whitacre tied things up at 4-4 early, and for a long stretch, it looked like we might be heading toward a dreaded tie. The Laser were playing hard, but they shot themselves in the foot with a "too many men on the ice" penalty with under ten minutes to go. The Murder kept the pressure cooking, and with just 22.8 seconds remaining on the clock, TRISTON WILSON became the hero of the hour, burying a feed from Hodges to seal the 5-4 victory.

"We knew we had to get off the schneid eventually," a clearly relieved, albeit exhausted, Murder player noted after the buzzer. "It wasn't pretty, it wasn't quiet, but we found a way to stop the bleeding."

For the Laser, the loss is a tough pill to swallow after such a spirited comeback effort, but they’ll have to look at the defensive miscues that kept them chasing the lead all night. Both teams entered the contest at the bottom of the standings with identical 0-2-1 records, but tonight, the Tulsa Murder finally found their rhythm, leaving the Laser looking to regroup as the season moves into the next phase. If this game is any indication, both of these squads are far more dangerous than their current win columns suggest.
