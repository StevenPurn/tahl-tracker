# Double-Digit Domination: Renegades Roll Over Tornadoes in 10-4 Rout

If you happened to wander into the rink last night, you might have thought you stumbled into a Renegades practice session—specifically, the one where they teach you how to fill the net until it looks like a spiderwebbed mess. In a game that saw the scoreboard operator working harder than a Zamboni driver on a double shift, the league-leading Renegades dismantled the Tornadoes with a resounding 10-4 victory.

The Renegades, currently sitting at the top of the standings, showed exactly why they are the team to beat. Bobby Watson kicked off the festivities just 17 seconds into the game, a goal so early it caught the concession stand staff still buttering popcorn. From there, it was the Elliott Langford show. Langford, riding a hot streak, added two quick tallies in the first period, effectively turning the Tornadoes' defensive zone into a revolving door of bad news.

"I tried to tell the boys to keep their sticks on the ice," said a fictional, bewildered Tornadoes coach post-game. "But at a certain point, I think we were just acting as extras in a Renegades highlight reel."

The Tornadoes did show signs of life in the second period, with Braden LaJoie and Gregory Douglas providing a glimmer of hope to narrow the gap. However, the Renegades’ offense is a hydra—cut off one head, and three more start passing the puck. Curtis Tucker and Alex Bateman slammed the door shut on any potential comeback by responding with goals of their own, ensuring the Renegades maintained their iron grip on the game.

The third period was an absolute fever dream of hockey offense. Eli Augustine snatched two goals in the span of mere moments, while Matthan Hillberg—who seemed to be everywhere on the ice, racking up assists like he was collecting frequent flyer miles—added a goal of his own to bury the Tornadoes under an avalanche of rubber. Bobby Watson eventually rounded out the scoring for the Renegades, netting his second of the night in a display of offensive efficiency that bordered on the cruel.

While the Tornadoes did manage a third goal from Christian Alvegran and another from Gregory Douglas, it was a classic case of too little, too late. The intensity peaked briefly at the 2:47 mark of the third when Bazil Cardamone and Alex Bateman decided to get up close and personal, resulting in offsetting roughing penalties, but even that couldn't stop the Renegades' momentum.

The Renegades move to 6-1-1 on the season, continuing their dominant form and proving that their top-tier status is no fluke. For the Tornadoes, who remain winless at 0-5-3, it’s a tough pill to swallow. As the Renegades skated off, they looked like a team that hadn't just won a game; they looked like a team that had finished a very productive workout, leaving the rest of the league to wonder if anyone can stop this offensive juggernaut before they truly shift into high gear.
