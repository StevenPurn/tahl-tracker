# Stingers Silenced: Riveters Mount Third-Period Comeback to Sink Bad Bees

**TULSA, June 14, 2026** — If there is one thing I’ve learned covering this league, it’s that you never walk out of the arena until that final horn echoes off the rafters. Tonight, at a sun-drenched 3:15 p.m. puck drop, the Tulsa Riveters reminded us exactly why the third period is where legends are made—or, in the case of the Bad Bees, where heartbreak happens.

The final score read 6-4 in favor of the Riveters, but the numbers don’t capture the sheer grit displayed in a game that felt like a heavyweight bout in a phone booth.

### Period 1: Buzzing Early
The Bees came out with a point to prove. Looking to shake an 0-2-0 start to their campaign, they set the tone at the 10:29 mark with an unassisted tally that had the visitors' bench vibrating. The Riveters, however, are a composed bunch. They chipped away at the ice, patiently waiting for a seam, until Theresa Rose Rabara snapped a shot home at 3:19 to knot things up. It was a disciplined opening frame, with both goaltenders acting as brick walls, keeping the scorelines modest.

### Period 2: The Seesaw Struggle
The second frame was an absolute barn burner. The Bees retook the lead early, but the momentum shifted like a pendulum. Suzanne Hawkins dished a beauty to help the Bees double their lead, but just 51 seconds later, Gabrielle Gibson proved she has a rocket of a release, cutting the deficit to one. Every time the Bees tried to construct a fortress, the Riveters were there to pick the lock. By the time the buzzer sounded for the intermission, the ice was scarred, the goalies were sweating, and the fans were breathless.

### Period 3: The Riveters’ Rally
This is where the game went from a battle to a rout. The Riveters didn’t just turn the tide; they brought a tsunami. Theresa Rose Rabara—who was a force of nature all afternoon—found the back of the net to tie it at 10:12, and the floodgates swung wide open. 

Gabrielle Gibson took center stage, putting on a clinic in finishing. She logged two goals in the frame, including a solo effort at 4:33 that essentially took the wind out of the Bees' sails. Ivy Emerson joined the party shortly after, putting the Riveters up by three. While the Bees managed to answer back at 8:57 thanks to some hard work by Remisha Blation, the damage was already done. The Riveters’ transition game was surgical in those final fifteen minutes, turning defensive zone exits into instant offense.

### The Aftermath
For the Riveters, this 6-4 victory is a massive statement. By moving to 2-0-1, they have cemented their spot near the top of the standings, keeping pace with the league-leading Valkyries. They’ve proven they don’t just have talent; they have the internal engine to mount a comeback when the pressure is at its peak.

The Bad Bees, meanwhile, leave the arena with a heavy dose of "what if." Despite the valiant effort and a late push, they remain winless. But make no mistake: there is bite in this Bees squad. They showed flashes of elite chemistry tonight, and if they can tighten up their defensive coverage in the final frame, that first "W" is surely just around the corner.
