# Dirty Birds Take Flight in 8-3 Rout of Puckheads

If you’re a fan of high-scoring hockey and aren’t particularly attached to the concept of defensive zone coverage, Saturday morning’s tilt between the Dirty Birds and the Puckheads was exactly what the doctor ordered. In a game that saw the net bulging early and often, the Dirty Birds cruised to an 8-3 victory, proving once again that they are a force to be reckoned with in this league.

The Birds came out of the gate with all the subtlety of a freight train. James Smith opened the scoring just under a minute into the contest, setting the tone for a morning where the Puckheads spent most of their time chasing shadows. By the time the buzzer sounded for the first intermission, the Birds had already established a 3-0 lead, with Gabriel Kierath and Joel Thorson joining the party to turn the ice into their own personal playground.

"I think we caught them still finishing their morning coffee," joked one Dirty Birds skater, who requested anonymity—mostly because he didn't want to admit he was still tying his skates when Smith netted the opener.

The Puckheads showed signs of life in the second period, with Jered Pieschke and Matthew Cooper finding the back of the net to briefly interrupt the onslaught. However, every time the Puckheads managed to put a dent in the lead, the Birds seemed to have an immediate answer. Nicklas Clark buried a beautiful feed from Smith, and Triston Wilson continued his torrid scoring pace, effectively slamming the door on any hopes of a Puckheads comeback.

Wilson, in particular, was the story of the morning. With a hat trick tucked firmly under his belt, he looked like a man who hasn't missed a morning skate in years. His third-period tallies effectively turned the final ten minutes into a formality. 

For the Puckheads, it was a morning to forget. Despite some grit from the likes of William Hofmeister, who chipped in a third-period goal to go along with his assist, the team simply couldn't find an answer for the Birds’ transition game. They were caught standing still far too often, allowing the Birds to capitalize on odd-man rushes that made the goaltenders look like they were trying to stop tennis balls with a flyswatter.

With this win, the Dirty Birds continue to solidify their position near the top of the standings. They’re playing cohesive, aggressive hockey, and they’re doing it with a confidence that most teams in this league haven't mustered yet. As for the Puckheads, they’ll have to head back to the drawing board to tighten up their gaps. If they don't, their upcoming schedule is going to feel a lot longer than these last sixty minutes.

It’s only a regular-season game, and nobody’s handing out trophies in November, but the Birds are flying high, and they aren't looking down.
