# Renegades Outlast Oklahoma Precast in Sunday Morning Goal-Fest

If you like your hockey with a side of defensive discipline and structural integrity, you definitely shouldn’t have been at the rink this Sunday morning. The Renegades and Oklahoma Precast treated the early crowd to a 9-8 thriller that featured enough goals to make a soccer fan pass out from the excitement, though any local coach with a shred of dignity was probably clutching their heart by the second period.

The Renegades walked away with the win, keeping their momentum alive, but the game was less of a tactical masterclass and more of a track meet. Hunter Bemies got the party started for the Renegades early in the first, setting the tone for a game where the red light behind the net spent more time on than off.

By the second period, the scoreboard operator was likely looking for overtime pay. The middle frame was an absolute whirlwind. Oklahoma Precast’s Nicholas Power, riding a hot streak, decided to turn the game into his personal showcase, finding the back of the net twice in quick succession. When Matthew Rankin followed up with a pair of his own—both courtesy of some nifty setups from James Stuckey—it looked like Oklahoma Precast might finally find a way to climb out of their two-win hole.

But the Renegades simply refused to pack it in. Austin Tyler was the clear thorn in the side of the Precast defense, putting on a clinic in puck placement. Every time Oklahoma Precast thought they had a cushion, Tyler seemed to be there, hammering home another tally to keep the Renegades within striking distance. By the time the dust settled on the second period, the Renegades had clawed their way back into a lead that felt as fragile as a sheet of thin ice.

The third period brought a brief moment of sanity—or perhaps just exhaustion—before the Renegades clamped down. Arin Olson managed a clean strike to keep the Precast alive, but discipline issues began to mount. A string of penalties, including a frustration-fueled unsportsmanlike call against Power and a trip by Stuckey, put the Renegades in the driver’s seat. Ethan Charny capitalized on the chaos, burying two goals in the final minutes to effectively close the book on the Precast comeback.

"It was like watching a tennis match where someone forgot to put up the net," joked one Renegades skater as he trudged off the ice, dripping sweat and clearly bewildered by the final tally. 

For the Renegades, the victory keeps them firmly in second place, proving they have the firepower to trade blows with anyone in the league. For Oklahoma Precast, it’s another tough mark in the loss column, though they proved they can fill the net against anyone. They’ll head home with an 8-9 scoreline and a lot of questions for their defensive corps, while the Renegades continue their march up the standings, looking to close the gap on the undefeated Shooting Blanks. 

It wasn't pretty, it wasn't disciplined, and it certainly wasn't a goaltender's dream. But for a Sunday morning, it was exactly the kind of chaotic theater that keeps us coming back to the rink.
