# Dirty Birds Ground Havok in Double-Digit Rout

If there’s one thing they teach you in hockey, it’s that you don’t need to pack a lunch if you’re planning on getting worked this hard. The Havok found that out the hard way on Sunday night at the rink, as the Dirty Birds turned the ice into their own personal playground, skating away with an 11-2 victory in a game that turned into a full-scale dismantling.

The writing was on the wall early. By the time the first intermission rolled around, the Birds had already established a 3-0 cushion, with Triston Wilson lighting the lamp first, followed by a quick-fire pair from Nicklas Clark. If you were hoping for a Havok comeback, you clearly weren't watching the same game the rest of us were.

The second period was where the game shifted from "competitive" to "do we have enough ink on the score sheet?" The Dirty Birds poured in goal after goal, with Gabriel Kierath, Michael Bergstrom, Spencer Sutherland, and James Smith all joining the party. Clark even managed to complete a natural hat trick—or at least a reasonable facsimile of one amidst the chaos—before the buzzer sounded.

Havok managed to find a pulse late in the second, with Logan Singleton putting one home off a feed from Jered Pieschke, followed shortly by a Zack Wade unassisted effort while the Birds were shorthanded. It was a momentary flicker of light, but the Dirty Birds had already set the house on fire. 

"I think we spent more time looking at the scoreboard than the puck," said one Havok bench hand, who preferred to remain anonymous—likely out of pure embarrassment. "It's one of those nights where you just hope the Zamboni driver takes his time so you can get to the parking lot faster."

The third period was more of a formality than a contest, with Elliott Langford tacking on two more to cap off his night and push the Birds into double digits. By the time the final whistle blew, the stat sheet looked less like a hockey report and more like a grocery list for a very long winter.

The Dirty Birds proved they aren't just here to fill a roster spot; they’re here to make the rest of the league miserable. For Havok, it’s back to the drawing board. After opening their season with a tie, they’ll want to put this one in the rearview mirror as quickly as possible. As for the Birds, they’ve officially announced their arrival—and they’ve got the goal differential to prove it.
