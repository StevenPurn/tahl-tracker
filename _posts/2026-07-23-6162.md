# Yeti-Sized Avalanche: The Yetis Bury Mojo in 9-1 Shellacking

**By: "Rusty" Blades, Senior Hockey Correspondent**

If you were looking for a quiet, defensive chess match on the ice this Wednesday night, you were in the wrong building. At 7:30 PM, the rink turned into a wide-open track meet—though, let’s be honest, only one team was actually running. In what can only be described as a complete offensive monsoon, the Yetis absolutely dismantled the Mojo, skating away with a staggering 9-1 victory that had the scoreboard operator scrambling just to keep the lights on.

### The First Period: A Blizzard of Goals
The tone was set faster than a slap shot from the blue line. The Yetis came out of the gate looking like they’d been caffeinated by a grizzly bear. Before some fans even had a chance to get their concessions, Corey Favier was already finding the twine—not once, not twice, but effectively putting the Mojo on their heels immediately. Jason Fennell joined the party early, and by the time the dust settled on the opening barrage, the Mojo were staring down a deficit that felt like trying to climb a mountain in flip-flops.

The Mojo’s discipline proved to be their undoing early on. With Austin Lowry heading to the sin bin for high-sticking and Thomas White joining him shortly after for a tripping call, the Mojo spent more time navigating traffic in the penalty box than they did navigating the Yeti defense.

### The Lone Spark
Braden Trinka managed to stop the bleeding momentarily at the 15:00 mark, finding the back of the net to give the Mojo faithful something to cheer about. It was a nice individual effort, a real "put your head down and drive to the paint" type of play. But, unfortunately for the Mojo, it was like throwing a thimble of water onto a forest fire.

### The Yeti Storm
Once the Yetis smelled blood in the water, they turned the offensive zone into their own personal playground. Jason Fennell notched another one, followed by a clinical display of passing and finishing from Kirk Nielsen and Denis Blaquiere. 

The highlight reel was busy tonight. The Yetis were cycling the puck with the precision of a Swiss watch, consistently finding the soft spots in the Mojo’s coverage. Whether it was Jason Bahr setting up Blaquiere or Fennell feeding Favier for his hat-trick clincher, the Yetis were masters of the "give and go." Every time the puck touched a Yeti stick, it felt like it was destined for the top shelf where mom keeps the cookies. 

### Final Tally: A Statement Made
When the final horn sounded, the scoreboard read 9-1. It wasn’t just a win; it was a total reclamation of space in the standings. 

Entering the night, the Yetis held the #6 spot and the Mojo the #7. This drubbing is a loud, echoing statement that the Yetis aren't just satisfied with middle-of-the-pack status. With this W, the Yetis leapfrog upward, creating distance between them and the bottom tier, while the Mojo are left with some serious soul-searching to do before their next tilt.

For the Mojo, the defensive breakdowns were systemic—too much "standing around watching the snow fall" and not enough active stick-checking. For the Yetis, it’s all momentum. You don’t score nine goals by accident; you do it by playing with pace, grit, and a hunger that was on full display tonight.

The league standings are shifting, the air is getting thinner, and if tonight is any indication, the Yetis are looking to climb even higher. As for the Mojo? They’ll need to shake off the rust—and the sting of this loss—if they want to avoid falling further into the cellar.

**Final Score: Yetis 9, Mojo 1.**
