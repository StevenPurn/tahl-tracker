# The Sting is Real: Bad Bees Overwhelm Power Puck Girls 6-2

If you were looking for a masterclass in puck distribution, Tuesday night at the rink provided a stark reminder of why you don’t leave the Bad Bees’ playmakers unattended. In a 6-2 victory over the Power Puck Girls, the Bad Bees proved exactly why they are sitting pretty in second place, leaning on a relentless offensive engine that kept the spreadsheet analysts—myself included—scrambling to keep up with the primary assists.

The game began with a flurry of activity that left the Power Puck Girls’ defense looking like they were skating through wet cement. The Bees’ scoring machine ignited at the 6:22 mark of the first period, and before the Power Puck Girls could even adjust their chin straps, it was 2-0. While April Belanger managed to claw one back for the PPG at 9:44, showing signs of life for the struggling squad, the writing was effectively on the wall.

The middle frame offered a glimmer of hope for the PPG faithful. Allison Flanders found the twine at 2:34, courtesy of a crisp feed from Whitney Beeler, narrowing the gap to 2-2. For a brief, glorious stretch of the second period, the momentum pendulum had swung. But then, enter the "Suzanne Hawkins Effect." Hawkins, who seemed to have eyes in the back of her head all evening, set up Michelle Karjadi for the go-ahead goal at 5:05. It was the statistical turning point of the night; from that moment on, the Bees tightened their defensive structure, even weathering a minor turbulence in the form of a Madison Greenwood interference penalty late in the period.

"We were trying to play a controlled game, but every time I looked up, Hawkins was threading a needle," joked one frustrated Power Puck Girls defender after the buzzer. "It’s hard to build a rhythm when the Bees are effectively conducting a symphony of cross-ice passes."

The third period was an exercise in systematic dismantling. Kristin Brooks capitalized on an unassisted effort at 2:30, and the Bees’ mysterious "Unknown" forward—who must be a nightmare for opposing scouts to track—added another helper from Hawkins at 7:49. Finally, Grace Wolff put the exclamation point on the evening with an unassisted marker at 9:55, extending her impressive point streak to six games.

For the Bad Bees, the win is a statement of intent, moving them to 6-2-1 and keeping their momentum firmly in the green. They showcased a balanced attack that was simply too much for the Power Puck Girls, who fall to 2-7-1. While the Power Puck Girls have shown grit in the past, tonight they were simply out-calculated and out-paced. 

As I tucked my scorecard away, the numbers were clear: The Bees are playing high-percentage, clinical hockey, while the Power Puck Girls are left looking for a systemic reset as they try to snap their current trajectory. In this league, hockey occasionally refuses to cooperate with the spreadsheet, but tonight, the scoreboard and the stats sheet were in perfect, painful harmony.
