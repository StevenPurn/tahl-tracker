# Tulsa Murder Edge Out Gold as Ice Bronze in Mid-Table Grudge Match

If you were looking for a way to spend your Saturday afternoon, you could have done worse than catching the Tulsa Murder and Gold as Ice Bronze trade paint at the local rink. In a tilt between two teams hungry to climb out of the basement, the Tulsa Murder ultimately skated away with a 3-2 victory, leaving Gold as Ice Bronze to wonder what might have been.

The game started with the kind of frantic energy usually reserved for the last five minutes of a tie game. Pete Fleming got the scoreboard ticking for the Murder just over three minutes in, capitalizing on crisp feeds from Nicholas Olson and Andrew Foote. But Gold as Ice Bronze wasn't about to roll over. Triston Wilson, continuing his absolute tear of a season, found the back of the net at 5:45 to extend his goal streak to four games. Not long after, Joshua Smith put Gold as Ice ahead 2-1 with a tally assisted by Wilson and Trent Ohman. At that point, it felt like we were in for a high-scoring barnburner.

But hockey is a funny game—sometimes the momentum shifts faster than a Zamboni driver on a caffeine crash. After that early fireworks display, the defense tightened up, and the goalies decided to turn the crease into a "no-fly zone."

The second period belonged entirely to the Murder. Jered Pieschke knotted things up midway through the frame, and just when it looked like we were headed to the second intermission tied, Kurtis Henderson delivered a dagger. With a mere 16.6 seconds left on the clock, Henderson tickled the twine off a helper from Adam Hoffdahl to put the Murder in front for good.

The third period was a tense, gritty affair. The Murder found themselves under fire, giving up a pair of power-play opportunities to Gold as Ice Bronze due to slashing and tripping infractions. However, the Murder’s penalty kill stood tall, absorbing the pressure and shutting down every attempt to find that elusive equalizer. 

"We just kept the lanes clogged and tried not to give them any room to breathe," said one Murder bench staffer after the buzzer, likely referring to the defensive wall they built in those final minutes.

For Gold as Ice Bronze, it’s a frustrating result, particularly after seeing Wilson extend his streak, but they’ll have to regroup. For the Tulsa Murder, the two points are a welcome shot in the arm as they look to distance themselves from the bottom tier of the standings. It wasn't the prettiest game of the year, but in a league this tight, a win is a win—and the Murder will happily take the walk home with the victory in their pockets.
