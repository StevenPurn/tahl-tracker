# A Total Eclipse of the Reapers: Outsiders Silence the Competition 7-0

If you showed up to the rink late on Tuesday night, you might have thought the scoreboard was busted. I checked it three times myself, but no, those zeros for the Reapers were as real as the chilly draft coming off the ice. The Outsiders, currently sitting pretty at the top of the standings, turned in a masterclass performance, handing the Reapers a 7-0 shutout that was about as one-sided as a seesaw with a grizzly bear on one end and a hamster on the other.

The Outsiders came into this one riding high on a winning streak, and they didn’t show a shred of mercy. The Reapers started their night on the back foot when Lucas Jameson took a delay-of-game penalty early in the first. The Outsiders spent the better part of the evening turning the Reapers' defensive zone into their own personal playground. Juan Padilla was the undisputed architect of the chaos, kicking things off with an opening goal at the 8:45 mark of the first period, assisted by the ever-reliable Tyler Waller.

By the second period, the ice was tilted so far in the Outsiders' favor that I’m surprised the Reapers didn’t have to climb uphill just to get to their own bench. Tyler Waller, who seems to be playing chess while everyone else is playing checkers, notched a goal of his own at 3:33, followed quickly by a solo effort from Tyler Holmes just a minute later. You could practically hear the collective sigh from the Reapers' bench—the wind had been taken completely out of their sails.

The third period was simply an exercise in dominance. Brett Lamb kept the rhythm going, followed by another beauty from Padilla, who was clearly having the night of his life. Justice Dick joined the scoring parade with under a minute left in the frame, and before the ice could even settle, Padilla hammered the final nail into the coffin with his third goal of the game at 6:45. 

Timothy Willis was the unsung hero of the night, quietly collecting three assists and acting as the engine room for the Outsiders' transition game. Meanwhile, the Reapers just couldn't catch a break; penalties to Denisse Solorio-Nateras and Stephen Jeffery only served to invite the Outsiders to keep their foot on the gas.

"We just couldn't find the handle on the puck tonight," a weary-looking Reapers player muttered near the locker room exit. "It felt like they had six guys on the ice, and we were skating through wet cement."

With this result, the Outsiders continue to stake their claim as the team to beat in this regular season. They’re playing a brand of hockey that is as disciplined as it is lethal. As for the Reapers, they’ll need to regroup quickly and find a way to shake off this shutout if they want to stay relevant in the upper half of the standings. Tonight, though, the Outsiders proved that when they’re clicking, they aren’t just winning games—they’re making a statement.
