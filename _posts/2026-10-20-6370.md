# Renegades Outlast Precast in Eight-Goal Offensive Frenzy

In a matchup that looked more like a track meet than a structured hockey game, the Renegades defended their perch atop the standings with a chaotic 8-6 victory over Oklahoma Precast on Monday night. While the score sheet suggests a defensive breakdown of epic proportions, for the casual observer, it was a masterclass in opportunistic transition play.

The Renegades entered the contest holding a slim lead in the standings, and they wasted little time asserting their rhythm. Hunter Bemies drew first blood at the 4:11 mark of the first period, followed quickly by a Caeleb Burch strike just over two minutes later. While Oklahoma Precast’s Michael Bergstrom managed to narrow the gap before the intermission, the flow of the game was clearly tilted toward the Renegades’ aggressive forecheck.

However, if you enjoy momentum swings that defy logic, the second period was your personal playground. Trailing by two, Oklahoma Precast’s Matthew Rankin embarked on a personal mission, netting two goals in just over a minute of play to level the score at 3-3. The momentum was palpable, but in this league, momentum is often as fragile as a composite stick blade.

The middle frame devolved into a dizzying sequence of scoring that saw the teams trade goals like baseball cards. Joseph Wilson proved to be the Renegades' primary engine during this stretch, completing a hat trick that effectively broke the Precast's resistance. Wilson’s back-to-back goals late in the second, supplemented by a tally from Eli Augustine and a second goal from Burch, pushed the Renegades to a commanding lead.

"I’m not sure we played defense as much as we just agreed to participate in a high-speed collision sport," joked one anonymous Renegade teammate after the buzzer. "When the puck is moving that fast, you just stop checking the math and start checking the opponent."

Oklahoma Precast did manage to claw back in the third, with Zack Wade and Joseph Pachuta providing a late spark. But the mountain proved too steep to climb, and Caeleb Burch effectively iced the contest with his hat-trick-completing goal at the 9:26 mark of the final period, thwarting any hopes of a dramatic Precast comeback.

From a statistical perspective, the Renegades' ability to capitalize on their transition opportunities—particularly the playmaking of Logan Singleton—proved to be the deciding variable. While the Precast displayed resilience, specifically through the multi-point efforts of Nicholas Power and Michael Bergstrom, their defensive zone coverage struggled to contain the Renegades’ top-six rotation.

With this win, the Renegades solidify their status as the team to beat, improving their standing as they maintain their hold on the top spot. Oklahoma Precast, meanwhile, remains in the hunt, but will surely be looking to tighten their defensive structure before their next outing. In a game where 14 total goals were scored, the only certainty is that the scorekeepers earned their keep.
