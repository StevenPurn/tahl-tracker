# Valkyries Surge Past Power Puck Girls in Late-Period Scoring Frenzy

If you look at the raw data of Sunday morning’s matchup between The Valkyries and the Power Puck Girls, you might be tempted to call it a defensive slog for two periods. But if you were sitting in the chilly confines of the arena at 11:45 a.m., you knew the game was merely a pressure cooker waiting for the safety valve to fail. Ultimately, The Valkyries leveraged a blistering third-period explosion to secure a 4-2 victory, moving themselves deeper into the top tier of the standings.

For the first forty minutes, the game was a masterclass in stagnation. Makenzie Giove opened the scoring for The Valkyries at 11:36 of the first period, unassisted, proving that sometimes the best offensive strategy is just finding the back of the net by yourself. However, the Power Puck Girls—staring down a losing streak—refused to yield. With just 36 seconds left in the opening frame, an unnamed Power Puck skater buried a feed from Kristin Brooks to tie the game at 1-1, sending both teams into a tactical stalemate that lasted well into the third period.

The turning point was not a strategy change, but a sheer force of will from the Valkyries' top line. Between the 10:38 and 12:19 marks of the final period, the game transformed from a methodical chess match into a chaotic spreadsheet-wrecker.

Haley Griffin became the architect of the Power Puck Girls' undoing, netting two goals in just 81 seconds. The first, assisted by Emily Adamic and Giove, broke the deadlock. Before the opposition could catch their breath, Griffin struck again, this time converting a sequence orchestrated by Adamic and Jessica Hopwood. Only 20 seconds later, Cynthia Rozell punctuated the outburst with her own goal—again assisted by the tireless Adamic—stretching the lead to 4-1.

"I’ve spent the better part of the morning trying to find a defensive explanation for that ninety-second window," noted one frustrated league observer on the sidelines. "There isn't one. It was just a clinical execution of high-danger scoring chances."

To their credit, the Power Puck Girls clawed one back at the 5:10 mark when an unnamed skater notched her second assist of the game by finding the back of the net on a helper from the ever-present Kristin Brooks. But the damage had been done. The Valkyries’ goaltending tightened up in the closing minutes, refusing to allow the Power Puck Girls the space to manufacture a miracle comeback.

The win is a significant momentum builder for The Valkyries, who remain undefeated in regulation time this season (1-0-2). For the Power Puck Girls, the loss is a tough pill to swallow after they showed grit in the early stages, but they will need to address their defensive coverage during that disastrous three-goal third-period stretch if they hope to climb out of the bottom half of the table.

As for the stats sheet? It’s a beautiful, lopsided mess—just the way a hockey purist likes it.
