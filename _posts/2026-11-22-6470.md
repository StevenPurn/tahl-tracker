# Puckedelics Snap Bone Zone’s Streak in Seven-Goal Thriller

In a clash between the league’s top two heavyweights, the numbers suggested a tactical standoff. Instead, the 8:15 a.m. matchup on November 22 devolved into a glorious, high-octane track meet that left the league’s most dominant defensive unit—The Bone Zone—stunned. By the time the dust settled, the Puckedelics had emerged with a 7-6 shootout victory, handing the league leaders their first loss of the season.

The Bone Zone entered the contest boasting an unblemished 7-0-1 record, and for the first 11 minutes, they looked the part. Jeffrey Greco opened the scoring off a precision setup from Jerry Bates and Garrett Williams. However, the momentum shifted violently in the final minute of the opening period. Brett Mooney’s equalizer at the 36.9-second mark proved to be a harbinger of the chaos to come. 

While a tripping penalty to James DePriest offered the Bone Zone a lifeline, it was the Puckedelics who seized control, scoring shorthanded courtesy of David Lawrence. Kirk Nielsen, clearly deciding that the back of the net was his personal residence, tacked on another before the intermission, giving the Puckedelics a 3-1 lead that forced the Bone Zone into an uncharacteristic state of disarray.

The second period showcased the sheer offensive volatility of Garrett Williams. The veteran went on a tear, scoring back-to-back goals to tie the game before eventually finding the equalizer again in the third. Williams, riding a four-game point streak into the morning, was effectively a one-man wrecking crew. Yet, every time the Bone Zone clawed back, Kirk Nielsen was there to push the Puckedelics back into the lead. 

Nielsen’s performance was nothing short of historic by recreational standards. With three goals in regulation and the eventual game-deciding tally in the shootout, he effectively nullified the Bone Zone’s chemistry. "I stopped looking at the scoreboard and just started looking for Nielsen," admitted one Bone Zone defender during the post-game intermission. "It’s hard to stick to a defensive system when the other guy is playing a game of 'anything you can do, I can do better.'"

The third period was a frantic back-and-forth affair, with Williams netting his fourth goal of the game with only 40.1 seconds remaining to force the shootout. It was a statistical anomaly: Williams recorded four goals in the game, yet it was still not enough to secure the two points. 

The shootout ended fittingly with Nielsen, the game’s primary disruptor, sliding the puck home to seal the 7-6 win. 

For the Bone Zone, the loss is a rare blemish on an otherwise perfect campaign. They remain at the top of the standings with 15 points, but the Puckedelics (now 6-1-2) have narrowed the gap to a single point. If this morning’s exhibition was any indication, the race for the top seed is no longer a coronation for the Bone Zone, but a genuine fight to the finish. As for the Puckedelics, they’ve proven that even the most impenetrable defensive schemes have a breaking point—usually occurring right around 8:15 a.m. on a Saturday.
