# A Statistical Shellacking: Bar Down Boys Dismantle Honey Badgers 13-0

In the world of recreational hockey, we often talk about "parity" and "parity-driven outcomes." Tonight, the spreadsheet did not just suggest parity was absent; it caught fire, curled into a ball, and wept. The Bar Down Boys dominated the Honey Badgers in a 13-0 victory that felt less like a regulation hockey game and more like a controlled demolition of the league’s bottom-ranked squad.

Coming into the contest, the Bar Down Boys were riding a solid wave of momentum with a 2-0-2 record, while the Honey Badgers were searching for a spark to jumpstart a winless campaign. By the time the final buzzer echoed through the rink, the Badgers were likely wishing they had stayed in the locker room to watch game tape instead.

The statistical onslaught began early. Jeffrey Greco acted as the primary architect of the destruction, netting an astronomical four goals on the night. His first strike at the 5:17 mark of the first period set the tone, and from there, the floodgates didn't just open—they were ripped off their hinges. Greco’s vision was on full display as he added two assists to his four-goal haul, proving that his involvement in the offensive zone was essentially a prerequisite for a goal being scored.

The second period was a masterclass in efficiency for the Bar Down Boys, who found the back of the net seven times. It was a dizzying display of transition play. Mark Hodges proved to be an absolute menace, recording two goals and two assists, often finding space in the slot that was left inexplicably vacant by the Badgers' defensive rotation. By the time Tyler Wimpee tapped one in at 9:49 of the second, it was clear that the Honey Badgers’ goaltending was facing a firing squad.

"I tried to tell the boys to focus on the forecheck, but honestly, it was like trying to stop a runaway train with a handful of streamers," noted one Honey Badgers defender, who requested anonymity to preserve his remaining dignity.

The third period saw the Bar Down Boys maintain their composure, adding three more tallies. Even the penalties—a pair of roughing calls at the 10:10 mark involving Jared Sewell and Benjamin Gartner—couldn't disrupt the rhythm of the Bar Down offense. Emil Hinkefent and Kirk Nielsen rounded out the scoring, ensuring that the 13-0 scoreline accurately reflected a game where one team seemed to be playing on a different planet.

Statistically speaking, this was a night of anomalies. To record thirteen goals without a single reply is rare; to do so with such a spread of contributors—Greco, Hodges, Wimpee, Hinkefent, Williams, and Nielsen all finding the scoresheet—suggests a level of depth that makes the Bar Down Boys a legitimate threat in the top tier of our standings.

For the Honey Badgers, the focus now shifts to damage control and regrouping. Currently sitting at 0-4-1, the team needs to find a way to tighten their defensive gaps and regain some confidence. Meanwhile, the Bar Down Boys march forward, sitting comfortably in second place and looking like a team that has finally figured out how to translate skill into sheer, overwhelming production.

If you’re a fan of high-octane offense, tonight was a treat. If you’re a fan of balanced, defensive hockey, tonight was a cautionary tale in what happens when the scouting report is ignored and the puck possession metrics go completely off the rails.
