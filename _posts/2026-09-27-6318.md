# Puckheads Explode for Eight in Dominant Rout of Havok

If there is one thing a spreadsheet enthusiast hates, it is a low-sample-size anomaly. Entering Saturday’s tilt at the rink, the Puckheads and Havok were both desperate to shake off sluggish starts to their respective campaigns. By the time the final buzzer mercifully sounded on an 8-3 Puckheads victory, the "anomaly" was firmly replaced by a masterclass in offensive efficiency.

The Puckheads, who sat at 0-1-1 heading into the weekend, clearly decided that puck possession was merely a suggestion rather than a strategy. The tone was set early by Gregory Douglas, who kicked off the scoring at 11:53 of the first period. Douglas, who would eventually finish the afternoon with a mesmerizing stat line of three goals and two assists, played with the kind of clinical precision that makes an analytics scout weep with joy.

The first period saw the Puckheads demonstrate a frustrating habit of putting themselves in the box, with Cameron Sartin serving a trip at 6:47. However, the short-handed unit proved resilient, and Sartin—perhaps motivated by a desire to atone for his earlier transgression—scored a goal of his own just moments after returning to the ice at 8:05, assisted by the ever-present Douglas.

"We talked about puck placement and high-danger zones," said a Puckheads spokesperson, who requested to remain anonymous, likely because they were still trying to catch their breath. "Or maybe we just talked about shooting at the net. Honestly, at 8-3, the philosophy becomes secondary to the scoreboard."

Havok attempted a push in the second period, with Dominic Najjar finding the back of the net at 11:38, followed by a gritty solo effort from Rocky Ricci at 1:46. But for every step Havok took, the Puckheads were there to shove them back. Richard Retkowski continued his point streak with a goal at 2:29, ensuring that whenever Havok showed a pulse, the Puckheads delivered a cardiac monitor-flatlining response.

The third period turned into a full-scale offensive barrage. The Puckheads tacked on three more goals—courtesy of William Hofmeister, another from the relentless Douglas, and a second tally for Retkowski—to turn a competitive game into an absolute rout. Michael Bergstrom managed a consolation goal for Havok at 9:18, but the damage was already irreparable. 

From a tactical standpoint, the Puckheads’ top line was nothing short of a statistical nightmare for the Havok defense. With Douglas securing a five-point outing and Cameron Sartin facilitating the offense, the Puckheads finally secured their first win of the season, moving them out of the basement of the standings. 

For Havok, a 0-3-1 record suggests it is time to go back to the drawing board—or perhaps, in this case, back to the whiteboard to figure out why the defensive coverage broke down with such rhythmic consistency. As the Puckheads celebrate their first win, the rest of the league would be wise to take note: when the Puckheads stop playing for the spreadsheet and start playing for the back of the net, they are a formidable force indeed.
