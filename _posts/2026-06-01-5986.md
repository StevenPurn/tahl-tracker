# A Total Fowl Play: Dirty Birds Ground the Honey Badgers in 10-3 Rout

**By: The Rinkside Reporter**
**Date: May 31, 2026**

If you were looking for a quiet Friday night at the rink, you clearly didn't check the schedule. What we witnessed tonight at the 8:45 PM puck drop was less of a hockey game and more of a full-scale avian uprising. The Dirty Birds came out looking like they’d been sharpening their skates on a whetstone all week, dismantling the Honey Badgers in a 10-3 barn burner that left the visitors looking for a hole to hibernate in.

### First Period: Taking Flight
The Dirty Birds didn’t just knock on the door; they kicked it down. It started at 10:15 of the first when Josh Moyer found the back of the net off a dish from Dane Bier. Just 13 seconds later—before the Honey Badgers could even catch their breath—Nicklas Clark doubled the lead. By the time Gabriel Kierath tucked one home with a minute left in the period, the Birds were flying high with a 3-0 cushion, effectively sucking the oxygen right out of the Badgers' bench.

### Second Period: The Smith and Clark Show
The middle frame turned into a highlight reel. Josh Moyer grabbed his second of the night at 11:45, but the Badgers finally showed some pluck. Mathew Sproles, playing with the heart of a lion, managed to solve the Birds' netminder twice in the period. 

However, the Dirty Birds were relentless. James Smith was the conductor of this orchestra, putting on a clinic with vision that would make a scout weep. Smith notched two goals in the second, effectively burying any hopes of a Badger comeback. The chemistry between Smith and Clark was palpable—they were moving the puck like it was on a string, consistently finding the soft spots in the Badgers' defensive coverage.

### Third Period: Pouring it On
Leading 6-2 entering the final stanza, you might have expected the Birds to coast. Instead, they hit the gas. Steven Purn joined the scoring party, followed by a late-game offensive explosion from Nicklas Clark, who secured a hat trick (and then some) with two quick tallies. 

Mathew Sproles managed to complete his own hat trick at 9:26 of the third, a valiant individual effort, but it was essentially a footnote in a game dominated by the Birds’ collective firepower. When the final horn sounded, the scoreboard read 10-3, a lopsided result that will force the Honey Badgers to go back to the drawing board.

### Key Takeaways
For the **Dirty Birds**, this was a statement win. Their transition game was crisp, their forecheck was stifling, and they consistently forced "traffic in front of the net," making life miserable for the Honey Badgers' defense. Nicklas Clark, already riding a goal streak coming into tonight, looked unstoppable, effectively turning the offensive zone into his own personal playground.

For the **Honey Badgers**, it’s a long bus ride home. While Mathew Sproles provided a silver lining with a gritty three-goal performance, the team struggled to contain the Birds’ speed through the neutral zone. Entering the season at 0-2, the Badgers need to tighten up their defensive structure before the next drop of the puck.

As we look at the early-season landscape, the Dirty Birds have served notice: they aren’t just here to participate; they’re here to ruffle some feathers. If they keep burying pucks "top shelf where mom keeps the cookies" like they did tonight, they’re going to be a tough bird to pluck all season long.
