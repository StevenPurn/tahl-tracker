# Ice Breakers Put on a Scoring Clinic in 9-0 Rout of Riders

If you were looking for a quiet Friday night at the rink, you certainly didn’t find it at the barn on November 1st. The Ice Breakers looked like they were auditioning for a spot on a highlight reel, skating circles around the Rohan Riders to the tune of a 9-0 final score that honestly felt like it could have reached double digits if the scoreboard had enough room.

From the opening faceoff, the Ice Breakers played with the kind of cohesion that usually requires a few more months of practice. The floodgates didn't just open; they were ripped off their hinges early in the first period. Within the span of a frantic few minutes, Luke Morley and Chris Jorgensen began a masterclass in offensive zone pressure that left the Riders’ defense looking like they were trying to catch smoke with a pair of mittens. 

Morley got the party started at 4:50, followed almost immediately by Jorgensen and David Hardy. Before the popcorn was even finished in the stands, the Breakers had tacked on another from Morley and a power-play beauty from Jorgensen to head into the break with a commanding 5-0 lead. It was a cold night for the Riders, and I don't just mean the temperature on the ice.

The second period saw more of the same, though the Riders tried to inject a bit of grit into the proceedings. Shawn Ward spent a little too much time in the box for the Breakers—serving penalties for roughing and interference—but the Riders just couldn’t find the back of the net to capitalize on the man advantage. Meanwhile, Justice Dick continued his scorching form, extending his goal streak to four games with a tally at 12:34, assisted by the relentless Luke Morley. Morley and Jorgensen weren't finished either, each adding another notch to their belts before the period closed out at 8-0.

By the time Nathan Klassen slammed home the ninth goal of the night early in the third, the Ice Breakers were essentially just keeping their legs moving. Riders captain Jordan White tried to keep spirits high, but a high-sticking penalty late in the game was emblematic of a frustrated squad struggling to find its footing.

It was a tough night for the Riders, who remain winless and are clearly looking for a reset button. For the Ice Breakers, however, the message is clear: they are a legitimate force in this league. With Chris Jorgensen now boasting a staggering six-game goal streak and the team’s recent form trending sharply upward, the rest of the standings should probably take notice. The Breakers aren't just winning; they’re turning the rink into their personal playground. If they keep playing like this, the league’s top two teams better be checking their rearview mirrors.
