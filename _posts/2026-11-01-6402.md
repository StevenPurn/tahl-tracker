# The Bone Zone Keeps Rolling, Bumps Mooseknuckles Aside 5-2

If you were looking for a masterclass in controlled chaos, you probably shouldn’t have been at the rink this Saturday afternoon. But if you wanted to see The Bone Zone keep their winning streak alive while the Mooseknuckles struggled to find their rhythm, then you got exactly what you paid for. The Bone Zone walked away with a convincing 5-2 victory, proving once again that they are currently the team to beat in this league.

The Bone Zone drew first blood at the 4:55 mark of the opening period when Cody Kern found the back of the net off a dish from Jerry Bates. It was a simple play, executed with the kind of efficiency that makes you wonder if these guys actually practice, or if they just have a better sense of where the net is than the rest of us. The Mooseknuckles didn’t help their cause early on, with Steven Belanger heading to the box for playing with a broken stick—a move that’s generally frowned upon unless you’re trying to audition for a slapstick comedy routine.

The second period was where the wheels really started to wobble for the Mooseknuckles. Garrett Williams and Tyler Wimpee extended the lead to 3-0, effectively silencing the Mooseknuckles’ bench. Just when it looked like the game might turn into a blowout, Steven Belanger atoned for his earlier equipment blunder by potting one at 3:53, finally putting the Mooseknuckles on the board.

"We were chasing shadows for the first twenty," noted one Mooseknuckles skater, who wisely chose to remain anonymous while peeling off his sweat-soaked jersey. "It's hard to catch up when you're busy rearranging your defensive zone every three minutes."

The third period brought a flurry of activity, headlined by Jerry Bates, who had himself an afternoon. Bates logged two goals and an assist, essentially acting as the architect of the Mooseknuckles' misery. While Benjamin Savory managed to chip one in for the Mooseknuckles late in the frame thanks to a feed from James Rozell, the hole was already too deep to climb out of. 

The physical intensity didn’t wane even as the clock ticked down. Tyler Wimpee, likely feeling a bit too much "team spirit," picked up a 10-minute misconduct for unsportsmanlike conduct in the waning moments, giving the officials a chance to flex their muscles.

For The Bone Zone, this win pushes their stellar start to 5-0-1, keeping them firmly planted in the second spot in the standings and extending their winning streak. They look like a team that knows their system, trusts their teammates, and rarely beats themselves. As for the Mooseknuckles, they’ll look to shake off the loss and regain the form that put them in the middle of the pack. 

It was a hard-fought afternoon of recreational hockey—some good goals, a few questionable decisions, and enough sweat to fill a Zamboni tank. Same time next week, folks. Don't forget to sharpen your skates.
