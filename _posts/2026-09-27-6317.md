# Precast Pummels Renegades in 8-3 Rout

If you wandered into the rink expecting the Renegades to flex their first-place muscle on Saturday afternoon, you were likely holding your breath—and not because of the crisp, cold air. In a game that saw Oklahoma Precast shake off their early-season sluggishness, the third-place squad dismantled the league-leading Renegades with a decisive 8-3 victory that left the scoreboard operator working overtime.

The game started with a flurry of activity, as the mysterious skater known only as "Not Signed In" opened the scoring just 16 seconds into the tilt, courtesy of a tidy feed from Bret Kurth. The Renegades managed a pulse shortly after when Elliott Langford leveled the score, but that was essentially the last time the Renegades looked comfortable. 

Kurth, who turned in a performance that would make any old-time coach weep with joy, answered the Renegades’ goal with a short-handed tally midway through the opening frame. Assisted by Matthew Rankin and a redeemed Nicholas Power—who had just stepped out of the box after a tripping minor—Kurth’s goal shifted the momentum firmly into the Precast corner.

The second period was essentially a masterclass in puck management by Oklahoma. Power extended the lead early in the frame, and even after a roughing penalty put them on the back foot, Kurth doubled down with an unassisted marker while playing short-handed. By the time Andrew Doepp found the back of the net just before the second intermission, the Renegades looked less like a first-place team and more like a group of guys who’d spent the morning moving heavy furniture.

"I’m not sure what the pre-game meal was over on their side," joked one observer in the stands, "but whatever it was, it didn't include legs."

The third period was more of the same. Kurth completed his dominance with another goal, and "Not Signed In" continued to haunt the Renegades' goaltender, adding two more tallies to secure a hat-trick-plus-one effort. While the Renegades managed to string together a pair of late-game goals from the anonymous skater and Calvin Baker, the effort was too little, too late.

The result is a major statement for Oklahoma Precast, who entered the day with only a single point to their name and now find themselves looking like the most dangerous team on the ice. For the Renegades, it’s back to the drawing board to figure out how a team that was atop the standings allowed eight goals in a single outing. 

As for the fans, they got exactly what they paid for: a reminder that in this league, the standings are just numbers on a page, and on any given Saturday, a team can either catch fire or get left out in the cold.
