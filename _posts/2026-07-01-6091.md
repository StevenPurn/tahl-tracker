# WENDIGOS SNAP THE SLIDE IN THRILLING SHOOTOUT VICTORY OVER SYNDICATE

**By: "Rinkside" Rickey, Lead Beat Reporter**

**June 30, 2026** — If you’ve spent any time at the rink this season, you know the atmosphere has been a little heavy in the basement of the standings. Entering tonight’s contest, the Wendigos and The Syndicate were both sporting identical 0-4-0 records, desperate to find a spark. Well, folks, the ice was on fire tonight, and when the dust settled, the Wendigos finally tasted glory in a 4-3 thriller that went the distance.

### First Period: A Frenetic Start
If you blinked, you missed the momentum shifting like a pinball machine. The Syndicate drew first blood at 10:26 when Brett Mecham capitalized on a feed from Michael Marshall. But the Wendigos—a team that hasn't had much to cheer about lately—answered back at 13:05 with Taylor Chinowth reminding everyone why he’s on a scoring heater, burying an unassisted beauty.

The Syndicate wasn't going to roll over, though. Marshall turned from playmaker to finisher at 14:41, and Logan Pardue padded the lead to 3-1 before the buzzer. It looked like the Wendigos were staring down another long night, but give them credit: they didn't fold.

### Second Period: The Grinding Gear
The second frame was all about the "don't let them breathe" mentality. The Syndicate’s Brandon Johnson found himself in the sin bin twice, but the Wendigos couldn’t quite capitalize on the man-advantage as the defensive lanes stayed as clogged as a highway during rush hour. 

Just when it seemed the score would remain locked, Joshua Copeland decided he’d had enough. With a mere 21.7 seconds left on the clock, he found the back of the net, breathing life into his squad and trimming the deficit to 3-2 heading into the final intermission. That late-period goal? That’s the kind of momentum that changes seasons.

### Third Period: Grit, Grime, and Redemption
The third was a cage match. At 10:05, Aden Ingram played the hero, knotting the game at 3-3 off a picture-perfect setup from Steven Jordan and Dickie Williams. 

The closing minutes were pure chaos. The refs were busy, with a flurry of penalties—Interference, Roughing, High-Sticking—turning the ice into a penalty-box hotel. Both benches were chirping, the fans were on their feet, and the goalies were standing on their heads to ensure no one broke the deadlock. We headed to the shootout, the ultimate test of nerves.

### The Shootout: Ice in the Veins
The shootout was a microcosm of the season: tension, precision, and high stakes. Taylor Chinowth continued his personal vendetta against the opposing goalie, tucking his shot home to start things off. Brett Mecham kept The Syndicate alive with a clutch response. But it was Aden Ingram, the man who brought the Wendigos back into the game in the third, who stepped up to seal the deal with the deciding goal.

### The Aftermath
For the Wendigos, this isn't just a win—it's the first point(s) of their campaign. They finally climbed out of the 0-4-0 basement, signaling to the rest of the league that the "Wendigo" is no longer hibernating. 

As for The Syndicate, they walk away with a tough loss, but they proved they have the firepower to compete. Both teams have a long road ahead to climb out of the bottom tier, but tonight, both proved that in this league, the gap between "winless" and "winner" is often just one bounce of the puck.

**Final Score:** Wendigos 4, The Syndicate 3 (SO)
