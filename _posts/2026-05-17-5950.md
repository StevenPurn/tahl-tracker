# Murder on the Ice: Tulsa Orchestrates a 7-1 Masterclass Over Mojo

**TULSA** – If you were looking for a quiet Sunday afternoon at the rink, you clearly took a wrong turn at the parking lot. In a game that saw the Tulsa Murder playing with the kind of clinical precision that would make a surgeon blush, our hometown boys absolutely dismantled the Mojo 7-1 this afternoon. 

With the ice still cooling off, one thing is clear: The Murder aren't just here to participate; they’re here to liquidate the competition.

### Period 1: The Hodges Show
The game started with a crisp, high-tempo feel, but it was Mark Hodges who blew the doors off early. At the 6:56 mark, Hodges took matters into his own hands. No assists, no fluff—just pure grit and a heavy wrister that set the tone for the afternoon. It was a statement goal that signaled to the Mojo bench that they were in for a long, cold day in the trenches.

### Period 2: A Penalty-Filled Slugfest
The middle frame was absolute chaos. It started with Jeffrey Pruitt finding himself in the sin bin for high-sticking, giving Mojo a glimmer of hope. But the Murder’s penalty kill was tighter than a new pair of skates. 

As soon as the teams returned to even strength, the floodgates opened. Tom Mortimer hammered one home at 12:47 to extend the lead, followed by a beautiful display of chemistry when Mark Hodges dished a perfect feed to Pete Fleming, who buried it with only 38 seconds remaining in the period.

Mojo did manage to find a pulse when Bradley Fidler squeezed one past the Murder netminder at 6:36, but the momentum was short-lived. Just two minutes later, Jeffrey Pruitt redeemed his earlier trip to the penalty box by setting up Adam Hoffdahl, restoring the three-goal cushion and effectively sucking the oxygen out of the Mojo locker room.

### Period 3: Putting the Game on Ice
Leading 4-1 heading into the final frame, the Murder could have shifted into cruise control. Instead, they decided to step on the gas. 

Jarred Butler kicked off the third-period party at 13:37, finishing off a slick tic-tac-toe play from Adam Hoffdahl and Kurtis Henderson. From there, it was the Jeffrey Pruitt show. Pruitt, looking like he was playing against traffic cones, notched two consecutive goals—one at 5:24 off a Henderson feed, and another at 9:59 set up by Butler—to put this one firmly in the "rout" category. 

By the time the final horn sounded, the scoreboard read 7-1, and the Mojo looked like they were ready for the bus ride home to be as long as possible.

### The Aftermath
For the Murder, this win is a massive injection of confidence, moving them to a comfortable 4 points in the standings. They showcased a balanced attack today—everyone from the blueline to the crease was humming in perfect harmony. 

As for Mojo, the 0-2 start is a bitter pill to swallow. They’ll need to head back to the drawing board and find a way to shore up their defensive zone coverage before their next outing, or they risk falling behind the pack early in the season.

It’s a great day to be a Tulsa hockey fan, folks. The Murder are loud, they’re fast, and right now, they’re looking like the most dangerous team on the ice. Keep your sticks on the ice and your eyes on this squad—if today was any indication, there are plenty more celebrations to come.
