# Jackalopes Break the Ice with Shutout Win Over Tulsa Murder

If you’ve been sweating the standings lately, you weren't alone. The Jackalopes and the Tulsa Murder hit the ice this afternoon both desperate to shed that "0" in the win column. By the time the final buzzer echoed through the rink, the Jackalopes had finally found their rhythm, skating away with a clean 2-0 victory that felt every bit as hard-fought as the scoreboard suggests.

For the better part of this game, it was a classic barn-burner of missed opportunities and stone-cold goaltending. Both squads looked like they were allergic to the back of the net until the second period, when the Jackalopes finally cracked the code at the 11:20 mark. James Whitacre was the man of the hour, burying the biscuit past the keeper off a crisp setup from Christopher King and Robert Verity. It was the kind of goal that lets a team finally exhale, though the Murder weren’t about to just roll over.

The middle frame took a turn toward the rugged side when Tyler Wick sent a Tulsa player sprawling with a trip at 8:41. The Jackalopes' penalty kill unit, however, looked like they had been chugging industrial-strength espresso all morning, keeping the Murder’s power play completely handcuffed.

The drama spilled over into the third period, which saw enough whistles to keep the refs busy for a week. Kyle Foster doubled the lead at 10:03, taking matters into his own hands with an unassisted beauty that proved to be the final nail in the coffin. Moments later, the tempers started to simmer, with a flurry of penalties—including a roughing call on Whitacre and some extracurricular shoving that saw Wick and Tulsa's Kurtis Henderson sent to the box in synchronized fashion at 4:53—that kept the tension dialed to an eleven.

Despite the parade to the penalty box, the Jackalopes stayed composed. Their defensive structure remained tighter than a brand-new pair of skates, effectively bottling up the Tulsa Murder every time they threatened to make a run at the lead. Even when Verity found himself in the box for tripping at 8:01, the Jackalopes’ defense stood tall, refusing to yield an inch.

"We just stopped overthinking it," said one Jackalopes player while unlacing his skates after the match. "Sometimes you just have to stop looking for the highlight reel and just put the puck where it needs to be. Today, it landed in the net twice."

With this win, the Jackalopes finally climb out of the basement of the standings, leaving the Tulsa Murder searching for answers as they look to shake off their current slump. It wasn't always pretty, and it certainly wasn't quiet, but for the Jackalopes, this 2-0 victory is exactly the kind of momentum builder they needed to get their season back on the rails.
