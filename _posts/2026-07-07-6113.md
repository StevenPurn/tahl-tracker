# Knights Rally Late to Stun Mojo in 6-5 Barn Burner

**TULSA** – If you were looking for a quiet, defensive stalemate on a humid July night, you were in the wrong building. The 10:15 p.m. puck drop between the Knights and the Mojo provided exactly what we crave in this league: enough end-to-end action to make your head spin and enough heart-stopping drama to leave the late-night crowd buzzing well into the early morning.

In a clash that defied the standings, the 11th-place Knights (2-4-0) looked like a team possessed, handing the 4th-place Mojo (3-3-0) a stinging 6-5 loss in an absolute thriller.

### Period 1: Mojo Finds Their Groove
The Mojo came out of the gate looking like they wanted to end things early. Tanner Bemeis opened the scoring just 1:01 into the contest, catching the Knights flat-footed. By the 5:46 mark, Kyle White extended the Mojo lead to 2-0, burying a dish from Corey Phillips. The Knights spent most of the frame chasing shadows, heading into the locker room down by a pair and seemingly headed for another long night.

### Period 2: The Momentum Pendulum Swings
The second period, however, was a different animal entirely. Travis Miller sparked the Knights’ engine at the 10:59 mark, courtesy of a slick feed from Matthew Rudick. 

The middle frame turned into a track meet. When Andrew Armendariz restored the Mojo’s two-goal cushion, it felt like the air might leave the Knights’ sails. Instead, Jered Pieschke answered back a mere seven seconds later. Not to be outdone, Rudick took matters into his own hands at the 7:03 mark, knotting the game at 3-3. The Knights’ bench, previously stagnant, was suddenly alive with the sound of chatter and the smell of a comeback.

### Period 3: A Wild West Shootout
If the second period was a track meet, the third was a full-blown street brawl—on ice. 

Doug Lowry reclaimed the lead for Mojo early, but the Knights refused to bow out. Erik Chillingworth and Eron Richards traded tallies in quick succession, vaulting the Knights into their first lead of the night at 5-4. When Austin Lowry tied it back up for Mojo with just over five minutes remaining, the tension in the rink was thick enough to cut with a skate blade.

The hero of the night? Travis Miller. With time ticking down and the game hanging in the balance, Miller notched his second goal of the night—his fourth point of the evening—to put the Knights up 6-5. Despite a late interference penalty against Kyle White that gave the Knights a power play, the score held.

### The Aftermath
For the Knights, this victory is a massive injection of adrenaline. They showed the kind of grit and "never-say-die" attitude that usually defines a turn-around season. Miller and Rudick were the catalysts, proving that no lead is safe in this league.

For the Mojo, it’s a tough pill to swallow. After entering the night holding firm at 4th place, they’ll be looking at the tape and wondering how they let a two-goal lead evaporate against an opponent at the bottom of the heap. 

As we look at the standings, the Knights climb out of the basement with 4 points, while the Mojo find themselves suddenly looking over their shoulders as the middle of the pack starts to tighten up. If tonight is any indication, the race to the postseason is going to be one wild ride.
