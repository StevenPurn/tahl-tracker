# Mother Puckers Stun Riveters in High-Scoring Offensive Outburst

If you walked into the rink expecting the second-seeded Tulsa Riveters to breeze past the Mother Puckers on Saturday morning, you were treated to a massive wake-up call—and probably a fair bit of whiplash. In a game that defied the standings, the Mother Puckers exploded for seven goals, steamrolling their way to a 7-3 victory and snapping a four-game losing skid in the most emphatic way possible.

The Riveters, coming into this one on a hot streak, actually drew first blood when Ivy Emerson sniped an unassisted goal at the 11:35 mark of the first period. For a moment, it looked like the script was set. But the Puckers had other ideas. 

"We decided it was time to stop being polite and start being a nuisance," said one Puckers skater, clearly enjoying the chaos. 

The momentum shifted dramatically toward the end of the first frame. Addison Gaut ignited the fuse at 1:17, assisted by Melissa McLauchlin, and the dam broke. Nicole Baker followed up shortly after, and Abigail Nisbet added a solo effort with just over two minutes remaining in the period. Suddenly, a 1-0 deficit had transformed into a 3-1 lead, leaving the Riveters bench looking like they’d just seen a ghost.

The second period was effectively the "Nicole Baker Show." The Puckers’ offensive engine didn't just stay running; it redlined. Baker tallied twice more—once unassisted and once while setting up Nisbet for her second goal of the game—while the Riveters struggled to find an answer for the relentless forecheck. By the time Baker buried her third point of the frame off a feed from Jessica Hinkefent at the 9:44 mark, the Puckers had turned a competitive game into a full-blown rout.

To their credit, the Riveters didn't pack it in. Hannah Atkins and Ivy Emerson added two quick tallies in the third to make things interesting, but the mountain was simply too high to climb. Kalea Maxwell put the icing on the cake for the Puckers with a late goal off an assist from Mckenna N. Allison, effectively slamming the door shut on any dreams of a Tulsa comeback.

While the Riveters will look to shake off this performance and get back to their winning ways, the Puckers are riding high. They entered the day at the bottom of the standings, but if they can bottle the energy they brought to the ice this morning, they might just be the team nobody wants to face in the coming weeks. For now, the Mother Puckers have earned their stripes, proving that in this league, any team can be a headache on their day.
