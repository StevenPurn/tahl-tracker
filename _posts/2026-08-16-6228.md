# Bone Zone Climbs the Mountain, Topples Top-Ranked Mooseknuckles in Shootout Thriller

If there’s one thing I’ve learned after forty years of smelling damp gear and stale coffee in rinks across this league, it’s that the Mooseknuckles don’t like being pushed around. But on Sunday afternoon, The Bone Zone didn’t just push—they bullied their way to a 4-3 shootout victory, proving that the gap between first and second place is thinner than the ice in the third period.

The Mooseknuckles entered the match looking to solidify their grip on the top of the standings, and they looked every bit the part early on. James Rozell drew first blood at the 13:01 mark of the first period, set up nicely by Ben Savory. It was a classic "get the legs moving" goal that suggested the leaders might have a routine afternoon. 

But The Bone Zone, currently riding a heater of a winning streak, refused to read the script. Lance Levasseur knotted things up halfway through the first, courtesy of a textbook feed from Garrett Williams, who is currently playing like he’s got a magnet in his stick blade. Before the period closed, Nicholas Strle put the Mooseknuckles back on top with a crafty finish off a Pete Fleming setup.

The middle frame turned into a defensive grinder, the kind that makes you appreciate a well-executed board battle, even if it doesn't land on the highlight reels. Both goaltenders were locked in, making the kind of saves that quiet a chirping bench.

The third period, however, was pure chaos. With the score tight, Ben Gartner seemed to provide the dagger for the Mooseknuckles at the 7:01 mark, giving them a lead that felt like it might stick. But hold your horses—the hockey gods rarely let a lead like that breathe. 

Barely thirty seconds later, Tyler Wimpee hammered home a pass from John White to keep The Bone Zone’s heart beating. Then, just over a minute later, Richard Looney struck paydirt, pulling the teams even and forcing the game into the lottery of a shootout.

In the skills competition, it was Looney again, playing the hero with the deciding goal to seal the 4-3 upset. 

"We stopped watching the standings and started watching the puck," said a Bone Zone player who was more interested in finding his water bottle than waxing poetic about the win. "It’s a long season, and if you’re going to chase the guys at the top, you might as well take their lunch money while you’re at it."

For the Mooseknuckles, the loss snaps some momentum, but they remain a dangerous outfit at 8-2-0. For The Bone Zone, this win is a massive statement. They came into this game sitting in second, and after today’s display of grit, they look less like contenders and more like the team everyone else is going to be watching in the rearview mirror. 

It wasn't a game for the purists—there was enough tripping and slashing to make a referee retire early—but for anyone who likes their hockey with a bit of dirt under the fingernails, it was exactly what Sunday afternoon required.
