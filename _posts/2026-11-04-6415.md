# Gold as Ice Hangs On to Stave Off Hot Garbage Rally

If you’ve spent any time at the rink on a Tuesday night, you know that a four-goal lead is the most dangerous score in hockey—mostly because it makes the team with the lead think they can start coasting. Gold as Ice learned that lesson the hard way on November 3rd, nearly watching a commanding lead evaporate before escaping with a 5-4 victory over Hot Garbage.

For the first two periods, it was the Dickie Williams show. Williams notched a hat trick, including a shorthanded effort at 4:55 of the first period to open the scoring. Despite a sluggish start from both sides and a couple of trips to the sin bin, Gold as Ice looked comfortable heading into the second frame. 

The middle period was a masterclass in efficiency for Gold as Ice. Williams added two more markers, while Mark Desimone chipped in with a goal of his own to stretch the lead to 4-0. It looked like a long night ahead for Hot Garbage, who seemed to be skating in quicksand while the Gold as Ice defense kept the crease as clear as a freshly Zambonied sheet of ice.

"I thought we had it in the bag," said one Gold as Ice player, who wished to remain anonymous, likely because he didn't want to admit he spent the third period breathing heavier than a pug in a sauna. "Turns out, you can’t just stop moving your feet because you’re up by four. These guys don't call themselves Hot Garbage for nothing—they're surprisingly sticky."

The tide turned in the third when Aden Ingram pushed the lead to 5-0 early in the frame. But just as the Gold as Ice bench started discussing where to grab post-game refreshments, the momentum shifted violently. Justin Phillips finally put Hot Garbage on the board at 1:37, and the dam broke. 

Joe Baker, Alan Dyke, and Abigail Nisbet all found the back of the net in a frantic stretch of offense that turned a blowout into a nail-biter. Suddenly, the Hot Garbage forecheck was aggressive, the puck movement was crisp, and the Gold as Ice goaltender was seeing more rubber than a tire factory. 

Ultimately, time became the greatest ally for Gold as Ice. Hot Garbage managed to claw their way to a respectable 5-4 final, but the clock ran out on their comeback bid before they could find that elusive equalizer.

For Gold as Ice, the win is a crucial two points that keeps them in the hunt in the middle of the pack. For Hot Garbage, the loss is a tough pill to swallow, though they’ll likely be taking confidence from that third-period surge into their next outing. As for me? I’m just happy I didn’t have to cover a shootout. My heart can only take so much, and the concessions stand was already closed.
