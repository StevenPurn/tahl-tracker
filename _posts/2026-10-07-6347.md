# Reapers Scythe Through Kraken Cold Ones in 4-2 Victory

If you were looking for a reason to keep your eyes on the rink last night, you weren’t alone. The Reapers and the Kraken Cold Ones took to the ice at 9:30 p.m. with identical three-point records, but it was the Reapers who left the barn looking like a team that finally found their rhythm, skating away with a 4-2 victory that felt a lot more comfortable than the score might suggest.

The Reapers came out of the gate with their hair on fire. Jose Herrick, who seems to have a magnetic attraction to the back of the net these days, opened the scoring at the 12:34 mark of the first period, assisted by Robert Norris. Not long after, Paul Sanders found the twine thanks to some crisp passing from Chance DeMier and Sherry Sanders, putting the Kraken in an early 2-0 hole. The Kraken Cold Ones did manage to stop the bleeding before the intermission when Paul Davis buried a feed from Shon Rogers at the seven-minute mark, providing a glimmer of hope for the Kraken bench.

But in the second period, Herrick returned to the scene of the crime. Snagging his second of the night—and effectively silencing any comeback talk from the Kraken—he stretched the lead to 3-1, with Norris and Lucas Jameson picking up the helpers. The Kraken tried to manufacture some grit, even picking up a roughing penalty via Paul Davis later in the frame, but the Reapers' defensive shell held firm under the lights.

The third period turned into a bit of a chess match, albeit one played with frozen rubber and short tempers. Sherry Sanders headed to the box for tripping at 10:06, and the Kraken capitalized on the power play momentum when Chase Stubblefield found the back of the net, thanks to some nice work from Robert Tyree and Justin McKinney. Just when it looked like the Kraken might put together a late-game rally, Robert Norris slammed the door shut at the 7:06 mark, potting the final goal of the night with assists from Jameson and Herrick.

"We just played our game," Herrick remarked after the buzzer, wiping a bit of sweat from his brow. "The puck was bouncing our way, and when we move it like that, we’re a tough group to keep up with."

For the Kraken Cold Ones, it’s a tough night that keeps them stuck in the bottom half of the pack. They’ve been struggling to find consistency, and losing a game like this against a fellow mid-table contender is going to sting during the post-game refreshments. Meanwhile, the Reapers will take this win and try to use it as a launchpad. With both teams sporting 1-2-1 records coming in, this win moves the Reapers to 2-2-1, giving them a much-needed morale boost as we head further into the season. 

It wasn't a masterpiece of coaching or a showcase of perfect systems, but it was a solid, hard-nosed night of local hockey. And really, that’s all we ask for on a Tuesday night.
