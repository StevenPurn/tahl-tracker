# Jackalopes Run Wild in 9-5 Scoring Frenzy Over Gold as Ice Bronze

If you were looking for a quiet, defensive chess match on a Saturday night at the rink, you definitely walked into the wrong arena. The Jackalopes and Gold as Ice Bronze decided to throw the playbook out the window, lighting the lamp a combined 14 times in a chaotic 9-5 Jackalopes victory that felt more like a tennis match than a hockey game.

From the drop of the puck, it was clear that defense was considered a mere suggestion by both squads. John White was the undisputed architect of the Jackalopes' offense, putting on a clinic that likely left the opposing goaltender needing a very stiff drink and a long nap. White kicked things off just seconds into the first period and never really looked back, anchoring an attack that kept the pressure dialed up to eleven.

Gold as Ice Bronze showed some real grit, refusing to let the game slip away early. Makenzie Giove and Eric Kolis both managed to find the back of the net to keep things interesting, but the Jackalopes had an answer for every question asked. By the time the second period wrapped up, the Jackalopes had stretched their lead, thanks in no small part to Bradley Fidler and Alex Ruiz cleaning up the scraps in front of the crease.

The real headline of the night, however, had to be the absolute heater TRISTON WILSON is on. Coming into the game on a six-game goal streak, Wilson wasn’t about to let a little thing like a lopsided scoreboard stop him. He was a one-man wrecking crew for Gold as Ice Bronze, potting a natural hat trick. 

"I don't know what they’re feeding him before he hits the ice," one fan muttered near the glass after Wilson’s third goal of the night, "but I want a plate of it." 

Despite Wilson's heroics, the Jackalopes’ depth was simply too much to overcome. Kyle Foster and John White—who seemed to be everywhere at once—continued to exploit the gaps, ensuring the Jackalopes kept their noses firmly in front. By the time the final buzzer rang, the scoreboard looked more like a grocery bill than a hockey score.

For the Jackalopes, this win is a breath of fresh air, moving them to 3-3-3 on the season and giving them a much-needed boost in the middle of the pack. It was the kind of offensive outburst that can wake up a locker room. On the flip side, Gold as Ice Bronze remains in a tough spot at 2-6-1, but if Wilson keeps playing like a man possessed, they’ve got plenty of firepower to turn things around once they tighten up the screws in their own end.

All in all, it was a high-octane night of local hockey. The kind of game that reminds you why we show up—win or lose, there’s always a story to tell over that post-game pint.
