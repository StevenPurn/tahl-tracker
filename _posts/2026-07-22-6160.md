# Murder in the Rink: Tulsa Dismantles Jackalopes in 10-4 Scoring Frenzy

**By: "Slap-Shot" Sammy, Hometown Beat Reporter**
**Date: July 21, 2026**

If you blinked, you missed it. If you went to the concession stand for a hot dog, you missed three more goals. Tonight’s tilt at the barn was less of a hockey game and more of a tactical strike, as the Tulsa Murder marched into the Jackalopes’ backyard and turned the ice into their personal shooting gallery. 

When the dust settled and the Zamboni took the ice, the scoreboard read a staggering **Tulsa Murder 10, Jackalopes 4.** 

### First Period: A Tale of Two Starts
The Jackalopes actually looked like they might pull off the upset early on. Garrett Williams opened the scoring at 10:48, courtesy of a slick feed from Robert Verity. When Christopher King buried another one with just over two minutes remaining, the home crowd was buzzing. 

But then, the Murder flipped the switch. Pete Fleming, riding a red-hot five-game goal streak, ignited a Tulsa barrage. The Murder tallied four unanswered goals in a dizzying stretch, with Fleming netting two and the red-hot Luke Brandon finding the twine. By the time the horn blew for the first intermission, Tulsa had completely wrestled momentum away, turning a 2-0 deficit into a 4-2 lead.

### Second Period: The Floodgates Burst
The Jackalopes tried to keep their heads above water, and Kyle Foster provided a glimmer of hope with an unassisted tally early in the second. But the Murder were simply firing on all cylinders. 

Tom Mortimer—still feeling it with his active goal streak—added another to the tally, and then things got physical. A parade to the sin bin for the Jackalopes (hooking and interference) gave the Murder exactly the oxygen they needed. Adam Hoffdahl took advantage of the open ice, burying a shot that sent the Jackalopes’ defenders scrambling. By the end of two, the game had transformed from a competitive bout into a total clinic.

### Third Period: The Closing Statement
The third period was all about the Murder padding their stats and cementing their status as a force to be reckoned with. Jonathan P. Sanders notched a pair, while Pete Fleming completed an absolute clinic of a night, adding his third goal to the ledger off a beautiful dish from Luke Brandon. 

Ronald Herwig managed to find the back of the net for the Jackalopes late in the frame, but at 10-4, it was far too little, too late. The Murder netminder stood tall when called upon, facing a flurry of late-game traffic but refusing to let the Jackalopes find any real rhythm.

### The Aftermath
For the **Tulsa Murder (5-3-0)**, this win is a massive statement. They climb the ladder with 10 points, firming up their grip on the top half of the standings and proving that when their offense is humming, they can light the lamp against anyone in the league. 

For the **Jackalopes (1-4-2)**, it’s back to the drawing board. Tonight’s result highlights some defensive lapses that simply can’t happen if they want to claw their way out of the bottom of the table. They’ll need to stop the bleeding and tighten up the neutral zone before the next puck drop.

It wasn't a barn burner in terms of closeness, but it was a masterclass in offensive pressure. If you like goals, you loved tonight. If you’re a Jackalopes fan, you’re probably looking for an aspirin. 

**Next up:** The Murder look to carry this momentum into their next series, while the Jackalopes look to find their defensive identity once more. See you at the rink!
