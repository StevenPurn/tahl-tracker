# Yeti Avalanche Buries Tulsa Murder in 9-4 Offensive Explosion

If you like your hockey served with a side of high-octane offense and a total disregard for defensive systems, the ice at the local rink was the only place to be this Saturday afternoon. In a matchup that looked like a defensive grudge match on paper, the Yetis absolutely dismantled the Tulsa Murder, skating away with a dominant 9-4 victory that reminded everyone in the league just how dangerous this Yeti squad can be when the puck starts bouncing their way.

The first period set a deceptive tone, with both squads trading blows and feeling out the pace. Jason Fennell broke the seal early for the Yetis, finding the back of the net at the 14:05 mark off some nifty work from Corey Favier and Kirk Nielsen. But just as the Yetis looked to head into the intermission with a lead, Tulsa’s Luke Brandon reminded us why he’s on a four-game point streak, burying a beautiful feed from Mark Hodges and Tom Mortimer with mere seconds left on the clock to knot things up at 1-1.

Then, the floodgates didn't just open—they were ripped off the hinges.

The second period was effectively the Jason Bahr and Jason Fennell show. After a hooking penalty gave the Yetis a power play opportunity, the team took the lesson to heart. Within a frantic four-minute window midway through the period, the Yetis scored four unanswered goals. Bahr tallied two of his own, sandwiching a Kirk Nielsen strike, and Fennell added another unassisted beauty before Brendan Burkman capped the carnage to put the Yetis up 6-1 heading into the final frame. 

"I think we just stopped checking the rearview mirror and decided to put the pedal to the floor," joked one Yeti bench regular after the game. "Once the third one went in, the Murder looked like they were trying to skate through wet concrete."

Tulsa Murder didn’t go quietly, showing flashes of the grit that kept them in the top three of the standings. Luke Brandon grabbed his second of the game early in the third, followed quickly by a strike from Pete Fleming, who extended his legendary goal streak to nine games. But any hopes of a Murder comeback were extinguished by a late-game surge from the Yetis. Denis Blaquiere, Corey Favier, and a hat-trick-completing goal from Fennell turned the scoreboard into something resembling a football game.

Justin Oxford managed a late consolation prize for Tulsa, but it was far too little, too late. 

For the Yetis, the win is a massive momentum builder. They entered the day sitting in fourth place, but this offensive clinic serves as a stern warning to the rest of the league: when the chemistry is this clean, the Yetis aren't just playing for position—they're playing for blood. As for the Murder, they’ll look to shake off the rust and get back to their winning ways, having learned the hard way that against the Yetis, if you give them an inch, they’ll take the whole rink.
