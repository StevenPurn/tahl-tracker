# Perfect Ten: Shooting Blanks Stay Undefeated in Tornado Taming

If you were looking for a Saturday morning wake-up call, you didn’t need a double shot of espresso—you just needed a ticket to the rink. The league-leading Shooting Blanks kept their immaculate record intact, moving to a pristine 10-0-0 after a 6-4 victory over the Tornadoes. It was a game that featured more back-and-forth action than a rhythmic gymnastics final, with the Blanks ultimately proving that their winning streak is about as fragile as an anvil.

The Tornadoes, desperate to snap a five-game slide, came out like a team that had replaced their water bottles with rocket fuel. Christopher Cieslak struck first at the 11:27 mark of the opening frame, burying a feed from Nicklas Clark and Steven Purn. But if you blink against the Shooting Blanks, you don't just miss a play—you miss an entire shift in momentum. A mere 66 seconds later, Austin Tyler knotted the score, and before the ice could even settle, Elliott Langford punched in a go-ahead goal just twenty seconds after that.

"We felt like we were playing hot potato with the lead," said one anonymous rink official while wiping popcorn off his clipboard. "Every time someone scored, the other team looked like they’d been told their breakfast was burning."

Christian Alvegran added to the madness by burying a helper from Tobias Alvegran to keep the Tornadoes in the hunt, but Bobby Watson’s late-period strike sent the Blanks into the break with a 3-2 edge.

The second period saw the Blanks try to pull away, with Elliott Langford doubling his tally at the 2:39 mark. Even when the Tornadoes’ Cieslak snatched his second of the game—his stick seemingly enchanted by the hockey gods—the Blanks had an answer. Nicholas Power, who was playing like he had an extra gear hidden in his left skate, pushed the lead back to two late in the period. 

The third period was pure, unadulterated chaos. Nicklas Clark narrowed the gap for the Tornadoes with a gritty goal at the 14:25 mark, setting the stage for a grandstand finish. The atmosphere turned electric—or perhaps just mildly electrified—when Tobias Alvegran was assessed a penalty for a "thrown stick." It was a moment of pure, cinematic frustration that left the bench gasping. The Blanks, smelling blood in the water, didn't hesitate; Nicholas Power capitalized on the ensuing sequence with an unassisted laser, effectively slamming the door shut on any hopes of a Tornadoes comeback.

For the Shooting Blanks, the win is a masterclass in consistency. Their roster is currently a collective hydra of point-streaks and offensive production, with Tyler and Power putting on a clinical display of chemistry. Meanwhile, the Tornadoes remain winless in their last six, though they played with the kind of ferocity that suggests a breakthrough is hovering just over the horizon.

As for the Shooting Blanks? They’re heading home with a 10-0-0 record, looking less like a hockey team and more like a force of nature that simply refuses to read the scouting reports of their opponents. The rest of the league can keep throwing sticks—and everything else—at them, but for now, the Blanks are in a league of their own.
