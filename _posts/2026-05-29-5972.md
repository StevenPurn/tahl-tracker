# Tyree’s Two-Step: Kraken Cold Ones Freeze the Wendigos in Shutout Victory

**By: Your Rinkside Reporter**
**Date: May 28, 2026**

The ice was crisp, the air was cold, and by the time the final buzzer echoed through the rafters at 9:30 PM, the Kraken Cold Ones had turned the Wendigos’ offense into a ghost story. In a contest that proved defense isn't just a suggestion—it’s a lifestyle—the Kraken skated away with a commanding 3-0 victory, effectively putting the league on notice.

### First Period: Tyree Takes Control
The Wendigos walked into the rink looking to keep their winning momentum alive, but Robert Tyree had other plans. Early in the opening frame at the 12:44 mark, Tyree capitalized on a defensive lapse, weaving through the neutral zone like a man possessed and snapping one past the Wendigos' tender. It was an unassisted beauty—the kind that makes the opposing goalie wonder if they left the oven on at home.

Just when the Wendigos thought they could catch their breath, Tyree struck again with 3:43 remaining. It was a mirror image of his first tally: pure hustle, zero help, and a quick flick of the wrists that found the twine. Heading into the first intermission, it was all Cold Ones, 2-0.

### Second Period: The Goalie Clinic
The middle frame was a textbook display of "nothing to see here." Both teams traded chances, but the goalies were dialed in, standing on their heads and robbing potential scorers with glove saves that looked like they were plucked straight from a highlight reel. The Wendigos tried to generate some "traffic in front of the net," hoping to deflect a lucky bounce, but the Kraken blueliners were clearing the crease with the authority of a bouncer at a sold-out show.

### Third Period: The Final Nail
The Wendigos pressed hard to start the third, sensing the clock was their new biggest opponent. But just as the pressure mounted, the Cold Ones delivered the dagger. At 1:44, Gregory Boyer took a crisp feed from Justin McKinney and buried it, extending the lead to 3-0. 

The Wendigos attempted a late-game rally, but the Kraken’s resolve held firm. Even when Chase Stubblefield was sent to the sin bin for a roughing penalty at the 8:30 mark, the Cold Ones' penalty kill unit played with enough grit to sand down a mountain, refusing to give the Wendigos even a sniff of a comeback. 

### The Bottom Line
For the Kraken Cold Ones, this victory is a massive statement. After stumbling in their previous outing, they returned to form tonight, proving they can lock down the defensive zone and strike with lethal efficiency. 

As for the Wendigos, this marks their first real test of the season. A shutout loss is never how you want to draw it up, but in a league this competitive, they’ll have to shake off the rust quickly. The standings are tightening, and with the rest of the field—from Gold as Ice to the Reapers—looking to climb the ladder, tonight’s result just made the upcoming schedule that much more interesting. 

The Kraken Cold Ones have officially thawed out, and the rest of the league better have their skates sharp. See you at the rink!
