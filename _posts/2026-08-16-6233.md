# Berzerkers Edge Out Danglers in Gritty Rinkside Grinder

If you like your hockey served with a side of whistle-heavy frustration and enough trips to the penalty box to keep the timekeeper awake, Wednesday night’s tilt between the Berzerkers and the Dirty Danglers was exactly what the doctor ordered. In a contest that looked more like a parade to the sin bin than a symphony of skating, the Berzerkers managed to scrap their way to a 3-2 victory, proving that sometimes, simply surviving the referee’s arm is half the battle.

The Danglers, riding a respectable three-game heater, struck first blood at the 10:59 mark of the opening frame when Charles Webber buried a feed from Daniel Maxwell. It was a textbook start for a team looking to climb the standings, but the Berzerkers—a team that clearly doesn't mind playing in the dirt—refused to buckle. 

In fact, the Berzerkers seemed to play better when they were down a man. Shortly after LC III Sinor took a seat for tripping, Alan Dyke reminded everyone why you shouldn't turn your back on the play, potting an unassisted short-handed goal at 12:47 to shift the momentum. Before the first period buzzer rang, Sinor made up for his earlier lapse by putting one past the keeper off a Hayden Wheeler setup, putting the Berzerkers ahead 2-1 heading into the break.

"It was one of those nights where the refs were looking for work and found it," noted one seasoned observer near the glass, and he wasn't wrong. The second period turned into a slow-motion car crash of whistles, hooks, and trips. Amidst the chaos, Zac Hughes extended the Berzerker lead to 3-1 with an individual effort at the 13:03 mark, a goal that proved to be the difference-maker.

The Danglers tried to manufacture a comeback late in the third, with Brett Mooney cleaning up a Lucas Jameson pass to draw within one at 12:34. However, the Berzerkers’ defense—and the sheer number of penalties being called—effectively killed any chance of a sustained Dangler rally. By the time the final horn sounded, the ice felt more like a hallway in a boarding school, with both benches thinning out from constant trips to the box.

"We played about forty minutes of hockey and twenty minutes of sitting on our thumbs," a weary-looking player muttered near the locker room exit. It wasn't pretty, it wasn't graceful, but for the Berzerkers, it was two points.

The result keeps the Berzerkers firmly in the hunt at mid-table, while the Dirty Danglers find their momentum halted in a game that felt more like a lesson in discipline than a showcase of skill. For both squads, the takeaway is simple: spend less time skating to the penalty box and more time skating to the net. If they can clean up the extra-curriculars, both teams have the pieces to make some noise as the season grinds on.
