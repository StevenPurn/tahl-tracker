# Bison Stampede Through Eagles in 6-2 Victory

If you were looking for a sign that the Tulsa Bison are playing at a level above the rest of the pack, you only needed to be at the rink last night. Riding a wave of momentum that would make a tsunami blush, the Bison waltzed into a 6-2 victory over the Lite Beer Eagles, stretching their winning streak to a cool half-dozen.

The game started with a frantic pace that suggested the Eagles might actually have some wings tonight. Ethan Hilton opened the scoring just as the first period was gasping its last breath, netting an unassisted goal with less than a second on the clock. It was the kind of buzzer-beater that usually gives a team a shot of adrenaline, but the Bison? They’re playing like a team that doesn't even know how to check their pulse.

The second period turned into a back-and-forth skirmish. Brett Mooney, who seems to have the puck glued to his stick lately, tied things up at 12:51, but the Eagles responded quickly when Corey Phillips buried a pass from Hilton to reclaim the lead. 

That was the last time the Eagles felt comfortable. 

"We were buzzing, we were skating, and then suddenly it felt like we were skating through wet cement," one anonymous Eagle was overheard muttering near the bench. Whether it was the cement or just the relentless pressure of the Bison’s forecheck, the tide turned for good in the final seconds of the second. Brian Cuff found the back of the net with less than a second remaining in the frame, effectively sucking the air out of the Eagles' locker room.

If the second period was a contest, the third was a coronation. The Bison opened the final frame with a clinical display of why they sit comfortably at third in the standings. JUAN PADILLA continued his hot streak, finding the net early, followed shortly by a solo effort from SPENCER SHEETS. 

Then, Brett Mooney decided he wasn't done for the night. He added two more goals in the third—one unassisted and one tucked away neatly off a feed from Brett Mecham—to complete his hat trick and put the game firmly out of reach. 

For the Bison, this is business as usual; their fifth consecutive win solidifies their standing as a legitimate force to be reckoned with. They’re playing a brand of hockey that is as disciplined as it is dangerous. As for the Eagles, it’s a tough pill to swallow after keeping it close for two periods, but they’ll have to regroup quickly if they want to climb out of that eighth-place spot.

As the final buzzer sounded, the contrast was clear: the Bison are marching toward the top of the leaderboard with heavy boots, while the Eagles are looking for a spark to reignite their season. The beer tasted a little colder for the Tulsa crowd last night—and if you ask anyone in a Bison jersey, they’ll tell you they’re already hungry for the next one.
