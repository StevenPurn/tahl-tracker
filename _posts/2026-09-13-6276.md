# Pickett Powers Hitmen to 6-4 Win Over Wolfpack

If you’re a fan of old-school, goal-scoring outbursts, the rink at 6:45 on Sunday night was the place to be. In a game that saw more lead changes and momentum swings than a bad penny, the Hitmen Silver outlasted the One Man Wolfpack 6-4, securing their first win of the season and leaving the Wolfpack looking for answers.

The star of the show was undoubtedly Bobby Pickett, who decided that the net belonged to him tonight. Pickett turned in a four-goal performance that would have made a sniper blush. He opened the scoring early, netting one at the 4:20 mark of the first period off a crisp feed from Nicklas Clark, and added another shortly after when Lukas Vodicka pushed the lead to 2-0. 

The Wolfpack showed some grit, refusing to roll over. Brandon Fidler clawed one back late in the first, but Pickett answered again in the second period, showing the kind of nose for the net that coaches dream about—and that defenders generally find infuriating.

The middle frame, however, was where the discipline—or lack thereof—began to boil over. The Wolfpack found themselves in a hole, exacerbated by a flurry of penalties. Michael Migliaro’s night took a turn for the worse; after an early slashing call, he eventually earned himself an early exit with a 10-minute misconduct for verbal abuse of an official in the third period. It’s hard to win when your best players are spending more time chirping at the refs than actually skating, and the Hitmen were more than happy to capitalize on the chaos.

The third period turned into a frantic back-and-forth affair. Kyle Okurowski put on a clinic for the Wolfpack, scoring twice to keep things interesting and briefly threatening a comeback. But just when it looked like the Wolfpack might find a way to tie it, the Hitmen leaned on their depth. Joshua Moyer scored at the 2:31 mark, and Pickett capped off his four-goal night late in the final frame to put the game on ice.

"Pickett could’ve put the puck in a hat from the blue line tonight and it still would’ve found the corner," one spectator noted, probably only half-joking.

For the Hitmen, the victory is a massive morale boost as they climb the standings. They looked composed, even when the Wolfpack pushed back, and played the kind of structured, opportunistic hockey that wins games at this level. The One Man Wolfpack, meanwhile, will likely want to take a long look at their penalty discipline before their next outing. You can’t win when you’re constantly short-handed, and by the time the final whistle blew, they were clearly out of gas and out of options.

It wasn't always pretty, and it certainly wasn't quiet—especially with the officials getting an earful—but it was a win. For the Hitmen, that’s all that matters on a Sunday night.
