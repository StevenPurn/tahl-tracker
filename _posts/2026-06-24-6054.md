# Yeti Sightings: Tulsa Murder’s Perfect Season Halted in High-Octane Barn Burner

**TULSA** – If you were looking for a quiet Tuesday night at the rink, you clearly took a wrong turn at the parking lot. In a game that saw the ice surface take more abuse than a Zamboni on a double shift, the Yetis pulled off the upset of the season, handing the high-flying Tulsa Murder their first loss of the campaign in a 6-4 thriller.

The Murder came into the contest boasting a pristine 3-0 record, but tonight, the Yetis didn’t just show up—they brought the snowstorm.

### Period 1: Trading Haymakers
The game started with the throttle pinned. Just 6.7 seconds into the contest, Corey Favier signaled his intentions, burying a feed from Jason Fennell to put the Yetis on the board before the fans had even finished settling into their seats. 

The Murder, looking a bit shocked, found themselves in a hole, but they aren't nicknamed the "Murder" for their manners. Even with Jarred Butler cooling his heels in the box for a trip, the Murder found some short-handed magic. Jason Bigelow went coast-to-coast, unassisted, to tie it up. Shortly after, Pete Fleming kept his streak alive, putting the Murder ahead 2-1. But the Yetis showed serious grit, as Aidan Doyle and Jered Pieschke both found the back of the net to give the visitors a 3-2 lead heading into the first intermission.

### Period 2: The Fennell Factor
If you’re looking for the man of the hour, look no further than Jason Fennell. He was involved in almost everything, orchestrating the Yeti offense like a maestro with a stick. 

The second period belonged to the visitors. Corey Favier grabbed his second of the night, followed by a Fennell beauty that pushed the lead to 5-2. Even when the Yetis went down a man after an Aidan Doyle tripping call, it didn't slow them down. Kirk Nielsen capitalized on a shorthanded opportunity to make it 6-2. 

The Murder, sensing the game slipping away, finally clawed back when Jarred Butler redeemed his earlier penalty with a strike at the 9:56 mark, trimming the deficit to 6-3.

### Period 3: Too Little, Too Late
The final frame was a display of desperate, gritty hockey. The Murder threw the kitchen sink at the Yeti crease, looking for any way to disrupt the rhythm. Jason Bigelow managed to find the twine again at the 3:57 mark, bringing the score to 6-4 and sparking a glimmer of hope in the home crowd.

However, the Yeti defensive wall—supported by some stellar glove work—held firm. A late tripping penalty to Spencer Charles took the wind out of the Murder’s sails, and despite a frantic push in the closing minutes, the Yetis skated off the ice victorious.

### The Aftermath
The landscape of the standings just got a whole lot more crowded. The Tulsa Murder (3-1-0) drop their first points of the year, falling from their perch, though they remain a dangerous #2 in the rankings. 

For the Yetis (2-2-1), this is the kind of momentum-shifting win that defines a season. They proved tonight that they have the firepower to go toe-to-toe with the league’s best. Jason Fennell’s multi-point masterpiece will be talked about at the water cooler tomorrow morning, but tonight, the city of Tulsa learned one thing: when you face the Yetis, you’d better keep your head on a swivel.

**Final Score:** Tulsa Murder 4, Yetis 6.
