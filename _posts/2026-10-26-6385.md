# Puckedelics Overcome Two-Goal Deficit to Stun Leafs

If there’s one thing this league has taught me over the years, it’s that you never turn off the lights until the final buzzer sounds. The Tulsa Leafs learned that lesson the hard way on Friday night at the local rink, watching a comfortable two-goal lead evaporate in a frantic, three-minute collapse that handed the Puckedelics a 3-2 victory.

For the first two periods, it looked like the Leafs might actually pull off an upset against the division leaders. They played a disciplined, lunch-pail style of hockey that would make a coach blush. Curtis Connors got the ball rolling—or the puck sliding—at 6:42 of the opening frame, burying a feed from Braden Trinka to open the scoring. When Trinka added a solo effort at 11:25 of the second, the Leafs held a 2-0 cushion and the Puckedelics looked uncharacteristically sluggish, seemingly allergic to the back of the net.

The Puckedelics entered the night sitting pretty at the top of the standings, but they spent most of the evening looking more like a group of guys who’d just met in the parking lot. James DePriest’s high-sticking penalty in the second period didn't help matters, and for a long stretch, the Puckedelics’ power play looked about as effective as a screen door on a submarine.

"We were skating in mud," one Puckedelics veteran admitted near the bench, though he didn't want his name in print—probably because his shift speed was hovering somewhere near 'stationary.'

But hockey is a funny game. All it takes is one bounce, and suddenly the pressure cooker is on the other side. With under four minutes remaining in regulation, the script flipped entirely. 

Triston Wilson finally broke the ice for the Puckedelics at 3:39, assisted by Joe Baker and Dylan Calhoun, cutting the lead in half. The Leafs, perhaps sensing the encroaching shadows of a late-game breakdown, tightened up, but it wasn't enough. Twenty seconds later, Baker turned from playmaker to goal-scorer, knotting the game at 2-2 off a helper from John White. 

The arena went from a casual hum to absolute bedlam. Less than a minute after the equalizer, Kirk Nielsen hammered home an unassisted go-ahead goal at 4:54, completing a comeback that turned a sleepy Friday night contest into a proper barnburner. 

To their credit, the Leafs didn't fold, but the momentum had shifted so violently it felt like the boards were leaning. When the final buzzer sounded, the Puckedelics walked away with their unblemished record intact, while the Leafs were left wondering how a 2-0 lead vanished into the rafters. 

The Puckedelics (5-0-1) continue their march atop the standings, proving that while they don’t always start pretty, they certainly know how to finish. The Leafs (1-3-2), meanwhile, will have to shake off the sting of this one. They played 45 minutes of winning hockey, but as we know, the only stat that matters is the one next to the final buzzer.
