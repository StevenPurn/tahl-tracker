# Ice Cold Statement: Gold as Ice Thaws the Bison in 4-2 Thriller

**TULSA** – If you were looking for a Saturday night barn burner, the arena was the only place to be. The Tulsa Bison and Gold as Ice skated onto the frozen pond tonight with high stakes on the line, but by the time the final buzzer echoed through the rafters, it was Gold as Ice who stood tall, proving exactly why they’re sitting pretty at the top of the standings.

In a 4-2 victory, Gold as Ice showed the kind of poise that makes championship dreams feel less like a fairy tale and more like an impending reality.

### A Tense Middle Frame
The first period was a defensive stalemate, a classic game of "who’s going to blink first?" The goalies were sharp, painting the crease with authority and turning away everything that dared enter their zip code. 

The dam finally broke in the second period, and it started with a bit of a discipline nightmare for the Bison. A tripping call on Brian Cuff opened the door, and Kalea Maxwell—fueled by the playmaking vision of Mark DeSimone and Taylor Chinowth—potted the opener. 

The Bison, however, aren't the type to roll over. Juan Padilla, looking for his fourth goal in as many games, knotted things up at 1-1 with a gritty finish off a dish from Joe Baker. But just when it looked like the Bison had the momentum, Aden Ingram reminded us why he’s on a scoring tear. Ingram scorched the twine unassisted at 7:39, restoring the lead for Gold as Ice and sucking the air right out of the Bison’s bench.

### The Third Period Surge
If the second period was a chess match, the third was a full-blown street fight. 

Gold as Ice came out with their skates on fire. Aden Ingram cemented his man-of-the-match status with his second goal of the night, a beautiful snipe set up by Robert Norris to push the lead to 3-1. Moments later, Dickie Williams joined the party, cleaning up a Taylor Chinowth rebound to push the lead to a commanding three-goal cushion.

The Bison’s spirit remained unbroken, though. With tensions boiling over—a classic roughing-and-slashing scrap between Joe Baker and Aden Ingram—the game got physical. Once the dust settled, Juan Padilla struck again with 7:44 left, dancing through traffic to tuck his second of the night into the corner. It was a beautiful display of stick-handling, but it was too little, too late.

### The Takeaway
For **Gold as Ice (3-0-0)**, this win is a massive statement. They’ve dismantled a quality Bison squad and remain undefeated, firmly entrenched in that elite tier of the league. They’re playing cohesive, unselfish hockey, and tonight, they proved they have the depth to bury teams when the pressure is at its peak.

For the **Tulsa Bison (2-2-0)**, this is a tough pill to swallow, but far from the end of the road. Juan Padilla is playing out of his mind, and if they can tighten up their discipline—staying out of the box will be priority number one at next practice—they’ve got the grit to climb back into the top three.

The ice has melted, the post-game sweat is drying, and one thing is for sure: the league standings just got a whole lot more interesting. We’ll see you at the rink!
