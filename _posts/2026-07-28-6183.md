# A Night to Forget: Mighty Drunks Flex Their Muscles in 8-1 Rout of The Mafia

**By: The Rinkside Reporter**
**Date: July 28, 2026**

If you caught the late-night action at the barn last night, you might have felt a chill in the air—and it wasn’t just the ice. The first-place Mighty Drunks arrived at the 10:15 PM puck drop looking like a team on a mission, dismantling The Mafia in an 8-1 demolition that proved exactly why they sit atop the league standings. 

### First Period: The Calm Before the Storm
The Mafia came into this one hoping to shake up the hierarchy, but the Drunks were feeling loose early. Despite a tripping call on Frederick Kemp that gave The Mafia an early man-advantage, it was the Drunks who drew first blood. 

Cian Slattery ignited the lamp at the 7:01 mark, courtesy of a slick feed from brother Connor. The Mafia showed a flicker of life just thirteen seconds later when Scott Hellard buried one, assisted by Aaron West and Nathan Garcia, to knot it at 1-1. But that was the last time the home crowd had anything to cheer about. Tyler Waller and Thomas A. Wolff rattled off back-to-back goals in the final minute of the period to send the Drunks into the locker room with a 3-1 cushion. 

### Second Period: A Tactical Takedown
If the first period was a skirmish, the second was a siege. The Drunks tightened the screws, with Tyler Waller putting on a clinic, notching his second of the night at 10:12. 

The Mafia’s frustration began to boil over as the game slipped away. Between a hooking call on David Hardy and a chaotic sequence late in the frame that saw Chad Olson and Aaron West both heading to the sin bin, the ice tilted heavily in favor of the Drunks. Cian Slattery hammered home his second of the night off a gorgeous setup from Monroe and Fahland, and Waller completed a personal hat-trick with a solo effort that silenced any lingering hopes of a Mafia comeback. By the time the horn blew for the second intermission, it was a 6-1 lead for the visitors.

### Third Period: Closing the Vault
The final frame saw the Drunks cruise in neutral, though they weren’t quite finished with the stat sheet. Jared Hallahan extended the lead at 14:02, and Matthew Holland capped off the scoring with a solo highlight-reel finish at 4:19.

The Mafia tried to find some sandpaper late, with Brian Maher drawing a roughing penalty, but the Mighty Drunks’ defensive structure remained as sturdy as a brick wall in front of their crease. The Drunks were relentless, moving the puck with the precision of a Swiss watch and consistently winning the battles for "traffic in front of the net."

### The Aftermath
For the Mighty Drunks, this 8-1 victory serves as a massive statement. They improve to 8-0-1, further cementing their position as the team to beat in this league. They aren't just winning; they are dominating the competition, playing a brand of hockey that is fast, physical, and frankly, fun to watch.

As for The Mafia, this is a night to tuck away in the "learning experience" folder. At 5-3-0, they remain a competitive squad, but they’ll need to regain their composure and tighten up defensively if they want to hang with the big dogs in the postseason push. 

The league standings remain tight, but tonight, the Mighty Drunks sent a clear message to the rest of the pack: *Catch us if you can.*
