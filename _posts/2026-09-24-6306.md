# Leafs Find Their Flow in 6-3 Victory Over Murder

If you’re a proponent of the "slow-burn" theory of hockey, the Tulsa Leafs' 6-3 victory over the Tulsa Murder on Wednesday night was a masterclass in calculated escalation. While the Murder jumped out to an early lead, the Leafs spent the remainder of the evening systematically dismantling the opposition’s structure, eventually finding their rhythm to secure their first win of the season.

The contest began with the Murder drawing first blood at 10:40 of the opening frame, courtesy of their number 9. It was a textbook start for the Murder, who seemed content to sit back and clog the neutral zone. However, the momentum shifted with a frantic final three minutes in the first period. Just as the Murder found themselves short-handed following a slashing minor to number 44, the Leafs clicked into gear. Hunter Sharpe capitalized on a dish from Braden Trinka with just 21.6 seconds remaining in the period to knot the game at 1-1.

"We were playing like we were skating in molasses for the first ten minutes," admitted one Leafs observer who prefers to remain anonymous but definitely enjoys a good spreadsheet. "Once Sharpe buried that equalizer, the ice tilted permanently."

The Leafs carried that energy into a dominant offensive display. Jason Bahr found the back of the net at the 7:06 mark, assisted by Zac Hughes and James Darnaby, before Braden Trinka decided to take matters into his own hands—literally. Trinka tallied an unassisted goal at 9:12 to push the lead to 3-1. By the time the second period concluded, the Leafs had effectively put the game out of reach, with Trinka adding his second of the night and Sharpe completing his brace, aided by Dillon King and Trinka.

Entering the third period with a commanding 5-1 lead, the Leafs showed the slight complacency that often plagues recreational squads leading by four. The Murder managed a brief flicker of life when their number 9 notched his second goal of the evening at the 4:00 mark. 

However, the Leafs' discipline under pressure proved the deciding factor. While Dan Munson’s interference penalty gave the Murder a window of opportunity, the Leafs' defensive structure held firm. Zac Hughes delivered the final blow for the Leafs with a solo effort at 40.2 seconds left in the period, effectively silencing any comeback talk. A late power-play goal from the Murder’s number 36 served as little more than a statistical consolation prize.

For the Leafs, the victory is a significant climb in a crowded standings pool. Moving from 0-0-1 to 2-0-1, they leapfrog several teams in the middle of the pack. The Murder, meanwhile, remain stuck at 0-1-1. While their number 9 proved to be a singular offensive threat, the team will need to tighten their defensive rotations if they hope to climb out of the bottom half of the table.

As the dust settles, the Leafs leave the rink having proven that while the Murder may be aggressive, they aren't necessarily efficient. For now, the Leafs can enjoy the view from the upper-middle echelon of the league—a place where the numbers, finally, look quite favorable.
