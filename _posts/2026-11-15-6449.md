# Mother Puckers Find Their Rhythm in Shutout Win Over Chix with Stix

If you’re looking for a reminder that hockey is a game played in the present tense, look no further than the Saturday morning tilt at the local rink. The Mother Puckers marched into the ice shed and proceeded to treat the Chix with Stix like a defensive drill, walking away with a decisive 5-0 victory to break their own recent skid and settle the tie in the standings.

It was clear from the opening whistle that the Mother Puckers had shaken off the morning cobwebs faster than their counterparts. Haley Griffin set the tone early, potting her first goal of the day just 2:08 into the contest. It was a gritty, unassisted effort that signaled to the Chix with Stix that it was going to be a long morning. Griffin wasn't done there; she doubled the lead at the 8:41 mark, burying a nice feed from Emily LaRance to send the Mother Puckers into the first intermission with a comfortable two-goal cushion.

While the Chix with Stix tried to find their legs in the middle frame, the Mother Puckers turned the ice into their personal practice facility. Kaley Pate struck at 2:39, converting a crisp play from Melissa McLauchlin and Taylor Soucy. Moments later, McLauchlin got in on the scoring herself, tapping one home off a setup from Kalea Maxwell. By the time the buzzer sounded for the second break, the game had effectively slipped out of reach, leaving the Chix with Stix bench looking like they’d just been through a three-hour power skating camp.

The third period settled into a bit of a defensive slog, which is usually where the "veteran" crowd starts to appreciate the finer points of the game—or at least starts thinking about where to get a decent cup of coffee. McLauchlin added the final nail in the coffin at 11:33, once again benefiting from a helper by Soucy to round out her multi-point performance. 

For the Chix with Stix, the morning was a forgettable affair. Entering the game on a rocky 1-4 slide, they never managed to generate the sustained pressure needed to crack the Mother Puckers’ shell. They’ll need to head back to the drawing board to snap this three-game losing streak.

On the other side of the red line, the Mother Puckers move to 3-5-1 on the season. It wasn’t a night for highlight-reel acrobatics, but it was a solid, professional effort—the kind of game that reminds you why we keep showing up at 10:15 a.m. on a weekend. They played the body, moved the puck, and didn't leave much for the opposition to complain about. It’s a step forward in a crowded mid-table pack, and for a team trying to find its identity, that’s exactly the kind of win they needed to build on.
