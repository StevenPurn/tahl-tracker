# Mighty Drunks Cement Perfection with Championship Shutout of Rohan Riders

The logic of the spreadsheet suggested this was a mismatch, and for the better part of two periods, the Rohan Riders did their level best to prove that hockey—much to the chagrin of data analysts everywhere—is played on ice, not on paper. However, in the championship tilt this past Sunday, the undefeated juggernaut known as the Mighty Drunks ultimately asserted their dominance, securing a 3-0 victory to put an emphatic exclamation point on a near-perfect season.

Entering the contest, the Riders (4-6-1) were hoping to derail the Drunks’ (11-0-1) historic run, but they were met with a defensive wall that proved impenetrable. Through the first frame and the majority of the second, the game was a claustrophobic affair defined by tactical stagnation and a high volume of extracurricular physicality. By the 10:37 mark of the second period, the referees were forced to intervene, offsetting matching slashing penalties to Aivar Kant and Jared Hallahan. When James Blythe III headed to the box for interference later in the period, the Riders had their windows of opportunity, but the Mighty Drunks’ penalty kill remained as stingy as a miserly accountant.

The dam finally broke in the third period. Tyler Waller, riding a wave of momentum that has seen him find the back of the net in five consecutive games, provided the spark. At the 11:52 mark, Waller unassisted broke the deadlock, effectively ending the stalemate that had hovered over the rink like a dense fog. 

"We knew they were going to sit back and clog the lanes," said Waller after the celebration. "My goal was just to keep the feet moving. If you wait for the numbers to favor you, you’ll be waiting until the ice melts."

With the pressure of the scoreless tie lifted, the Drunks found their rhythm. Jared Hallahan redeemed his earlier trip to the penalty box by burying a pass from Matthew Holland at 14:36 to push the lead to 2-0. Finally, Tyler Waller put the game—and the season—firmly in the rearview mirror at the 7:04 mark, notching his second of the night off a crisp feed from James Blythe III.

The shutout victory is a fitting capstone for the Drunks, who entered the night having rattled off five straight wins. For the Riders, who battled valiantly to reach this stage, the result is a tough pill to swallow, but they can take solace in the fact that they remained the only team to hold the champions to a draw during the regular season.

While the Riders will look to reshape their roster and improve upon their sixth-place standing for the upcoming campaign, the Mighty Drunks leave the ice as undisputed kings of the league. If there is a statistical lesson to be taken from this championship run, it’s that a winning streak isn't just a pattern—it’s a habit. And for the Mighty Drunks, that habit seems impossible to break.
