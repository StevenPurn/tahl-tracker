# Greasers Grease the Wheels in Wild 8-6 Comeback Over The Mafia

If you left the arena at the end of the second period to beat the traffic, you missed an absolute barn-burner. In a Tuesday night tilt that saw more lead changes than a revolving door, the Tulsa Greasers climbed out of a deep hole to stun The Mafia 8-6, snapping their losing skid in spectacular fashion.

The first period was a frantic, high-octane affair that felt like a pinball machine set to "maximum intensity." Christopher Levy opened the scoring for the Greasers, but The Mafia responded with a flurry of four unanswered goals—including tallies from Joshua Hendrickson, Brett Lamb, Jacob Meyer, and Brian Maher—to take a commanding 4-1 lead into the intermission. 

"We were playing like we were wearing skates made of concrete in that first frame," Greasers coach "Big Sal" Scaletta joked after the buzzer. "I told the boys in the room, 'If you don't start moving your feet, I’m going to make you skate laps until the Zamboni runs out of ice.'"

The Greasers clearly got the message. The middle period was all about the comeback effort. Dylan Walker ignited the comeback with a gritty unassisted goal, followed quickly by a laser from Robert Millea. Despite a late second-period strike from The Mafia’s Scott Hellard to keep the lead at 5-3, the momentum had shifted firmly toward the Greasers’ bench.

The third period, however, was where the "Tulsa Turnaround" truly took flight. After Brian Maher extended The Mafia’s lead early in the final frame, the Greasers responded with a four-goal barrage that sent the crowd into a frenzy. Mitchell Buckmaster played the role of hero, lighting the lamp twice in the final period, while Austin Cruz and Robert McAnally also found the back of the net in quick succession. By the time McAnally buried the insurance marker with just under a minute left on the clock, the Greasers had successfully erased the deficit and completed the 8-6 miracle.

"It was pure chaos out there, the good kind," said an exhausted McAnally, who ended the night with two goals. "Once we got that second one to drop, you could feel the ice tilting. We just kept our foot on the gas."

The win is a massive morale booster for the Greasers, who move to 2-5-2 on the season and prove they have the offensive firepower to hang with anyone in the league. For The Mafia, who were looking to climb higher in the standings, it’s a tough pill to swallow after leading for the majority of the night. 

Brian Maher extended his impressive goal streak in the loss, proving he’s still the man to watch in this league, but it wasn't enough to stop the surging Greasers. Both teams now head back to the drawing board, but for the folks in Tulsa, tonight was a reminder that in hockey, the scoreboard doesn't stop ticking until the final whistle blows.
