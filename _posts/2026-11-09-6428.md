# Puckheads Shred the Wolfpack in 7-1 Rout

If you believe the advanced metrics, the One Man Wolfpack entered Friday night’s contest looking to build on their spotty form, but someone forgot to tell the Puckheads that this was supposed to be a competitive hockey game. In a display of offensive efficiency that bordered on clinical, the Puckheads systematically dismantled the Wolfpack 7-1, effectively moving to .500 on the season and leaving their opponents searching for answers in the basement of the standings.

The tone for the evening was set early in the first period. Gregory Douglas, who would eventually emerge as the game’s primary offensive catalyst, opened the scoring at the 10:43 mark. It was an unassisted effort that seemed to catch the Wolfpack flat-footed, a recurring theme for a team that struggled to find its defensive structure all night. By the time the horn sounded on the first period, the Puckheads had already established a dominant 3-0 lead thanks to additional tallies from Andrew Retkowski—assisted by William Hofmeister—and a late unassisted strike from Kellen Nitz.

For the statisticians in the stands, the transition game was the story of the second period. Douglas struck again at 2:22 to extend the lead to 4-0, putting the Wolfpack in a hole so deep that not even a Herculean effort could bail them out. While Kyle Okurowski managed to break the shutout bid for the Wolfpack at 5:33, the goal served as little more than a statistical footnote. 

"We were watching the puck more than we were chasing it," one unnamed Wolfpack supporter remarked while adjusting their scarf. "At this point, we’re just playing for the shift-by-shift moral victories."

The third period turned into a full-scale clinical demonstration by the Puckheads. Any lingering hope for a Wolfpack comeback was extinguished in a 41-second span midway through the frame. Jake Sartin found the back of the net at 6:10, followed immediately by Cameron Sartin at 6:46. Richard Retkowski added the finishing touch at 7:51, cementing the 7-1 final score.

While the Puckheads’ defensive core largely stifled the Wolfpack's transition game, it was the offensive depth that truly defined this win. With contributions scattered across the roster—including a multi-point night for Gregory Douglas and effective playmaking from the Sartin duo—the Puckheads looked like a team finally hitting its stride. 

For the Puckheads, the win improves their record to 4-3-1, inching them closer to the top tier of the standings. For the Wolfpack, the struggle continues. With a 1-5-2 record and a penchant for conceding in bunches, they head into their next matchup with plenty of film to review and a significant gap to bridge if they hope to climb out of the league’s lower quadrant. As for the Puckheads, they proved that when the puck is moving and the shots are finding their mark, the spreadsheet doesn't just predict the outcome—it dictates it.
