# The Bad Bees Sting the Riveters in an Absolute Barn-Burner

If you were looking for a quiet, sleepy Saturday morning at the rink, you were definitely at the wrong place. The Bad Bees, sitting near the bottom of the heap, decided they’d had enough of the underdog narrative and pulled off a 6-5 upset for the ages against the high-flying Tulsa Riveters. It was the kind of game that reminds you why we drink our coffee cold and keep our eyes glued to the glass—pure, unadulterated chaos on ice.

The Riveters rolled into this one looking like a well-oiled machine, their 7-0-2 record serving as a reminder that they don’t lose often. But the Bees, currently in a fight to keep their heads above water, played with the desperation of a team that had a point to prove. 

The first period was a frantic back-and-forth affair. An "Unknown" Bees player struck first to wake the crowd up, but Theresa Rose Rabara answered back for the Riveters just nineteen seconds later. Michelle Karjadi put the Bees back on top before Gabrielle Gibson reminded everyone why she’s on a scoring tear, burying one to knot it up heading into the second.

That’s when the Riveters looked like they were going to pull away. The second period turned into a Tulsa clinic, with Rabara and Ivy Emerson finding the back of the net, followed by a second goal from Gibson. At 5-2, the Riveters’ bench was looking pretty relaxed—perhaps a little too relaxed. 

"We got caught admiring our own reflection in the plexiglass," one Riveters fan muttered while clutching their lukewarm latte. 

The Bees, however, refused to pack up their gear. They clawed back with a second unassisted goal before the second frame ended, and then turned the pressure up to eleven in the third. Two more unassisted strikes from the Bees’ mystery scorers sent the home crowd into a frenzy, leveling the score at 5-5 and dragging a shell-shocked Riveters squad into the dreaded shootout.

In the skills competition, it was Liliena Storer and Lauren A. Mincey who delivered the heroics for the Bees. While the unstoppable Gabrielle Gibson did her part for the Riveters, the Bees’ goaltending stood tall when it mattered most, locking down the win to give the Bees their second victory of the season.

For the Riveters, it’s a sting that’s going to smart, especially with their undefeated streak coming to a sudden, screeching halt. But for the Bad Bees? This is the kind of momentum that turns a season around. If they can keep this level of grit in their toolkit, the rest of the league had better start watching their backs—or at least, watching their goalie's five-hole.
