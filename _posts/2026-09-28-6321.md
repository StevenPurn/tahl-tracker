# Hitmen Silver Overwhelm Blizzard in Second-Half Surge

If you showed up to the rink on Friday night expecting a defensive stalemate between the Hitmen Silver and the Blizzard, you clearly haven't been paying attention to the Hitmen’s brand of hockey. In a 7-2 victory that saw the league leaders flex their collective muscles, the Hitmen proved once again that they don’t just play the game—they eventually exhaust the life out of it.

For the first thirty minutes, however, it looked like the Blizzard might actually pull off an upset. Gary Hennenfent was the primary thorn in the Hitmen's side, tallying a pair of goals—one early in the first and another shortly after the second-period whistle—to put the Blizzard up 2-0. It was the kind of efficient, gritty hockey that usually keeps an underdog in the conversation. Robert Eustice deserves a nod for his vision, picking up primary assists on both of Hennenfent’s strikes.

Then, the script flipped. With only 15.8 seconds left in the middle frame, Calvin Baker finally cracked the seal on the Blizzard’s net off a crisp feed from Christopher Gardner. It was a classic "backbreaker" goal, the kind that coaches lose sleep over. 

"We were feeling pretty good about our lunchboxes for a period and a half," remarked one Blizzard defender, who requested anonymity to avoid being laughed at by his own goalie. "Then the Hitmen remembered they were the Hitmen."

Whatever was said in the Hitmen locker room between the first and second period surely wasn't repeated in polite company, but it clearly worked. Shortly after Baker’s momentum-shifter, Nicklas Clark found the back of the net off a Joshua Moyer setup, knotting the score at 2-2. From that point on, it was all Hitmen.

The dam broke completely in the third period. Bobby Pickett and Joshua Moyer scored twenty-six seconds apart just past the midway mark of the final frame, effectively ending any hopes of a Blizzard comeback. Keegan Guthrie, Nicklas Clark—who notched his second of the night—and Christopher Gardner joined the scoring parade, piling on four more goals to turn a competitive contest into a lopsided affair. 

The Blizzard’s night effectively ended on a sour note when Zachary Stidham was tagged for tripping with just over three minutes remaining, neutralizing any late-game desperation push. 

For the Hitmen Silver, this result solidifies their standing at the top of the table. They’ve now navigated their first four games without a loss, displaying a terrifying ability to turn a deficit into a blowout. The Blizzard, meanwhile, will want to put this one in the rearview mirror and forget the final twelve minutes of the third period ever happened. 

In this league, you’re only as good as your next shift. For the Hitmen, those shifts are currently turning into a statement that they’re the team to beat. For the Blizzard, it’s back to the drawing board to figure out how to stop a freight train once it gains momentum.
