# Wolfpack and Goats Trade Punches in 4-4 Stalemate

If you’re looking for a definitive answer on who holds the bragging rights between the One Man Wolfpack and the Killer Goats, you’re going to have to wait for the next dance. The two teams battled to a 4-4 draw on Monday night at the rink, a contest that featured more lead changes than a frantic game of musical chairs.

Both sides entered the night hungry for their first win of the season, and it showed early on. The Killer Goats struck first at the 11:19 mark of the opening period when Christopher Steuck found the back of the net unassisted. It was the start of a wild back-and-forth affair that saw the teams trade goals like a bad hand of poker. 

Kyle Foster pulled the Wolfpack even with less than two minutes remaining in the first, only for Aaron Quinton to capitalize on a setup from Steuck and Michael Doyle with just under thirty seconds on the clock. But the Wolfpack weren't about to head to the locker room trailing; Zack Wade, fed by the streaking John Digges, knotted it up again at 3:06, followed shortly by a solo effort from Jared Zellers that gave the Wolfpack a brief 3-2 lead. 

"It was one of those games where if you blinked, you missed a scoring chance," remarked one spectator from the bleachers, clutching a lukewarm coffee. "At this point, I’m just happy they’re keeping the lights on."

The second period tightened up, mostly because both squads seemed to realize that playing defense is actually a component of hockey. Joshua Gonya leveled the score for the Goats at 5:54, and the physicality picked up as the period wore on, resulting in matching minors to Zellers and Quinton. It was a rare moment of structure in a game that otherwise felt like a pond hockey clinic.

The deciding frame saw the tension dial up. Nelson Pina put the Goats ahead 4-3 midway through the third, capitalizing on an assist from Brian Smith and Travis Miller. Just as it looked like the Goats might trot away with the two points, Brandt Mueller hammered home the equalizer for the Wolfpack with less than six minutes remaining. 

For the Wolfpack, the draw does little to pull them out of the lower half of the standings, but it does extend their unbeaten-in-regulation streak to two. The Killer Goats, meanwhile, remain winless on the season, though they’ve yet to drop a decision in regulation. 

Both teams walk away with a single point, which, in a league this tightly bunched, is better than a poke in the eye with a sharp stick. They’ll head home tonight knowing that while the scoreboard says even, the real winners were the fans—if only because they got their money's worth of goal-scoring.
