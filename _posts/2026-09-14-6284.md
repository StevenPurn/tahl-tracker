# Bison Stampede: Tulsa Puts Nine Past Reapers in Lopsided Affair

If you were looking for a defensive masterclass on Friday night at the rink, you were in the wrong building. The Tulsa Bison turned the ice into their personal practice facility, dismantling the Reapers by a final score of 9-1. It was the kind of game that makes you wonder if the Zamboni driver was the only one on the Reapers' side putting in a full shift.

The writing was on the wall early. After Kelly Rundle headed to the box for interference just over eleven minutes into the first period, the Reapers never really found their footing. Brett Mecham broke the ice shortly after that penalty expired, and from there, the Bison offense moved the puck with the kind of clinical efficiency that usually requires a whiteboard and a lot of shouting from the bench. By the time the first period buzzer sounded, Lucas Jameson had already notched two goals, effectively putting the game out of reach before most of the fans had finished their first coffee.

"We played like we were skating through wet cement," said one anonymous Reapers bench-warmer, conveniently hiding behind his water bottle as he trudged off the ice. 

The second period was a carbon copy of the first, just louder. Justin Phillips, James DePriest, and Brent Brown all found the back of the net, turning the scoreboard into a mathematical tragedy for the Reapers. Thomas Aurilio proved to be the puppet master for the Bison, quietly racking up three assists on the night with the kind of calm playmaking that makes the game look far easier than it actually is.

To their credit, the Reapers didn't just fold the tent entirely. They finally broke the shutout bid with just over five minutes remaining in the third period when Kelly Rundle converted a feed from Edgar Solorio. It was a nice goal, sure, but at 8-1, it was the hockey equivalent of putting a band-aid on a broken leg.

The Bison added two more for good measure before the final horn, including Jameson’s hat-trick clincher at 6:20 of the third, leaving the Reapers to ponder what went wrong while the Bison celebrated their first win of the season. 

It was a rough night for the Reapers, who now find themselves staring at the bottom of the standings with an 0-1-1 record. For the Tulsa Bison, the victory serves as a serious statement of intent. They didn't just beat the Reapers; they dismantled them. If they can keep that level of puck movement going, the rest of the league might want to start paying closer attention to the Bison’s next shift on the ice.
