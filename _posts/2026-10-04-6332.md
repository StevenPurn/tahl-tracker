# Bad Bees Sting the Puckers in Sunday Morning Shutout

If you were looking for a high-octane goal fest this Sunday morning, you probably came to the wrong rink. But if you appreciate a blue-collar, defensive chess match played out before most folks have even finished their first cup of coffee, the Bad Bees’ 2-0 victory over the Mother Puckers was a masterclass in patience.

The 10:15 a.m. puck drop felt like a sleepy affair at the start, with both squads trading neutral zone clutters like they were passing a collection plate. The Mother Puckers found themselves in a bit of a scramble early on when Abigail Nisbet headed to the box for a roughing minor at the 7:30 mark of the first period. To the Puckers' credit, their penalty kill unit looked like they’d been drinking espresso by the gallon, turning away every Bees attempt and keeping the scoreboard as clean as a fresh sheet of ice heading into the first intermission.

The game stayed locked in a scoreless stalemate, turning into the kind of tension-filled grind that makes your knuckles white on the glass. Just when it looked like we were heading into the second break deadlocked at goose eggs, Grace Wolff decided she’d had enough of the foreplay. With less than a second left on the clock in the second period—literally, at the 00.9 mark—Wolff slipped one past the keeper. It was the kind of buzzer-beater that drains the air right out of the opposing bench, sending the Bees into the dressing room with a one-goal lead they definitely didn't steal.

If the first goal was a gut punch, the second was a calculated strike. Moments later at 35.9, the Bees struck again, with an uncredited sniper burying a feed from Casey Cochrane to push the lead to 2-0. 

The Puckers fought valiantly, but the Bees’ defense played with the stubbornness of a mule in a mud pit, shutting down any hopes of a comeback. While the Mother Puckers remain winless, struggling to find their footing in the standings at 0-3-1, the Bad Bees are proving to be the real deal. With this win, the Bees improve to 3-0-1, keeping them firmly in the hunt near the top of the leaderboard.

As the ice surface was cleared and the players headed for the parking lot, the Bees looked like a team that knows exactly who they are: efficient, stingy, and ready to make a serious run this season. The Puckers, meanwhile, will have to head back to the drawing board to figure out how to wake up their offense before their next tilt.
