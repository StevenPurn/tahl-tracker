# The Lodge Wakes Up with an 8-3 Shellacking of Mojo

If you caught the 9:30 PM tilt between the Lodge and Mojo on Tuesday, you might want to check the arena’s foundation—because the Lodge didn’t just win, they brought the house down. In a regular-season clash that started as a skirmish and ended as a statement, the Lodge dismantled Mojo 8-3, finally finding their rhythm and climbing out of the basement of the standings in high-octane fashion.

The Lodge arrived at the rink hungry, having stumbled through their first two games without a win. They set the tone early in the first period when Braden Trinka found the twine at the 13:49 mark, courtesy of a slick setup from Michael Randall. Michael Galle followed that up shortly after, burying a helper from Trinka and Seth Miller to put Mojo on their heels. While Bradley Fidler managed to claw one back for Mojo before the buzzer, the momentum was clearly residing in the visitors' locker room.

The second period was a chaotic, back-and-forth affair that would have made a cardiac specialist nervous. The Lodge extended their lead through Mark Stacy and a second goal from Trinka, but Mojo showed some grit. Andrew Armendariz and Matthew Allington traded shots in quick succession to bring the score to a precarious 4-3, making it look like we were in for a nail-biter. 

"I was halfway through my Gatorade when they started mounting that comeback," joked one Lodge bench staffer after the game. "I told the boys, 'Look, let's stop playing ping-pong and start playing hockey.'"

Whether it was a stern lecture or just a shift in the wind, the Lodge responded by slamming the door shut. Trinka completed his heavy lifting on the night by burying his third goal at the 7:08 mark, effectively taking the wind out of Mojo’s sails. From there, the dam broke. The third period became a showcase for the Lodge, with Michael Galle, Michael Randall, and Gabi Gibson all finding the back of the net to stretch the lead into an emphatic 8-3 finish.

The defensive effort in the final frame was nothing short of a brick wall, even with a late-period hiccup that saw Christopher Fleming cooling his heels in the box for a tripping minor. By the time the final buzzer rang, the Lodge had turned an 0-1-1 start into a reason for optimism.

It was a tough night for Mojo, who couldn't find an answer for the Lodge’s balanced scoring attack after their brief second-period rally. For the Lodge, this is exactly the kind of offensive outburst that can ignite a season. If they keep moving the puck like they did tonight, the rest of the league might want to keep a close watch on the standings—because the Lodge has officially checked in.
