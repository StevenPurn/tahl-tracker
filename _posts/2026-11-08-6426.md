# Renegades Put on a Scoring Clinic in 7-1 Rout of Oklahoma Precast

If you wandered into the rink expecting a tight, defensive battle between the league’s top two squads, you were likely looking for a different building. In a game that saw the Renegades cement their spot atop the standings, the league-leading Renegades dismantled Oklahoma Precast 7-1 on Saturday evening, turning what was supposed to be a measuring-stick game into a total runaway.

The Renegades didn’t just win; they dictated the pace from the drop of the puck. Eli Augustine proved to be the early thorn in Oklahoma’s side, striking twice in the first period. His opening tally at the 10:28 mark—set up by Elliott Langford and Joseph Pachuta—seemed to rattle the Precast defense, and Augustine followed it up with an unassisted solo effort with 4:18 left in the frame. By the time the buzzer sounded for the first intermission, the Renegades had already built a lead that looked more like a mountain than a cushion.

Oklahoma Precast tried to find their legs in the second, but Caeleb Burch effectively extinguished any hopes of a comeback. Burch notched a pair of unassisted goals, one at 14:05 and another at 6:37, showcasing the kind of opportunistic play that has the Renegades sitting pretty at 5-1-1. When you’re scoring back-to-back unassisted goals, it usually means your opponent is struggling to exit their own zone, and tonight, Oklahoma Precast spent most of their evening chasing ghosts in their own end.

To their credit, Oklahoma Precast finally broke the goose egg in the third period when Bazil Cardamone found the back of the net at 10:03. For about two minutes, it looked like a potential spark for a late surge. Instead, the Renegades responded with a three-goal barrage that turned the score from a respectable defeat into a rout.

Michael Pasque, who had spent some time in the penalty box earlier in the game for tripping, redeemed himself with a goal at 2:12, assisted by Augustine and Curtis Tucker. The floodgates swung wide open after that. Just 27 seconds later, Ian Schultz capitalized on a feed from Langford and Augustine, and Elliott Langford capped the madness at the 3:25 mark with his own goal.

"We just let them play catch the whole time," one anonymous league observer noted while nursing a lukewarm coffee. "It’s hard to win when the other team is basically running a track meet."

For the Renegades, the victory is a loud statement to the rest of the league, keeping their momentum white-hot as they extend their lead in the standings. Oklahoma Precast, meanwhile, will likely want to wipe the tape of this one clean and look toward next week. They’ve got the pieces to be competitive, but Saturday night in the rink was a stark reminder that in this league, if you don't show up with your skates sharp and your focus sharper, the Renegades are more than happy to show you the door.
