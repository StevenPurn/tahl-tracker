# Dirty Birds Ground the Blizzard in 13-3 Goalfest

If you were looking for a quiet evening of defensive hockey at the rink this Friday, you definitely walked into the wrong barn. The Dirty Birds put on an absolute offensive clinic against the Blizzard, turning what looked like a competitive matchup on paper into a 13-3 rout that had the scoreboard operator double-checking their equipment by the end of the night.

The Blizzard actually drew first blood, with Joshua Moyer finding the back of the net at the 11:01 mark of the first period. For a fleeting moment, the visiting bench looked energized, perhaps sensing a chance to jump ahead in the standings. That hope, however, lasted exactly forty-four seconds. James Smith ignited the Dirty Birds' engine with an equalizer, and from that point on, it was all traffic one way.

By the time the first intermission rolled around, the Birds had rattled off four unanswered goals. Nicklas Clark, Sean Donnelly, and Triston Wilson all joined the party, leaving the Blizzard defenders looking like they were trying to catch smoke with their bare hands.

If the first period was a light shower, the second was a monsoon. The Dirty Birds poured in seven goals in the middle frame. Michael Bergstrom was essentially playing a game of catch with himself, racking up points and setting up teammates with surgical precision. The Blizzard finally found a bit of a spark when Zachary Stidham beat the keeper at 14:51, but it was like trying to put out a forest fire with a water pistol. By the time the buzzer sounded to end the second, the score was a staggering 11-2.

The third period settled into a bit of a slog as both teams started looking toward the parking lot, though there were still a few highlights to be had. Timothy N See spent two minutes in the box for high-sticking, and the Blizzard managed to scratch one more goal across via Robert Eustice. But for every jab the Blizzard landed, the Birds countered with a knockout blow, with Gabriel Kierath and Triston Wilson adding final tallies to bring the count to an unlucky 13 for the visitors.

Triston Wilson kept his point-scoring streak alive and kicking, looking like a man possessed out there, while Michael Bergstrom’s playmaking ability turned the Blizzard’s zone into his own personal office.

For the Dirty Birds, this win is a massive statement of intent, pushing them firmly up the table and proving that when their offense is clicking, they can outrun anyone in the league. As for the Blizzard, it’s one of those nights you just toss in the trash, forget about, and hope the bus ride home doesn't feel quite as long as the second period did. Both teams are back at it soon, but the Birds are definitely the ones currently chirping the loudest.
