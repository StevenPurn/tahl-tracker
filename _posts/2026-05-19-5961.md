# A Scoring Storm: Tornadoes Flatten Renegades in 12-5 Goal-Fest

**By: "Rinkside" Rick Henderson**
*May 18, 2026*

If you were looking for a defensive masterpiece tonight, you were definitely at the wrong arena. But if you wanted to see the twine ripple like a curtain in a hurricane, boy, did we have a show for you. Under the lights of the arena on this crisp May evening, the Tornadoes unleashed a full-blown meteorological event, dismantling the Renegades in a 12-5 victory that felt more like a video game score than a professional hockey match.

Entering the night, both squads sat deadlocked at 2 points in the standings. By the final horn, the Tornadoes had clearly decided they were tired of sharing.

### Period 1: Cieslak’s Opening Act
The Renegades barely had time to lace up their skates before Christopher Cieslak decided he was going to own the night. The guy was an absolute buzzsaw, opening the scoring at the 10:06 mark and never looking back. By the time the buzzer sounded on a wild first frame, Cieslak had completed a natural hat trick—his third coming with just 3:28 left on the clock.

While the Renegades tried to hold the umbrella against the deluge, managing to respond with strikes from Justin Gibson and Alex Bateman, the Tornadoes simply had too much firepower. Michael Alfaro and Christopher Gardner tacked on goals of their own, sending the Tornadoes into the intermission with a commanding lead that left the Renegades looking like they’d been caught in a gale-force wind.

### Period 2: The Onslaught Continues
If the Renegades hoped the break would cool the Tornadoes' jets, they were sorely mistaken. The second period was a clinic in puck possession. Wolfgang Baran and Brandt Mueller ignited the lamp in quick succession, and before the Renegades could blink, Christian Alvegran added a shorthanded beauty—unassisted and purely opportunistic.

Then, Cieslak decided he wasn't finished. He netted his fourth and fifth goals of the night in a span of just 23 seconds. It was top-shelf, middle-shelf, and bottom-shelf artistry. Austin Tyler did his best to keep the Renegades afloat with a pair of goals, but by the time the ice was resurfaced, the Tornadoes held a lead so large it could be seen from space. Logan Singleton’s high-sticking penalty only added to the Renegades' headache, as the Tornadoes spent the latter half of the frame effectively playing keep-away.

### Period 3: Closing the Curtain
The final frame saw the intensity taper off, but Cieslak wasn't done padding his stat sheet, notching his *sixth* goal of the game at 12:14. Tobias Alvegran added the final nail in the coffin midway through the period. 

The Renegades’ Logan Singleton managed to find the back of the net to make it 12-5, but at that point, the outcome was written in ink. The final minutes were marred by some physical play, including a pair of trips to the sin bin for Christopher Gardner, but the Renegades couldn't muster a late-game rally to put a dent in that mountain of a deficit.

### The Aftermath
When the dust settled, the Tornadoes had effectively blown the Renegades off the map. Christopher Cieslak’s six-goal performance will be talked about at the water cooler for weeks—it was arguably the most dominant offensive display I’ve seen in years. 

For the Tornadoes (4 pts), this blowout win is a massive statement of intent, vaulting them ahead of the pack. For the Renegades (2 pts), it’s back to the drawing board. They’ll need to tighten up the traffic in front of their own net if they hope to stop the bleeding before their next outing. 

The wind is blowing in favor of the Tornadoes tonight—and for the Renegades, it’s a storm they’ll want to forget as quickly as possible.
