# Danglers Explode for Double Digits in 10-5 Rout of Cold Ones

If you were looking for a defensive masterclass this Sunday morning, you were undeniably in the wrong building. The Dirty Danglers and the Kraken Cold Ones combined for a staggering 15 goals in a contest that turned the scoreboard into a flickering neon advertisement for offensive chaos. When the dust finally settled on the 10-5 victory for the Dirty Danglers, the only thing more taxed than the scoreboard operator was the collective sanity of the two goaltenders.

The Danglers, sitting at 1-3-1 entering the match, clearly decided that the best way to climb out of the basement was to turn the offensive spigot wide open. The catalyst for this surge was undeniably Dylan Calhoun, who continued his torrid pace by tallying a four-goal masterpiece. Calhoun, now riding a five-game point streak, looked like he was playing a different sport than the rest of the skaters on the ice. He opened the scoring at the 10:19 mark of the first period and refused to let his foot off the gas, essentially acting as the gravitational pull around which the entire Danglers offense orbited.

The game began with a flurry of activity that left the Kraken Cold Ones reeling. The Danglers’ momentum was punctuated by a ruthless efficiency on the power play; specifically, after a roughing penalty sent Paul Davis to the box, Aden Ingram needed only 11 seconds to capitalize on a feed from Brett Mooney. By the time the buzzer sounded on the first period, the Danglers had already established a dominant rhythm, thanks in no small part to Ethan Hilton, who secured a brace of goals before the first intermission.

The Cold Ones did manage to show signs of life, fueled largely by the relentless ROBERT TYREE. Tyree, who entered the contest on a two-game point streak, proved he was the only player in a Kraken sweater capable of manufacturing consistent offense, recording two goals and two assists on the afternoon. Despite his efforts, the Kraken’s defensive structure resembled a screen door in a hurricane, consistently leaving their netminder vulnerable to the Danglers' high-octane transition game.

"We essentially decided that back-checking was a suggestion rather than a requirement," joked one frustrated Kraken bench staffer during the second period, as the Danglers continued to exploit gaps in the neutral zone. 

The second period saw the Danglers extend their lead, with Kalea Maxwell finding the back of the net, followed closely by Calhoun’s second of the night. Even as the Kraken attempted a rally through Aaron Crum and a late third-period goal from Tyree, they simply couldn't trade punch-for-punch with a Danglers squad that seemed possessed. By the time Ethan Hilton notched his second goal of the game and Aden Ingram added his own late in the third, the result was a mathematical certainty.

For the Dirty Danglers, this 10-5 shellacking serves as a massive confidence booster and a total recalibration of their goal differential. They head into the next week of the season looking like a team that has finally found the rhythm that eluded them in their opening month. As for the Kraken Cold Ones, the spreadsheet suggests they have some serious work to do in their own zone if they hope to reverse their recent slide. On a morning where the puck seemed to have a magnetic attraction to the back of the net, the Danglers were simply the ones holding the magnet.
