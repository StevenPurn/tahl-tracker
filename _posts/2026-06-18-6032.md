# Mojo Rising: A Seven-Goal Offensive Clinic Drowns the Laser

**By: The Rinkside Reporter**  
*June 17, 2026*

If you like your hockey served with a side of high-octane chaos and a dash of late-game heroics, last night’s tilt at the rink was the place to be. Entering the night deadlocked in the standings with 2 points apiece, Mojo and Laser looked like two teams desperate to climb out of the middle-of-the-pack muck. By the time the final buzzer echoed through the rafters, Mojo had sent a clear message to the rest of the league: they’ve got the firepower to turn the lights out on anyone.

### First Period: Laser Strikes First
The game opened with the intensity of a double-espresso shot. Laser drew first blood at the 12:50 mark when **Jeremy Kuzmarov** capitalized on a defensive lapse, firing one home to give Laser the early edge. Despite a hooking penalty sent Ben Castagna to the sin bin later in the frame, Mojo couldn’t convert on the man advantage, and we headed to the intermission with Laser nursing a slim 1-0 lead.

### Second Period: The Seesaw Struggle
The middle frame was a flat-out barn burner. **Matthew Allington** knotted things up for Mojo at 3:32, but Laser’s **Colton Taylor** decided he wasn’t going to let that slide. Taylor went on a heater, potting back-to-back goals in a frantic 18-second span to push Laser to a 3-1 advantage. 

But if you’ve watched this Mojo squad, you know they’ve got a short memory. **Jarred Butler** answered back at 6:45, assisted by Allington, to stop the bleeding and cut the deficit. The tension was thick enough to skate on, and even with a trip to the box for Laser’s Chad Pancoast, the score remained a tight 3-2 heading into the final frame.

### Third Period: Mojo’s Scoring Monsoon
The third period was absolute theater. **Pete Fleming** brought the Mojo faithful to their feet at 10:06, tying the game 3-3. While Laser’s **Bryce Perkins**—keeping his point streak alive—responded shortly after to reclaim the lead, it was all Mojo from there.

With under a minute left in the period, **Austin Stauffer** found the twine off a feed from Butler to equalize yet again. That goal seemed to pop the balloon for Laser. Mojo poured it on, with Jarred Butler netting his second of the night, followed almost immediately by another from Fleming. The momentum was so heavy it felt like a physical breeze coming off the ice. 

A late-game lapse by Jesse Stockley sent him to the box, but even shorthanded, Mojo looked dangerous. By the time **Jesse Stockley** redeemed himself with a goal of his own off a Kyle White assist, Mojo had turned a nail-biter into a decisive 7-4 rout.

### The Aftermath
For Mojo, this 7-4 victory is the tonic they needed to break their recent skid. Jarred Butler was the unquestioned star of the show, proving that his point streak isn't just about showing up—it’s about taking over. 

**What this means for the standings:**
Mojo vaults forward, leaving their 1-1-0 start in the rearview mirror and firmly establishing themselves as a team that can out-slug the competition. Laser, meanwhile, finds themselves needing to tighten up the defensive zone. With the Tulsa Murder and Mooseknuckles still setting the pace at the top, tonight’s fireworks in the Mojo-Laser match proved that in this league, the gap between the middle and the top can disappear faster than a slapper top-shelf where mom keeps the cookies.

The league is taking notice. If you’re playing against Mojo next week, bring an extra helmet—you’re going to need it.
