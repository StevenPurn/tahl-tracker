# Mighty Drunks Fend Off Late Surge to Clip Ice Breakers 6-5

If you were looking for a masterclass in defensive structure on Tuesday night, you were in the wrong arena. What we witnessed at the rink was a high-octane, chaotic, and statistically fascinating collision between the second-place Ice Breakers and the surging Mighty Drunks. When the dust settled, the Drunks walked away with a 6-5 victory, snapping the Ice Breakers' three-game winning streak and proving that in this league, a comfortable lead is often just a suggestion.

The game began with a statistical anomaly that tilted the ice early. The Drunks came out firing, fueled by Gage Walker’s solo effort at 11:50 of the first, followed shortly by Cian Slattery and Glenn Prouse. By the time the dust settled, the Drunks held a 3-0 cushion before most of the fans had finished their first coffee. However, Justice Dick—clearly feeling his oats—decided he wasn’t interested in a blowout. In a sequence that will surely be studied by efficiency experts, Dick scored two goals in the span of six seconds (9:48 and 9:54). It was, quite frankly, an absolute disaster for the Drunks’ defensive integrity, turning a lopsided affair into a one-goal game in the blink of an eye.

The second period belonged entirely to the Drunks’ offensive persistence. Matthew Holland and a second goal from Gage Walker pushed the lead to 5-2. Watching Walker facilitate and score with such clinical precision, one has to wonder if the Ice Breakers’ scouting department simply forgot he was on the ice. 

The third period brought both heroics and headaches. The Ice Breakers attempted a late-game revival, with Tobias Roeding finding the net off a dish from David Hardy, followed by Justice Dick completing his hat trick at 2:39 to pull within one. But just as the momentum felt like it might swing irrevocably in the Breakers' favor, the wheels metaphorically—and perhaps literally—came off for Chad Olson. A 10-minute unsportsmanlike penalty followed by a 2-minute minor at the 4:53 mark essentially hamstrung the Ice Breakers' comeback bid, as the officials decided to take a very hard line on the evening's emotional temperature.

"When you start handing out twelve minutes in penalties for talking, you’re not managing a game, you’re managing a library," an anonymous Ice Breakers player grumbled in the hallway post-game. 

Glenn Prouse added his second of the night on the ensuing power play, giving the Drunks a 6-4 lead. While Chris Jorgensen extended his personal goal streak to five games with a late tally at 54.7 seconds, it was too little, too late for the Breakers.

For the Mighty Drunks, the win is a crucial validation of their upward trajectory, moving them to 3-1-1 and tightening the gap in a competitive top half of the standings. The Ice Breakers, despite the loss, remain at 8 points, though the league’s spreadsheet now looks significantly more congested. If there’s one lesson to take from this 11-goal thriller, it’s this: in recreational hockey, the only thing more dangerous than a 3-0 lead is a team that refuses to look at the scoreboard.
