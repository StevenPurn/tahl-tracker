# Northup’s Hat Trick Stings Tuskens in Thrilling 3-3 Stalemate

If you were looking for a calm, quiet night at the rink on Saturday, you picked the wrong zip code. The Rohan Riders and the Tulsa Tuskens skated to a 3-3 draw that left the ice feeling like it had been through a woodchipper, and frankly, I don’t think either side is walking away feeling like they left anything in the tank.

The story of the night was undoubtedly Samuel Northup, who played like a man possessed. He notched a hat trick for the Riders, essentially acting as a one-man wrecking crew against the Tuskens' defense. Northup opened the scoring late in the first period, assisted by Brandon Zinsious, just moments after the Riders managed to kill off a shaky tripping penalty from Robert Millea. It was the kind of goal that makes you think, "Okay, here we go," but the Tuskens weren’t about to let Northup have the spotlight all to himself.

The second period turned into an absolute barn-burner. Garrett Caraway, who clearly ate a bowl of pure lightning for breakfast, struck back for the Tuskens early in the frame. Northup answered the bell with his second of the night, set up nicely by Glenn Prouse and Alexander Rowin, but the Tuskens responded with a lethal one-two punch. Caraway buried his second of the night just minutes later, and Dylan Walker followed up by finding the back of the net to give the Tuskens a 3-2 lead heading into the final frame. 

The mood in the building was thick enough to cut with a skate blade. The Tuskens seemed poised to walk away with the points, especially after holding the lead through most of the third period. But, with the clock reading a terrifying 04.1—just a blink of an eye before the buzzer—Northup pulled a rabbit out of his hat. He found the back of the net unassisted, snatching the draw out of the jaws of defeat and sending the Riders’ bench into a frenzy.

"I just saw the gap and prayed to the hockey gods," Northup told me near the locker room, his jersey soaked and his grin wider than a rink-side glass. "Sometimes you just have to throw it at the paint and see what happens."

The draw leaves both teams in a bit of a deadlock in the standings, as both the Riders and the Tuskens move forward with identical records. It’s early in the season, and neither side is separating from the pack just yet, but if this game is any indication of how these two clubs handle the pressure, we’re in for a long, competitive winter. Both squads head home tonight with a point, a few bruises, and a whole lot of questions about how they’re going to tighten things up before the next puck drop.
